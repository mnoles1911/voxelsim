# Rugged log raft

The revised `raft` spec uses 25 mm cubic voxels, a nominal 4 m length and
2 m beam, seven independently sized logs, and two 11 cm crosspieces near the ends.
Logs have alternating taper, bow, oval sections, raised branch collars,
staggered chipped ends, exposed sapwood/heartwood, off-centre end grain,
drying checks and irregular islands of worn or missing bark.
Each log/crosspiece intersection has three rope turns, a tightening knot and
a short tail. The central deck is clear of crosspieces. Slight timber overlap
is a lattice connectivity allowance, not a model of wood compression.

The raft now uses the isometric review camera so its log ends and deck can
be inspected together. Canoe and glider generation is unchanged.

## Generated files

`python tools/raftprobe.py` writes the seed-1 `.vxa`, `.vox`, spec, stats and
review image under `out/artifact/raft-0001*`, plus `raft-validation.json`.
These are review exports; the existing draft curation status remains in place.
The updated spec and generator are available in Forge; use its normal Keep
action to select a variant for the game library.

## Validation

Seeds 1–4 have one face-connected component, no repair bridges, clean pipeline
health, and 100% timber coverage through the central half of the deck. Deck
relief there is 15–17.5 cm. Seed 1 measures 4.175 × 2.10 × 0.525 m including
the uneven ends and lashings, with 152,958 occupied voxels. VXA read-back
preserves every material voxel and the 25 mm pitch. Repeated generation is
deterministic and all four seeds differ. Existing canoe/glider read checks pass.

The boat's current physics body ignores player collision and the asset body
is visual only. Standing/crawling on a moving raft requires a separate engine
movement/support change; geometry validation does not verify that behavior.

## Internet reference pass after owner feedback

Reviewed 2026-09-05. The previous pass looked like standardized cylinders
with painted stripes. The revised surface is tied to identifiable features,
rather than adding more independent colour noise.

- [Packtabla timber photograph](https://packtabla.com/imgs/01_packtabla.jpg):
  directly inspected close-up of partly debarked logs. Dark irregular bark
  islands, pale exposed wood, ragged flaking boundaries and branch scars.
- [Nature Travels raft photograph](https://www.naturetravels.co.uk/images/gallery/vv58rs1/vv58rs1-2.jpg):
  directly inspected real log raft with rope turns, nonuniform timber,
  warm debarked surfaces and darker localized scars.
- [Vildmark i Värmland: raft construction](https://www.vildmark.se/en/experiences/the-art-of-building-a-raft/):
  their full-size rafts use dried spruce in varying dimensions and multiple
  layers. This game's smaller single-layer platform is an artistic adaptation,
  not a structural or buoyancy claim about their design.
- [US Forest Service: Weathering of Wood, R. Sam Williams](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/22122):
  weathering affects the surface and can produce grey colour, raised grain,
  and checking. Here those observations inform exposed grey wood and localized
  end splits; grey patches do not represent hollow rotten interiors.
- [Skogkurs timber defects](https://skogkurs.no/fagartikler/virkesfeil-3/):
  supplementary visual reference for knots and branch remnants on felled logs.

Implementation choices inferred from these references: seven different bark
coverage histories, alternating taper, bounded smooth surface fields at two
scales, branch-collar bulges with dark knot centres, and broad sapwood patches.
These are modeled features in the material voxel grid, not a painted preview.

The upright-tree `bark` palette assigns tan end grain to every top face and
dark bark to side faces, creating repeated false stripes on horizontal logs.
The raft uses the existing muted `skin_brown` colour slot for the bark body,
with `beak_horn` for grey-brown fissures, `plume_buff` for pale sapwood/rope,
and `heartwood`/`deadwood` for warm and weathered timber. Artifact materials
already reuse these appearance slots; no engine palette IDs or global colours
were changed. The hull material menu now admits the existing brown slot.

The generator lives in `forge/raft.py`; `artifact.py` retains the form dispatch.
Only the raft spec's hash baseline should move after this revision.
