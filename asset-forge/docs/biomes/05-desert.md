# Desert — master species list

**Biome id 5** — `vxc::BiomeId::DESERT`, `voxel-core/include/voxelcore/biome.h:29`; mirrored `asset-forge/forge/biomes.py:67`.

| | |
|---|---|
| Climate envelope | precipitation < 400 mm/yr **and** mean annual temperature >= 24 C (`biome.h:231`). Both halves are load-bearing — an arid column that is not hot returns GRASSLAND from the same line, which is why this world had literally zero desert for a long stretch. |
| Surface material | sand (`MAT_SAND`, `biome.h:246`) |
| Share of land (shipped world) | **9.74%** over 289 tiles, against Earth's ~8.6% — the closest match any conditioning channel achieved. Best tile `-3_-11` is 96.6% desert (`docs/measurements/biome-screenshot-targets-2026-08-01.txt`). An earlier 121-tile pass in the same file read 8.85%; **use the 289-tile column**, which that file's own two corrections say supersedes it. |
| Water present | Effectively no. No permanent open water is generated by a hot arid climate. The three exceptions are a through-flowing river fed from outside the desert, a spring or oasis, and an ephemeral pan that holds water for weeks. See the fish table. |
| Asset kinds hosted | tree, bush, rock, grass, reed, flower, fish, cetacean, bird (`biomes.py:67-68`) |

This is the one biome where the ground itself is the strongest visual: a continuous sand surface with almost nothing standing on it, so every asset placed here reads at long range against an empty plane and has to hold up in silhouette alone. That cuts both ways for the build queue — the tree list is genuinely short (nine entries, and two of those are cacti) because a hot arid biome genuinely has few trees, and padding it would make the desert look like a thin forest. What carries the biome instead is rock: thirteen rock specs already ship for it, more than any other kind, and they are the landforms a player actually navigates by.

One note on the hosts column. `biomes.py:67-68` admits `cetacean` here because the hosts tuple is shared across every plantable biome, not because there is anywhere in a desert for a whale to be. There is no cetacean section in this file and there should never be one.

## How to read the tables

**Status** is one of:
* `shipped: <spec-name>` — a spec already exists in `asset-forge/specs/`.
* `queued` — a generator exists for this kind; this is authoring work only.
* `gen: <name>` — needs a generator that does not exist. See the index for the gap list.

**Lattice** (animal tables only) is the voxel size the asset should be authored at, by the house rule in `asset-forge/forge/kinds.py:29-58` and `asset-forge/docs/marine-megafauna-research.md:364-365`: a species is drawn at the COARSEST voxel size at which its smallest identifying feature is still about three voxels across. Trees and rocks are not listed with a lattice because they join the world's terrain grid and are 10 cm and nothing else (`kinds.py:34-42`); ground cover, bushes, flowers and reeds are 5 cm.

**⚠** marks a species whose defining feature is at or below what the lattice will hold, explained in the note under its table.

## Trees

Nine entries, and that is the honest number. A biome under 400 mm/yr supports scattered deep-rooted trees along dry watercourses and at springs, not a canopy, and the list should stay short so that placement density is what makes the desert look empty rather than the artist having to hold back a full palette.

Two of the nine are cacti, deliberately filed here rather than under a succulent gap. Reasoning, because the brief asks for it rather than a reflex: the tree generator's **whorl** growth model puts branches off a single dominant trunk at set heights, which is exactly a saguaro's arm arrangement; what it has to be pushed to do is turn each branch vertical a short way out from the trunk and carry no foliage at all. That is a parameter range question, not a new generator, so saguaro and Joshua tree are `queued`. The ones that genuinely do not fit are in the bush table below.

## Bushes / shrubs

This is where the succulents that the tree generator cannot reach ended up, and where the one real `gen: succulent` case sits.

The prickly pear is the honest gap. Every existing woody generator builds a branch as a **cylinder**; a prickly pear is a chain of flat oval pads, each pad joined edge-on to the last at an angle, and there is no flattened-disc primitive anywhere in `forge/kinds.py:66-135`. The barrel cactus is the opposite call: it is a squat ribbed dome sitting part-buried in gravel, which is exactly what the **rock** generator already does (accreted lumps, eroded, part-buried), and its ribbing is below 5 cm and lost anyway — so it is `queued` against the rock generator with a green material, not a gap.

Turn the dorsal, anal, caudal and pelvic fins off, keep the loft, add a rattle at the tail, and a snake is closer to shipped than any mammal in this table.

⚠ notes for this table:
* **Fennec fox** — the ears are the species. Each ear is a thin flat plate, roughly 10 cm tall and well under 1 cm through, so at the 1 cm lattice it is a one-voxel sheet: it exists but has no thickness to shade. Author the ear two voxels thick and slightly oversized, and say so in the spec notes. Do not drop to a finer lattice for the whole animal — a 0.4 m fox at 5 mm is 80 voxels for a background animal.
* **Caracal** — the black ear tufts are a few millimetres thick. They cannot be represented as hair at any lattice this project will ever use. Author them as solid tapered black spikes two voxels wide and accept that they are a symbol rather than a shape.
* **Addax** — the horn spiral is the identifying mark and the horn is ~4 cm thick at the base, tapering. At 2 cm that is two voxels at the root and one at the tip, and a one-voxel spiral will read as noise. Either thicken the horns above life size or author this species at 1 cm.
* **Cape hare** — same as the fennec: ears carry the identity, ears are flat plates. 1 cm with deliberately thickened ears.
* **Lesser Egyptian jerboa** — at 1 cm the entire body is ten voxels. This animal cannot be authored at life size at any lattice in use. If it ships, it ships at roughly twice life size, which is the fix already used for small perched birds — and it must be written into the spec notes, not left for someone to rediscover.
* **Sidewinder** — the horn scale over the eye is under 1 cm and will not survive. The rattle will, if authored as three or four blunt segments rather than the real tapering stack.
* **Deathstalker scorpion** — the legs and pincer arms are 2-3 mm across. At 1 cm an 8 cm scorpion is eight voxels long with one-voxel legs, which by the rule in `marine-megafauna-research.md:341` reads as a mistake rather than a feature. This one needs authoring at two or three times life size to exist at all. That is a real decision about how the desert reads at ground level, not a technicality — flag it before building.

## Fish

**Four entries, and the honest reason the table is this short: a hot arid biome has essentially no permanent open water.** The desert fish list is not thin because the research is incomplete; it is thin because the habitat barely exists. Every entry below depends on one of exactly three exceptions, and each row says which:

1. **A through-flowing river** fed by rainfall somewhere outside the desert, which crosses the sand without being fed by it. Note that the engine classifies a river by the land around it (`biomes.py:37-42`), so this water is tagged DESERT and the fish in it must be too.
2. **A spring or oasis** — a permanent pool at a water table, often small, warm, and salty.
3. **An ephemeral pan** that fills after rain and dries within weeks. Nothing that needs a year in water can live here.

Placement matters more than the species list. A desert fish spec with a normal `detail.per_100m2` will look absurd, because it will only ever be evaluated where water actually exists, and where it exists it is a rare feature.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Nile tilapia | Deep laterally compressed body, long spiny dorsal running most of the back, vertical dark bars on an olive-silver flank and a barred tail. Through-flowing rivers only. | 0.3 | 1 cm | `queued` |

⚠ **Desert pupfish and mosquitofish** — at 1 cm these are five to seven voxels long, which is below the point where a fish has a shape at all. They must be authored above life size or not at all; the pupfish at roughly 2.5x is about 15 cm and 15 voxels, which is at the bottom edge of what the fish generator is documented to work at (`kinds.py:101-106` describes the kind as "from a 20 cm minnow"). Given how rarely a player will be at a desert spring, the honest recommendation is to ship the pupfish oversized as the single visible spring species and skip the mosquitofish.

One more species worth naming but not tabling: the **Arabian killifish**, a 7 cm blunt-headed silvery-olive fish of salty warm desert springs. It fails the lattice for exactly the same reason as the pupfish and adds nothing the pupfish does not already cover, so it is recorded here rather than queued.

## Build priority

1. **Nothing at all, for one release.** The rocks and both trees already ship, and the desert is the one biome whose ground material carries the scene on its own. Before adding assets, confirm the thirteen shipped rock specs are actually being placed on tile `-3_-11` — a desert that reads empty because placement weights are wrong will not be fixed by authoring more assets.
2. **Date palm and umbrella thorn acacia.** Two `queued` tree specs using existing growth models (frond and colonize). They are the two silhouettes that say "desert" from a kilometre away, and between them they cover both water-adjacent and open-plain placement. Cheapest possible visible change.
3. **Creosote bush, four-wing saltbush, brittlebush.** Three bush specs off the shipped bush machinery. Scattered low grey mounds at wide spacing are what an arid plain actually looks like, and they fill the middle distance that trees cannot.
4. **Saguaro and ocotillo.** Both are `queued` against existing generators (whorl with foliage off; a splayed strand fan). Both are strong, unusual silhouettes that no other biome will reuse, so they are the highest identity-per-spec on this page.
5. **The quadruped generator.** Sixteen of twenty land animals wait on it, and so does most of every other biome file. It is the largest single unlock in the whole library, and nothing in the desert-specific list should be allowed to jump ahead of it on the argument that a specific animal is wanted — the generator is the work.
6. **`gen: serpentine`, immediately after — or before — the quadruped.** Per the note under the land-animal table, this is very likely the fish generator with fins disabled rather than a new generator. If that estimate holds it is a day's work for a whole clade, and it should be tested before the quadruped is scoped, because a cheap win that turns out to be cheap changes the order.
7. **Everything else.** Flowers, ground cover and the fish are low-yield here: the flowers only appear after rain, the grasses are sparse by definition, and the fish need water that mostly does not exist.

## Where the numbers come from

**Every species size on this page is an approximation from general knowledge. Nothing here is measured, and nothing here is sourced.** They are typical adult figures, rounded to the precision I actually believe — which is usually one significant figure past the metre — and they exist for exactly one purpose: to choose a lattice. They are good enough for that and they are not good enough to quote.

Concretely: **before any figure on this page is written into a spec's `notes` field, check it.** This project has already shipped a fabricated citation — an orca eye patch documented as "21.8 x 5.9 cm, aspect 3.7:1, measured" in three separate places, where the numbers turned out to be two dimensionless indices lifted from an unrelated paper, and a second agent later found the identical trap in a different source. A number in a spec's notes acquires an authority it did not have in a planning document, and that is exactly how the orca figure survived.

The only references in this file are repo paths and line numbers, and each was verified by reading the file:

* `voxel-core/include/voxelcore/biome.h:23-42` — the `BiomeId` enum; DESERT is id 5 at line 29.
