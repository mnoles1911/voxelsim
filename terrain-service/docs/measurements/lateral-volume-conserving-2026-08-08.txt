THE LATERAL LEVEL RULE, MADE TO SPREAD WATER INSTEAD OF DELETE IT. Same tile,
same window as lateral-level-rule-2026-08-08.txt: tile (-4,-4), npz-lvl2
replayed offline, 2048 px on the wettest region, 63,466 wet cells. 2026-08-08.

THE DEFECT. The rule shipped at bake_ver 21 forces adjacent wet cells to share a
level by LOWERING ONLY. It fixed the surface -- adjacent-cell steps over one
voxel 20.76% -> 1.54% on this crop -- and it DELETED the water it lowered.
End to end the tile carried 17-31% less water than at bake_ver 17; (-4,-4) went
364,354 -> 249,900 wet cells. The owner's complaint has always been water
FAILING to fill low ground, so a rule that only removes works against its own
purpose.

THE FIX, in one sentence: levelling water does not destroy it, it spreads it.
This is the lesson apply_discharge_budget already paid for -- its first version
deleted the surplus, could not make a wide-and-shallow river on a flat floor,
and had to become a per-section level solve. The level solve is now a shared
helper, `_fill_to_volume`.

HOW IT WORKS NOW
  1. The lowering sweeps are UNCHANGED, and each one records which neighbour a
     cell was lowered toward. Following those links to a cell that was never
     lowered partitions the lowered water into PONDS; ponds whose cells touch
     are pooled into one body of water with one budget.
  2. The levelled plane is NOT re-solved. Every cell the sweeps touched keeps
     exactly the surface they gave it -- verified bit-identical on the crop.
  3. Each pond spends the water it lost wetting DRY GROUND that lies below the
     surface of the water beside it, deepest first, at that water's own level,
     reached ring by ring THROUGH THE WATER BODY as well as across the fringe.

TWO ANSWERS THAT LOOK RIGHT AND ARE NOT, both measured before the third was
written, against the delete-only rule's 54,908 wet / 1.54% over one voxel:
  re-solve each pond against its INPUT level     63,725 wet   20.21% >1vox
  re-solve each pond against its LEVELLED bottom 50,562 wet    6.31% >1vox
The first conserves 99.3% of the volume by standing 14,599 of the 26,790 lowered
cells back up -- it gives the water back by UNDOING THE RULE, and 20.21% is
where the plane started. The second is worse than deleting on both counts:
flattening a pond onto its lowest cell throws away more water than the levelling
did and drops the level out from under the water beside it.

THREE MECHANISMS THAT EACH MOVED THE NUMBER, in the order they were needed:
  * per-pond budgets only, fringe only        57,530 wet, 25% of the water placed
  * + conduits through existing water         57,911 wet, 27%
  * + budgets pooled across touching ponds    61,933 wet, but 4.03% >1vox
  * + a new cell may only join water that
    already agrees with itself to one voxel   60,073 wet, 1.51% >1vox
The last one is load-bearing: a dry cell wedged between two wet regions a metre
apart in level has no one surface to join, and admitting it re-broke the
levelling the water was paying for.

RESULT, 8 sweeps, tol one voxel, 16 rings
                       wet    >100mm  >300mm  dry-below  volume
  bv18 shipped      63,466    20.76%   5.05%      9,215  106,121 m3
  delete-only       54,908     1.54%   0.51%      8,717   83,016 m3   -13.5% wet
  THIS RULE         60,073     1.51%   0.52%     10,155   96,271 m3    -5.3% wet
The levelling is untouched -- 1.51% against 1.54%, and the over-300 mm steps are
identical -- while 5,165 of the 8,558 cells the levelling cost come back and the
water goes from -21.8% to -9.3%. It re-places 57.4% of what it takes; the other
9,850 m3 is water the ground beside it had no room for under a surface nothing
is allowed to raise.

THE BOUND IS THE VOLUME, DEMONSTRATED. Remove the budget and run the same rings:
63,466 -> 82,119 wet at 8 rings and 171,532 at 32, which is fill_to_local_
surface's 209x flood reproduced on demand. With the budget in place:
  rings      4        8       16       32       64
  wet    57,741   59,372   60,073   60,502   60,548
  >1vox   1.548%   1.524%   1.509%   1.498%   1.497%
32 and 64 agree to four figures. 16 is where the curve flattens; the whole stage
costs 2.1 s on the crop against the levelling's own 1.9 s.

WHAT GOT WORSE, recorded rather than smoothed over. Dry cells lying 0.1-5 m
below adjacent water go 8,717 -> 10,155, past even the 9,215 the plane started
with. Spreading water makes more shoreline and a shoreline is where dry ground
meets water above it. Split by depth the SHALLOW band (0.1-0.3 m) improves,
3,663 -> 3,169; the whole increase is 0.3-5 m, 5,054 -> 6,986, the new frontier
the extra 5,165 wet cells exposed and could not afford to cross.

THE TRADE THE OWNER MAY STILL WANT TO MAKE. Every additional wet cell past this
point costs surface smoothness, and the two ends of that trade are measured
above: 54,908 wet at 1.54%, or 63,725 wet at 20.21% (which is the rule switched
off in all but name). There is no setting between them that is free.

CAVEATS
 * OFFLINE. This is the shipped bv18 plane replayed, not a bake. No bake was run.
 * NO DRIED MASK. The npz does not carry the cells the discharge budget
   stranded, and in the bake those are refused as recipients. Blocking a random
   half of the dry cells as a sensitivity test gives 57,760 wet (-9.0%) and
   1.52% over one voxel, so the real recovery lies between that and 60,073.
 * Receivers are recomputed on the shipped post-B5 elevation while the bake
   routes on the pre-B5 surface, the same caveat the first measurement records.

INVARIANTS, checked on this crop and in tests/test_bake_pipeline.py
 * no cell that came in wet ends above its input level (phase 2 never touches
   one), and the function still asserts it before returning
 * no drawn channel cell is ever dried -- new in this change; the previous rule
   could and did dry them
 * no cell the discharge budget stranded is re-wetted
 * nothing is reached across dry ground: a hole behind a sill stays dry
 * the delete-only branch is kept (water_lateral_conserve_volume=False) so
   bake_ver 21 stays reproducible
