# Design Doc: Terrain Amplification Pipeline — 30 m Diffusion Tiles → 10 cm Voxels

**Status:** Draft for engineering review
**Context:** Cloud GPU serves InfiniteDiffusion tiles (30 m/px elevation + 4 climate channels) to a local UE5 client. Client voxelizes into a chunk-streaming system (working). Current 30 m → 10 cm upsampling uses unconditioned simple noise and reads as unrealistic/random.
**Goal:** Maximally realistic Earth-like terrain at 10 cm per cubic voxel, while preserving the properties that make the streaming system work: **determinism, tile-local generation, O(1) random access, real-time budgets.**

---

## 0. Why the current approach fails (diagnosis)

Adding generic noise (white noise, uniform fBm/Perlin) on top of an interpolated 30 m surface fails for four specific reasons. Every idea in this doc targets one or more of them.

1. **Wrong spectrum.** Real terrain detail follows a power-law spectrum whose slope and amplitude vary by landform. Uniform noise has one global spectrum → reads as "TV static draped over felt."
2. **No spatial conditioning.** Real micro-relief depends on slope, curvature, drainage, and climate. Noise that ignores these violates the "erosion grammar" the eye is fluent in (channels in hollows, talus below cliffs, smooth alluvium in valley floors).
3. **Not consistency-constrained.** If added detail doesn't integrate to ~zero over each parent cell, the terrain drifts from the diffusion model's output and low-frequency lumps appear.
4. **Isotropic and continuous only.** Real ground at 10 cm is substantially made of *discrete objects* (boulders, clasts, hummocks, ledges) and *anisotropic* features (rills aligned with gradient, strata, ripples). Scalar isotropic noise cannot produce either.

The fix is not "better noise." It is a **conditioned amplification stack**: each octave of new detail is generated by the process that dominates at that scale, conditioned on data derived from the coarser levels.

**Resolution ladder** (each stage owns one band of the spectrum):

| Band | Owner | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 60 m wavelength | Diffusion model (existing) | InfiniteDiffusion 30 m tiles |
| 60 m → ~2 m | **[2] Learned SR** (cloud) | Conditional SR net trained on 1 m lidar |
| ~2 m → ~0.5 m | **[5]+[6]** (client) | Regime-conditioned spectral noise + geomorph passes |
| ~0.5 m → 10 cm | **[7]+[8]** (client) | Discrete clasts, volumetric perturbation, stratigraphy |

**Invariant (applies to every stage):** output is a pure function of `(world_seed, stage_id, tile_coords, parent_tile_data)`. No global mutable state, no simulation passes requiring unbounded context.

**Consistency invariant:** detail added at stage *k* must have (approximately) zero mean over each parent cell of stage *k−1*. Enforce explicitly (see §5.3, §6.4).

## 2. Idea #1 (highest impact): Learned super-resolution stage, 30 m → 1 m

### Rationale
The band from 60 m down to ~2 m wavelength (gullies, stream banks, outcrop structure, terracettes, landslide scars) is where realism is won or lost, and it is the **only sub-30 m band with real training data available**. No procedural method reproduces this band as convincingly as a model trained on real 1 m DEMs. This mirrors exactly what the base project already does between 7.7 km and 30 m — you are extending the hierarchy one level down.

### Data
- **USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar DEMs** (public domain, covers most of CONUS, diverse climates/lithologies).
- Supplement with European national lidar (e.g., UK EA 1 m, Netherlands AHN, Swiss swissALTI3D) for regime diversity.
- Build paired training data by downsampling 1 m → 30 m (matching the diffusion model's effective PSF as closely as possible — measure it, don't assume box filter).
- Conditioning channels per patch: 30 m elevation, slope, the 4 climate variables (sampled from WorldClim at patch location, same source the base model trained on), and **flow accumulation computed on the 30 m grid** (see §3).

### Model
- Start with the same architecture family as the existing SR stages in `terrain-diffusion` (EDM-style diffusion) so InfiniteDiffusion's lazy tiled sampling applies unchanged; the repo's `annotated_infinite_panorama.py` is the reference for the tiling contract.
- 32× SR in one hop is aggressive; prefer **two chained stages** (30 m → 5–6 m → 1 m) or a single stage at 16× to 2 m. Evaluate both; the 2 m endpoint may be sufficient given stages [5]–[7] own everything below ~2 m.
- **Latency mitigation (important):** a full diffusion SR at this density will dominate cloud cost. Plan a distillation path: train the diffusion model first (quality ceiling), then distill to a 1–4 step consistency/flow model or a plain regression CNN + GAN loss for serving. Keep the diffusion model as the offline "ground truth" generator for validation.
- Predict **residual detail** (1 m minus upsampled 30 m), not absolute elevation. This makes the zero-mean consistency constraint trivial to enforce (subtract per-parent-cell mean of the residual before adding) and prevents drift.

### Serving & protocol
- Cloud serves two tile products: `T30` (existing) and `T1` (new, residual detail + derived channels). Client requests `T1` only inside the near/mid LOD rings (§10).
- Tiles are immutable and content-addressed: `hash(model_version, world_seed, tile_xy, stage)`. Cache aggressively cloud-side and client-side (disk). `T1` tiles are the expensive artifact; everything below them is regenerable locally in milliseconds.
- Ship `T1` as 16-bit quantized residual (cm precision over ±~50 m residual range is plenty) + zstd. Budget ~0.5–2 MB per 1 km² tile.
- **Fallback:** if `T1` is late (network/GPU), client renders from `T30` + procedural stages only, and hot-swaps when `T1` arrives. Because stages [5]+ are seeded independently of `T1` content only through parent values, the swap is a crossfade of the carrier surface, not a full regeneration.

### Acceptance criteria
- Radially averaged power spectrum of generated 1 m terrain matches real 1 m lidar within regime classes (slope of the power law within ±0.15).
- Hillshade A/B panels (generated vs. real, same climate/slope class) pass blind review by the team at better than chance.

## 3. Idea #2: Hydrology conditioning (server-side flow accumulation)

### Rationale
Drainage is the one thing tile-local generation cannot invent coherently: a channel's existence at (x, y) depends on square kilometers of upstream terrain. Without it you get valleys with no streams and gullies that start and stop randomly — a top-3 realism killer. The fix is the standard trick: **do the non-local reasoning at the coarse resolution where it's cheap, ship it down as a conditioning channel.**

### Implementation
- On the cloud, after generating 30 m elevation for a region, compute:
  - **D8 or MFD flow accumulation** over a generous apron (e.g., 4096² window centered on the served region; recompute lazily as the frontier expands). Priority-flood depression filling first (Barnes et al. algorithm; fast, standard).
  - **Stream raster**: `flow_accum > threshold`, thinned to centerlines, with Strahler order.
  - Optional: distance-to-stream and height-above-nearest-drainage (HAND) rasters — extremely useful downstream for floodplain/terrace/bank logic.
- Serve these as extra channels on `T30` tiles. They condition:
  - the SR net (§2) — trained with real flow-accum as input so it learns to incise channels where flow says so;
  - client-side rill carving (§6.2) and regime classification (§4);
  - later: actual water voxel placement, river width = f(flow, precip).
- **Determinism note:** flow accumulation over a window is not strictly O(1) random access. Make it deterministic by fixing the window policy (window derived from tile coords by a fixed rule, e.g., aligned 4096² superblocks with overlap blending), and version it. Superblock results are cached/content-addressed like tiles.
- **Edge handling:** superblock borders will have truncated catchments. Mitigate with a coarse global pass: run flow accumulation once on the *coarse map* (7.7 km px) to estimate inflow at superblock edges and inject as boundary conditions. Crude but removes the worst artifacts (major rivers dying at superblock seams).

### Cost
Priority-flood + MFD on 4096² is sub-second on CPU, trivial on GPU. Negligible next to diffusion inference.

## 4. Idea #3: Landform/material regime classifier (the "250-line layer")

### Rationale
The Minecraft mod's most transferable lesson: keep semantics as a thin, hand-tunable, rule-based layer between the neural output and everything downstream (their biome classifier is ~250 lines of Java driven by elevation + 4 climate variables). For us, the analog is a **surface regime map** — the single control signal that makes noise, erosion, scattering, and materials all stop looking random, because they all become functions of *what kind of ground this is*.

### Inputs (per 1 m column, all cheap derivatives of `T1`/`T30`)
`elevation, slope, plan curvature, profile curvature, flow accumulation, HAND, temperature, temp_std, precipitation, precip_cv, (optional) aspect`

### Output
A regime ID + blend weights (soft classification, top-2 regimes with weights, so transitions dither instead of hard-edging). Initial regime set:

| Regime | Trigger sketch | Drives |
|---|---|---|
| `BEDROCK_OUTCROP` | slope > ~50°, or slope > 35° with low soil-index | high-freq angular noise, strata, overhang carving |
| `TALUS_SCREE` | below outcrop (downslope within N m of `BEDROCK`), slope ≈ 30–38° | clast scattering (power-law sizes), angle-of-repose relaxation |
| `BLOCKFIELD` | cold (freeze-thaw index high) + moderate slope + rocky | dense large-clast scattering, minimal soil |
| `SOIL_TURF` | moderate slope, positive soil index | low-amp smooth noise, hummocks, terracettes on steeper turf |
| `ALLUVIUM_FLOODPLAIN` | HAND low + near stream + low slope | very smooth, micro-channel braiding, bank geometry |
| `CHANNEL` | on stream raster | carve incised channel; width/depth = f(flow, precip) |
| `WETLAND` | flat + high precip + concave | hummock/hollow micro-topo, water table voxels |
| `DUNES` | arid (precip low) + sand-index + low relief | anisotropic ripple/dune noise aligned to a deterministic wind field |
| `BEACH_COASTAL` | near sea level + adjacent to ocean mask | planar swash slope, berms |
| `GLACIAL/PERIGLACIAL` (later) | cold + high elevation | moraine texture, polygon ground |

Soil index: a simple deterministic function `f(profile curvature, slope, precip, temp)` — thick soil in concave/gentle/wet/warm, zero on convex steep cold ridges. This one scalar does double duty for regime selection and stratigraphy depth (§8.2).

### Engineering
- Implement as a branchless table-driven function (score per regime = weighted feature thresholds; softmax top-2). Runs in the same compute-shader pass that computes slope/curvature from `T1`. Target: < 0.2 ms per 512² tile on GPU.
- Keep it hot-reloadable (data asset / JSON thresholds) — this layer is where artists/designers will iterate constantly, exactly as the mod author predicted for biomes.
- Debug view: colorize voxel surface by regime in a UE5 debug CVar. This is the single most useful tuning tool; build it first.

## 5. Idea #4: Regime-conditioned spectral detail synthesis (replaces the current noise)

### Rationale
Between the SR output (~1–2 m) and the clast scale (~0.5 m) you still need continuous stochastic relief — but with the **right spectrum per regime** and the **right anisotropy**, seeded deterministically. This is "noise by example": measure real ground, replay its statistics.

### 5.1 Fit spectra offline
- From the same 1 m lidar corpus (plus any 0.25–0.5 m lidar you can source, e.g., some 3DEP/state datasets), compute per-regime radially averaged residual spectra (residual = DEM minus 30 m-smoothed version), bucketed by the regime classifier run on real data.
- Distill each regime into a small table: per-octave gain `g[regime][octave]` for octaves from 2 m down to 12.5 cm (5 octaves), plus an anisotropy descriptor (see 5.2). Store as a tiny texture/asset.

### 5.2 Runtime synthesis
- Base primitive: **tileable gradient noise (or wavelet noise, which is band-limited and superior for octave control)** evaluated per octave, seeded by `hash(world_seed, stage, octave, tile_xy)`. Wavelet noise's tight band-limiting matters here: it lets each octave land in its assigned spectral slot without leaking energy into bands owned by other stages.
- Per column: `detail = Σ_octaves g[regime][o] * noise_o(x, y)` with regime gains blended by the soft classification weights.
- **Anisotropy where it matters:**
  - Slope-aligned stretching for turf terracettes and rills: shear the noise domain along the downslope direction (from the gradient of the 1 m surface).
  - Wind-aligned for `DUNES`: deterministic global wind direction field (function of coarse coords + seed), ripple wavelength from real ripple statistics (~10–20 cm wavelength at the fine octave, dune-scale at coarse octaves).
- **Gradient modulation (cheap, big win):** multiply high-octave gain by a function of local slope *and* regime, and add a "ruggedization" term where profile curvature is convex. Real roughness concentrates on convexities and steeps; valley fills are smooth. Uniform gain is precisely what makes the current output look like static.

### 5.3 Consistency
- Wavelet/gradient noise is zero-mean by construction over large areas, but enforce it locally anyway: after summing octaves for a tile, subtract the per-2 m-cell mean of the synthesized detail (one small reduction pass). This guarantees the 1 m surface's shape survives.

### 5.4 Engineering
- One compute shader pass over the tile's 25 cm working grid (see §10 for why 25 cm, not 10 cm, is the heightfield working resolution): inputs `T1` height + derivative textures + regime map; output residual detail texture. Target < 1 ms per 512² region.
- Kill the existing noise path entirely rather than layering on top of it; its spectrum will pollute the calibrated one.

## 6. Idea #5: Fast local geomorphic passes

### Rationale
Noise alone, however well-calibrated, is still *texture*. Two or three cheap process-based passes create the causal structures — connected rills, angle-of-repose aprons, soil filling hollows — that make ground read as *shaped* rather than *textured*. Constraint: strictly tile-local, fixed iteration count, deterministic.

### 6.1 Thermal (slope-limited) relaxation
- On the tile's working grid (post-§5), run K iterations (K ≈ 8–16, fixed) of thermal erosion: for each cell, if slope to a neighbor exceeds the regime's talus angle (≈ 33–38° granular, effectively ∞ for `BEDROCK_OUTCROP` which is exempt), move a fraction of the excess height downslope.
- Effect: interpolation creases and noise spikes collapse into natural angle-of-repose forms; the bases of steep faces automatically grow talus ramps. This one pass removes most of the "math surface" look.
- Jacobi-style (gather) formulation → embarrassingly parallel, order-independent, deterministic. Run with a 16-cell apron so tile borders converge identically to their neighbors (§9).

### 6.2 Micro-rill and channel carving from flow
- Use the shipped flow-accumulation/stream channels (§3) plus a *local* flow-routing pass on the 1 m surface (D8 over the tile + apron, seeded with the coarse flow at the apron boundary as inflow).
- Carve: `depth = a * flow^b` (b ≈ 0.3–0.4), width similarly, with a parabolic/trapezoid cross-section stamped along the local drainage lines; banks get a distinct sub-regime (`CHANNEL_BANK`) so §7 can roughen them and §8 can expose strata.
- On erodible regimes (`SOIL_TURF`, `TALUS`), additionally carve micro-rills every few meters where local flow exceeds a small threshold — this reproduces natural drainage density, one of the strongest realism signals on hillslopes.

### 6.3 Deposition / soil smoothing
- Where the soil index (§4) is high, blend the surface toward a smoothed copy (soil drapes and buries micro-relief) and *raise* concavities slightly (colluvial fill). This is a two-texture lerp keyed on soil index — nearly free, and it produces the ridge-sharp/valley-smooth asymmetry real terrain has.

### 6.4 Consistency & budget
- Thermal relaxation conserves mass by construction; carving/deposition do not — track the per-tile net volume change and subtract its low-frequency component (per-8 m-cell mean) so the 30 m/1 m surfaces remain authoritative.
- All three passes are compute-shader friendly; combined target < 2 ms per streamed region. Fixed iteration counts, no convergence loops (determinism + budget).

## 7. Idea #6: Discrete clast and feature scattering

### Rationale
At 10 cm, a large fraction of visible ground is **objects**: boulders, cobbles, ledges, hummocks, fallen blocks. No continuous field produces these. They also directly exploit the voxel medium — a boulder you can see under is something a heightmap can't be.

### Implementation
- **Placement:** deterministic blue-noise point process per regime — e.g., jittered-grid or Poisson-disk via per-cell hash `hash(seed, "clast", cell_xy)` deciding presence, size, rotation. Density and size follow per-regime distributions:
  - `TALUS/BLOCKFIELD`: power-law size distribution (many small, few large; exponent ~ −2.5), high density, sizes 0.2–3 m, angular.
  - `SOIL_TURF`: sparse rounded erratics + hummocks (soft bumps merged into the field rather than discrete SDFs).
  - `CHANNEL/ALLUVIUM`: imbricated rounded cobbles 10–40 cm concentrated in the channel bed, sorted by flow (bigger clasts where flow is high).
  - `BEDROCK_OUTCROP`: detached blocks at ledge bases; density seeded by local relief.
- **Geometry:** each clast is an SDF primitive — a distorted superellipsoid or a small set of intersecting half-space cuts for angular talus (3–6 random planes → convex angular block; cheap and reads perfectly at 10 cm). Rotation/burial depth randomized; bury 20–60 % below the surface so clasts sit *in* the ground, not on it.
- **Composition:** clasts are unioned (smooth-min, small k) into the volumetric field during §8 voxelization, tagged with a rock material distinct from surrounding soil → correct material boundaries for gameplay/destruction later.
- **Physical plausibility check (cheap):** reject or slide clast positions where local slope exceeds repose *unless* regime is `BLOCKFIELD/TALUS` (where perched blocks are expected). One hash-seeded nudge downslope is enough; don't simulate.
- **Budget:** clasts per chunk bounded by regime cap; evaluation is per-voxel SDF sampling against a small spatial-hash bucket of nearby clasts (build the bucket list on chunk-gen, ~dozens of primitives per 8 m chunk worst case).

## 8. Idea #7: Volumetric conversion — SDF terrain, stratigraphy, overhangs

### Rationale
Everything above still yields a heightfield. At 10 cm a pure heightfield is a visible lie: no overhangs, no undercut banks, no ledged cliff faces, uniform "dirt all the way down." The voxel medium's whole payoff is 3D structure; spend it where it's visible: **steep faces and channel banks**.

### 8.1 Density/SDF field
- Voxelization samples a signed density: `d(p) = surface_height(p.xy) − p.z + Σ perturbations(p) ∪ clasts(p)`.
- Keep the base field a heightfield-SDF (cheap, exact) and add volumetric terms only where masks allow (steepness mask, bank mask) so the common case stays a 2D texture fetch.

### 8.2 Stratigraphy (materials from depth model)
- Per column, compute layer boundaries top-down: `organic/turf (0–0.2 m, regime-gated) → soil (soil_index-driven, 0–3 m) → regolith/saprolite (0.5–2 m) → bedrock`.
- Voxel material = layer at that depth. Instantly gives: cut banks exposing soil profiles, thin-soiled ridges with bedrock voxels at the surface, correct digging/mining feel, and a huge realism win in any excavated or eroded exposure.
- Bedrock gets a **strata field**: material and micro-displacement banded by a deterministic 3D function `strata(p) = f(dot(p, dip_normal(region)) + fold_noise)`, with regional dip/strike seeded from coarse coords. Cliff faces then show bedding — the strongest single cue for "real rock."

### 8.3 Overhangs and ledges on steep faces
- Where slope > ~55° (mask from §4/§6): perturb the SDF with medium-frequency 3D noise *aligned to the strata field* — displacement stronger along weak strata → natural ledge/recess banding, overhanging resistant beds, chimneys where a vertical joint-noise term dominates.
- Channel banks (`CHANNEL_BANK` mask): apply a lateral undercut term near water level (`d += undercut(p.z − water_z)`), producing cut banks that overhang the stream — very high realism per flop.
- Keep total volumetric displacement bounded (≤ ~1.5 m) so it can't violate the 1 m surface's silhouette at distance, preserving LOD consistency.

### 8.4 Optional later: karst/caves
- Deterministic 3D ridged/Worley tunnel field gated by climate (warm+wet) and a carbonate flag (could be derived from a coarse lithology noise). Out of scope for v1; the SDF architecture above makes it a pure additive term later.

## 9. Determinism, seams, and the tile contract

Non-negotiable rules for every stage:

1. **Pure functions.** `output_tile = F(world_seed, stage_id, tile_coords, parent_data)`. All randomness via counter-based hashes (e.g., PCG/xxhash of `(seed, stage, feature, cell)`), never sequential RNG state.
2. **Aprons, not global sync.** Every local pass (thermal, rills, scattering) computes over `tile + apron` where apron ≥ the pass's influence radius (thermal: K cells; rills: local routing distance; clasts: max clast radius). Two neighboring tiles then compute *identical* values in their shared apron → seamless by construction, no stitching pass, chunks remain independently regenerable.
3. **Parent-conditioned only.** A stage may read any already-cached coarser product covering its apron, never sibling fine tiles. This preserves O(1) random access and lets the streamer generate chunks in any order.
4. **Version everything.** `(model_version, spectra_table_version, classifier_version, pass_params_version)` participate in cache keys. A parameter tweak invalidates cleanly instead of producing world seams between old and new chunks.
5. **Ocean/sea-level and world-vertical policy** fixed as constants of the world (the MC mod pegs max relief to 10 000 m real-world; adopt an equivalent explicit datum so all stages agree on `water_z`).

## 10. Performance, LOD, and budgets

### Working resolutions
- Do **heightfield work at 25 cm**, not 10 cm: passes §5–§6 on a 25 cm grid, then let §7 clasts and §8 volumetric terms (evaluated per-voxel at 10 cm) supply the final octave. Sub-25 cm continuous relief is visually dominated by clasts/vegetation anyway; this cuts heightfield compute and memory ~6×.
- Suggested grid stack per streamed region: `T30 (server) → T1 residual (server) → 25 cm working grid (client GPU) → 10 cm voxels (client)`.

### LOD rings (around player)
| Ring | Radius (tune) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| R0 | 0–150 m | Full stack: 10 cm voxels, clasts, volumetric SDF |
| R1 | 150–600 m | 25 cm/50 cm voxels from working grid; clasts above ~0.5 m only; no volumetric carving except silhouette-safe |
| R2 | 600 m–3 km | Heightfield mesh/voxels from `T1` (1 m) directly |
| R3 | beyond | `T30`-derived far mesh |
Because §8.3 bounds volumetric displacement and all detail is zero-mean vs. parents, ring transitions are silhouette-stable; blend with vertex-morph or dither, standard clipmap practice.

### Budget targets (per 512 m × 512 m region, client GPU, order-of-magnitude)
| Pass | Target |
|---|---|
| Derivatives + regime classify | < 0.3 ms |
| Spectral synthesis (5 octaves) | < 1 ms |
| Thermal + rills + deposition | < 2 ms |
| Clast list build | < 0.5 ms CPU |
| Voxelization incl. SDF/strata/clast sampling | dominated by existing voxelizer; clast+strata terms ≈ +30–50 % per-voxel cost in R0 only |

### Caching
- Client disk-caches `T30`, flow superblocks, and `T1` (immutable, content-addressed). Working grids and voxels are regenerated, never persisted (edits/dig operations persist as sparse deltas over the deterministic base — free "infinite world, finite save").

## 11. Validation & tuning tools (build these early)

1. **Regime debug colorizer** (§4) — CVar surface tint by regime. First tool to build.
2. **Spectrum probe:** in-editor command that samples a region, computes the radially averaged residual power spectrum, and overlays the target per-regime curve. Directly answers "does our noise match reality" without eyeballing.
3. **Hillshade A/B harness:** render top-down hillshade of generated 1 m/25 cm terrain next to real lidar hillshade of matched regime/climate patches; blind-review internally. This catches "grammar" violations spectra miss.
4. **Geomorph metrics dashboard (offline):** drainage density, slope–area relationship, hypsometric curve, talus-angle histogram vs. real-DEM references. Run on CI when spectra tables/classifier change.
5. **Artifact checklist** (regression-test seeds/locations): terrace lines on flats, grid-aligned creases, streams dying at superblock seams, clasts floating/perched on steeps, silhouette pop at LOD transitions.
