# Tree asset generator — build plan

Date: 2026-08-09
Research and prior art: `docs/tree-asset-generator-research.md`

**Status 2026-08-09.** Phases 1 and 2 are built and working — `asset-forge/`,
**twelve species**, deterministic, ~40-800 ms per tree depending on size, with
a local web app (`python -m forge.cli serve`) for browsing variants, turning
sliders and keeping approved trees to a library.

Phase 0 (the engine side) is **deferred, not skipped**: it needs the editor
box, which another session holds. Nothing generated here can be stamped into
the world until Phase 0 lands, for the two reasons in section 8 of the research
doc.

Known gaps, in the order I would take them:

1. **A strand pass** — long trailing branches, for weeping willows, lianas and
   hanging moss. Space colonization cannot produce these: targets are consumed
   on arrival, so a branch stops rather than trailing. The species that wanted
   it is currently shipped as `river-broadleaf`, named for what it actually is
   rather than for the willow it is not.
2. **A whorl skeleton backend**, after vengi's `tree_pine`, selected per spec.
   Conifers, palms and araucaria are all better generated by explicit whorls
   than by approximating them with a crown envelope.
3. **Bark noise** — large trunks rasterize into visibly fluted cylinders.
4. **Phase 3, plain-language editing.** The parameter table is already served
   as JSON for exactly this; the model edits the spec, never the voxels.

Working name: **asset-forge**, at `D:\voxelsim\asset-forge\`.

An app that generates cubic-voxel trees by the hundreds, takes designer feedback
through sliders and plain language, and keeps the approved ones in a library the
game can draw from.

Trees are real voxels in the world — destructible and diggable — so every asset
has to satisfy the engine's 10 cm grid and its material set, not just look right
in a preview.

---

## 1. The one idea the whole thing hangs on

**A species is a JSON file. Everything reads and writes that one file.**

Sliders write it. Plain-language requests patch it. Batch generation reads it.
A seed varies within it. The library stores it. Nothing downstream needs to know
whether a change came from a slider, a sentence, or a preset.

That means a tree is never a blob of voxels we have to keep. It is
`(species spec, seed)` — a few hundred bytes that regenerate the exact same
voxels every time. That property is what makes "hundreds of variants" cheap, and
it is the same determinism discipline the rest of the repo already runs on
(`docs/determinism.md`).

## 2. How a tree gets made

Four steps, each independently testable.

1. **Envelope** — the spec describes the crown as a volume: a sphere, a cone, a
   flat-topped umbrella, a wind-sheared wedge. Scatter attraction points inside
   it.
2. **Skeleton** — grow branches toward those points (space colonization, §3 of
   the research doc). Points get consumed as branches reach them, so branches
   compete for space and never intersect. The result is a set of branch curves
   with a radius at every point. Trunk behaviour, taper, droop and split angles
   come from Weber–Penn-style parameters in the spec.
3. **Rasterize** — draw each branch segment straight into a 10 cm voxel grid as
   a capsule, with a **hard minimum radius of one voxel**. This is the step that
   makes the difference. Generating a mesh and voxelizing it — the obvious plan —
   loses every branch thinner than 10 cm, and the crown can end up not connected
   to the trunk at all. Drawing into the grid directly means connectivity is a
   decision we enforce, not an outcome we hope for.
4. **Foliage** — clump leaf voxels around branch tips, with density, droop and
   clumpiness from the spec. Dead desert trees just set this to zero.

Only step 2 is real algorithm work, and it is a few hundred lines.

## 3. Where the assets go

- **`.vox`** for the Blender and MagicaVoxel round trip. Trees over 256 voxels
  on any axis (a 30 m jungle emergent is 300) get split into several models,
  which is how MagicaVoxel handles large scenes anyway.
- **A compact native format** for the engine: bounding box, palette of material
  IDs, run-length or sparse body. This is what actually ships.
- **Thumbnails** for the gallery — the app's real output, because the choosing
  is the designer's job and it happens by eye.

## 4. Heroes and filler, from the same spec

Both come out of one pipeline:

- A **hero** is an approved `(spec, seed)` pair — you looked at that exact tree
  and kept it.
- **Filler** is the same species at other seeds.

**Recommendation: pre-bake a bank of seeds per species rather than generating
trees at runtime.** Say 64 seeds each across 40 species — that is 2,560 distinct
trees, far past the point a player notices repetition, and it means the
generator never has to be ported to C++, never runs on the streaming thread, and
never becomes a source of frame-time variance. The world picks a bank entry from
the biome and a position hash. If a species later turns out to look repetitive,
raise its seed count and re-bake; nothing else changes.

## 5. The app

A local web UI, served by a small Python process.

- **Gallery first.** Generate 100, show a wall of thumbnails, click to keep. The
  gallery is the primary screen, not a detail view — the core action is
  choosing, and choosing needs many trees on screen at once.
- **Sliders** on the selected species, live-regenerating.
- **A text box** that patches the spec: "shorter, more gnarled, sparser canopy".
  The model edits parameters, never voxels — precedent and reasoning in §5 of
  the research doc.
- **Every thumbnail carries its conditions** — species, seed, spec version — so
  a judgement is always attached to something reproducible.

Web rather than a desktop toolkit purely because a hundred-thumbnail grid with
keep/reject is a solved problem in a browser and a chore anywhere else.

## 6. Phases

**Phase 0 — prove a tree can exist at all (no generator).**
Append tree materials to the `Material` enum, give them mine costs, hand-author
a crude box "tree" 12 m tall, stamp it on real terrain, then confirm two things
in a capture: it renders, and it can be dug.

This phase exists because of the finding in §8B of the research doc — the
streaming layer skips generating chunks that `surfaceUpperBoundMm` proves empty,
and a tree is solid *above* the surface, so a crown can land in a chunk the
engine has already decided is air. Until the surface bound accounts for
vegetation height, every tree we generate is invisible. A box proves this for a
day's work; a generator would prove it for a month's.

Also covers the three tails in §8A: mine costs, the LOD majority arrays, and the
mesher digest trap that a material append has sprung once before.

**Phase 1 — generator core, headless.**
Spec schema, skeleton, rasterizer, foliage, `.vox` export, CLI. Deliverable is a
contact sheet: one species, 100 seeds, rendered as thumbnails. Judged by eye.

**Phase 2 — the app.**
Web UI, sliders, gallery, keep/reject, library on disk. At the end of this phase
the tool is usable and the species count starts growing.

**Phase 3 — plain-language feedback.**
Text to spec patch, with the render shown back for judgement.

**Phase 4 — into the game.**
Bake seed banks, place by biome, chop and dig behaviour, LOD at distance.

**Phase 5 — later, optional.**
Once the library holds a few hundred approved trees, that library is training
data; a learned voxel model can propose new ones in the established style
(Scaffold Diffusion, research doc §5). Not a starting point — it needs the
library to exist first.

## 7. Species targets

The biome list gives the first pass: small and large temperate broadleaf,
tundra/taiga pine, jungle emergent and understorey, savanna acacia, dead desert
tree, plus the non-tree scatter that comes later (rocks, boulders, bushes,
reeds, grass, flowers) — the same pipeline handles those with a different
skeleton step, or none at all.

## 8. Main risks

1. **The empty-chunk bound** (§8B research doc). Phase 0 exists to hit this
   first. If raising the surface bound by a vegetation allowance turns out to
   cost real streaming performance, that is a design conversation, and it is far
   better to have it now than after a library exists.
2. **Trees at distance.** A forest of 100-voxel trees is a lot of geometry. The
   LOD chain reduces by material majority (`mips.h:60-77`); thin branches will
   lose to air and trees will thin out or vanish as they recede. Worth a
   deliberate look during Phase 4, not a surprise.
3. **10 cm is coarse for foliage.** A believable leaf cluster at 10 cm is a
   design problem as much as a technical one, and it will need eyes on real
   captures early — which is why Phase 1's deliverable is a contact sheet rather
   than a passing test.
