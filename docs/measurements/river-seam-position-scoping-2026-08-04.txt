THE ANSWER FIRST, IN TWO PARTS.

  1. THE TWO CURRENCIES ARE SEPARABLE. Moving the discharge entry cell while
     the area entry cell stays put is a ~10-line change, water-only, and leaves
     the terrain bit-identical. BAKE_VERSION alone; TERRAIN_VERSION untouched.

  2. IT WOULD NOT FIX ANYTHING, BECAUSE THE DEFECT IS NOT POSITION. Measured on
     the shipped bv12 dumps, the river crosses the seam into the RIGHT CELL --
     one fine pixel, 1.88 m, from where it left the upstream tile. What it
     loses is 14.6% of its MAGNITUDE, in that correct cell. And the fix the
     record names -- "place the entry cell by the fine surface's own
     accumulation maximum" -- is measurably the WORST of the three candidate
     rules, not the best.

  So: nothing was built. This is the negative, with the measurements behind it.

Seven confident claims about this river have already been wrong. This document
makes two of them explicit, because both are currently written down as fact:
docs/measurements/river-seam-and-width-2026-08-04.txt lines 108-133 and

PART 0 -- WHERE THE OLD MEASUREMENT WENT WRONG
================================================================================

The record says: "the coarse D8 trunk sits at 30 m column 886 (x = -157,740)
and the fine trunk at column 880 (x = -157,905) -- 165 m apart", and concludes
that the injected water "does not arrive where the upstream tile's own
accumulation arrives".

Two things are wrong with that, and they compound.

FIRST, THE 165 m WAS MEASURED AT THE WRONG LINE. `_edge_entries` is called with
`child_z = filled` and `child_origin_m = geom.padded_origin_m(...)`, i.e. the
PADDED domain (pipeline.py:3915-3921). The deposit therefore lands on the
outermost 30 m ring of the padded domain -- 960 m OUTSIDE the interior, in the
apron. The 165 m figure was taken at the INTERIOR boundary, where nothing is
injected. Geometry, for the record:

    tile (tx,ty) interior world fine rows = ty*8192 .. ty*8192+8191
    (-11,-6) interior  rows -49152..-40961
    (-11,-6) PADDED    rows -49664..-40449
    so (-11,-6)'s injection line is world row -40449
       == (-11,-5) LOCAL ROW 511, not (-11,-5) row 0.

SECOND, THE WRONG RIVER WAS FOLLOWED. The reach the long profile walks -- the
one that loses 14.6% at km 5.2 -- carries 3.354e6 m3/yr and crosses at tile
-11 local col 2428/2429. The trunk at col 5896 that the seam record measured
carries 2.316e7, seven times more; it is a different, larger river 2.7 km east.
Both cross the same seam. The 14.6% belongs to the small one.

  Confirmed against the long profile's own CSV (D:/tmp/longprofile-bv12.csv,
  index 2345, chain 5221.678 m): composed row 8192 col 10620 tile (-11,-5)
  Q 3.353682e6 -> composed row 8191 col 10621 tile (-11,-6) Q 2.864686e6. Those
  map to tile-local (row 0, col 2428) and (row 8191, col 2429).

PART 2 -- MOVING THE ENTRY CHANGES THE ARRIVAL BY 2 METRES
================================================================================

WHERE THE REACH REALLY CROSSES THE INJECTION LINE. Traced upstream from
(-11,-5) row 0 col 2428 by max-Q donor over the 8-neighbourhood (a 3-cell
"one row per step" trace falls off this channel immediately -- the reach runs

    the reach crosses (-11,-5) row 511 at col 2240, carrying Q 3.244e6.

WHAT THE REAL PYRAMID CROSSES WITH. Superblock (-3,-2), level 0, 2048^2 @ 30 m,

carries q. 832 crossings into (-11,-6); 417 on the (-11,-5) face, carrying

        Q m3/yr    col    offset from the fine river (col 2240)

      1.665e+06   2816             +1080 m
      1.359e+06   1792              -840 m
      2.240e+05   2224               -30 m

So the position error at the injection line is 240 m -- 128 fine px, 8 coarse
cells -- not 165 m. The current rule cannot express it: the candidate set is
the 16x16 fine footprint of ONE 30 m parent cell, so the deposit can move at

AND IT DOES NOT MATTER. Routing each candidate deposit down to the interior
edge over (-11,-6)'s padded domain:

    entry rule                                 deposit   arrives   vs true 2428
    CURRENT (argmin z in the 30 m footprint)     2127      2429        +2 m
    the fine river's own cell                    2240      2429        +2 m
    argmax pre-injection fine acc, +/-240 m      2061      2227      -377 m

The current rule and the fine thalweg -- 212 m apart at the border -- converge
to the SAME cell 960 m downstream. That is the apron doing exactly what it is

  METHOD, AND ITS LIMIT. (-11,-6)'s padded domain is emulated over cols

================================================================================
PART 3 -- THE NAMED FIX IS THE WORST OPTION, AND HERE IS WHY
================================================================================

"Place the entry cell by the fine surface's own accumulation maximum" assumes
the fine accumulation carries a thalweg at the injection line. It does not, and
it cannot, BY CONSTRUCTION: the injection line is the OUTERMOST row of the
child's own padded domain, so nothing upstream of it exists inside that domain.
Measured on the emulated padded accumulation with no edge injection -- exactly
the field the proposed extra pre-pass sweep would produce:

    border row accumulation   p50 5.15 m2   max 2073 m2   (one cell = 3.52 m2)
    at the fine river (col 2240)      4.76 m2   RANK 765 of 1400

The river's own cell is at the 55th percentile of a field whose maximum is 590
cells' worth of area. There is no thalweg in it to snap to. Snapping anyway
sends the deposit to col 2061 and the water arrives 377 m off.

ELEVATION IS NO BETTER AS A GENERAL RULE, though it happens to work at one
radius here by coincidence:

    argmin z within +/-  15 m of the crossing -> col 2104   error 255.0 m
    argmin z within +/-  60 m                 -> col 2144   error 180.0 m
    argmin z within +/- 120 m                 -> col 2147   error 174.4 m
    argmin z within +/- 240 m                 -> col 2240   error   0.0 m
    argmin z within +/- 400 m                 -> col 2325   error 159.4 m
    argmin z within +/- 800 m                 -> col 2538   error 558.8 m

Zero error at exactly +/-240 m and 159-559 m either side of it is not a rule,
it is a fitted constant. The river's cell ranks 681 of 1400 by elevation over
the window: it is not a local minimum at all.


================================================================================
PART 4 -- WHERE THE 14.6% ACTUALLY GOES
================================================================================

The fine tier and the 30 m pyramid disagree about how much water is in this
channel, at the same line, before any placement question is asked:

    fine tier, the reach at (-11,-5) row 511 col 2240        3.244e6 m3/yr
    pyramid, crossings within +/- 60 m of col 2240           2.482e5   (5)
    pyramid, crossings within +/-120 m                       2.544e5   (9)
    pyramid, crossings within +/-240 m                       3.041e6  (15)
    pyramid, crossings within +/-480 m                       3.189e6  (31)

To match the fine tier's 3.244e6 the pyramid needs a 480 m-wide bite of its own
boundary -- 31 separate crossings, by then reaching into neighbouring channels.
Within the 240 m that actually belongs to this river it hands over 3.041e6, 6.3%
short, and the fine routing then loses a little more on the way in. That is the
14.6%.

This is a DISCRETISATION mismatch between the tiers, not a bug: (-11,-5)
generates this river's discharge from 256x more cells than the 30 m network
does, and a boundary condition sampled at 30 m cannot reproduce it exactly.
The lever on it is the MAGNITUDE the pyramid carries -- a finer pyramid level
at the boundary, or carrying the fine tier's own Q between tiles -- and not
where a cell is placed.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE for an entry-placement fix to help: the injected
water would have to arrive somewhere other than the fine trunk. PART 1 shows it
arrives one pixel away from it. It does not.


================================================================================
PART 5 -- THE SEPARATION, SCOPED BUT NOT BUILT
================================================================================

Recorded because the question was asked and because the answer is not the one
the code's own docstring implies -- so the next person to ask does not re-derive
it.

THE TWO CURRENCIES ARE ALREADY SEPARATE DOWNSTREAM. In `bake_tile`:

    _entries = _edge_entries(child_z=filled, ...)              pipeline.py:3915
    inflow   = _scatter_entries(_entries, inflow_source.acc, ...)      -> B2c
    inflow_q = _scatter_entries(_entries, inflow_source.q,  ...)       -> B6

  * `inflow` goes to `accumulate_mfd(..., inflow=inflow)` -> `acc64` -> B2d
    stream-power incision -> the ground.
  * `inflow_q` goes ONLY to `_water.discharge_source(...)` at B6, which feeds
    its own separate D8 sweep whose "only consumer is the `q >= q_drawable`
    threshold" (pipeline.py:4459-4465).

The single shared object is the `_entries` tuple. Computing a second entry set
for the discharge and leaving `inflow` untouched leaves `acc64` bit-identical,
hence incision bit-identical, hence the elevation plane bit-identical.
`tools/verify_water_only_change.py` is the gate that would prove it.

AND THE INVARIANT THE DOCSTRING PROTECTS IS ALREADY WEAKER THAN IT READS.
`_edge_entries` says both currencies "must cross the boundary at exactly the
same cell, or the width law reads a Q that belongs to a different channel than
the area the incision cut". But bake_ver 11 deliberately routes them by
DIFFERENT RULES over the same surface -- area by `accumulate_mfd` at
`consts.mfd_p`, discharge by `accumulate_d8` -- so they already diverge one
cell downstream of the shared entry. What survives today is a same-ENTRY-CELL
invariant, not a same-channel one, and separating the entries costs strictly
less than bv11 already spent.

SO IT IS AVAILABLE, CHEAPLY, IF A LATER CHANGE NEEDS IT. It is not built here
because nothing in PART 1-4 gives it a job.
