PART 2 -- THE WIDTH. RETRACTED AND RE-MEASURED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FIRST ANSWER (WRONG): "the terrain offers 11.2 m, the law asks 9.0 m, the bake
draws 3.8 m -- the extent rule leaves 3x the room unused."

HOW IT WAS WRONG. The transects were taken perpendicular to a hand-walked trunk
path, and the path was built from d8_receivers(fill_depressions(SHIPPED
surface)). The bake accumulated discharge on a DIFFERENT surface -- the B2a
fill, before meso, refill and reopen-basins. So the walked path is not the path
the water took, and the transects were not reliably perpendicular to flow. An
oblique transect inflates width by 1/cos.

HOW IT WAS CAUGHT. Discharge along that path swung over seven orders of
magnitude (1.2e6 -> 77 -> 7.1e6 -> 2.0e7 -> 281 -> 2.7e7 -> ... -> 9.7e7 m3/yr)
and DECREASED downstream on 13.6% of steps. Discharge cannot decrease along a
true D8 path. The path was wrong, not the world.

SECOND ANSWER, using the bake's own instrument. `water.lateral_extent_stats`
samples wet cells and steps perpendicular to EACH CELL'S OWN D8 receiver -- no
path walking, so the failure above cannot occur. Receivers from
d8_receivers(shipped surface), no fill, which is the call pipeline.py:4785
makes. "drawn" comes from the same function with a substitute ground field that
is -inf on drawn-wet cells and +inf elsewhere, so its `ground <= water` test
becomes exactly "is this cell drawn" -- same geometry, same sampling, no
reimplementation.

  wet alpine block, bake_ver 13, 20,000 sampled transects per tile, metres

    tile      stat     p50    p90    p95    p99     law p50   drawn/law p50

  THE DRAWN RIVER IS 10-17x WIDER THAN THE LAW, NOT NARROWER. The bake's own
  overshoot gate `ratio_over_4_frac` fires on 79.9%, 80.8% and 87.5% of sampled
  cells on the three tiles.

  The extent rule uses 75-82% of the room available to it (drawn/room p50), not
  a third. The room is not going unused.

RECONCILING THIS WITH water-deep-dive-brief 4.1, WHICH SAYS THE OPPOSITE. Both
are right; they are different populations.

  * 4.1 measures the CENTRELINE at km 20 of one composed reach, where Q is high:
    law 18.2 m, drawn 3.75 m. Too NARROW.
  * This measures the p50 over 20,000 randomly sampled wet cells, where the
    median wet cell is a sub-perennial headwater whose law width is FLOORED at
    1.5 m (water-wet-country-2026-08-05.md makes the same point about depth:
    "the median wet cell in both regions is a sub-perennial headwater at the
    floor, not a river"). Too WIDE, by 10-17x.

  So the drawn width is roughly 20 m EVERYWHERE: far too wide at headwaters, too
  narrow at trunks. THE DEFECT IS THAT DRAWN WIDTH BARELY TRACKS DISCHARGE AT
  ALL -- which is exactly what Spearman(drawn width, Q) = +0.457 against
  Spearman(law width, distance) = +0.728 already said, now with the two ends of
  the distribution measured separately.

  The cause is structural: `fill_to_local_surface` decides extent by asking how
  much ground lies below the local water surface. That is a property of terrain
  flatness, which is roughly scale-free, not of river size. Discharge enters only
  through the water surface's own height, which moves as Q^0.352 -- far too
  weakly to shape the width.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WRONG TURNS, recorded so they are not repeated
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. COMPARING THE BAKED SURFACE AGAINST BILINEAR 30 m SOURCE IS USELESS HERE.
     It gave an 8.6 m "notch". But bilinear over a 30 m grid sits ABOVE a
     sub-pixel apex on concave ground and BELOW it on convex ground, and
     channel/non-channel splits almost perfectly along concave/convex -- so the
     sign pattern that looks like incision is what interpolation bias produces
     anyway. The loose bound on that bias was 9.1 m p50 on channel cells, the
     same order as the signal. Only the same-lattice stage dump can separate them.

  2. dump_stage_heightfields.py's "S1" IS NOT THE SHIPPED SURFACE. It is the
     B3.relaxed stage sink; B4 meso, B4b refill and B5 reopen_basins all run
     after it and together move the channel 12.6 m. Taking it as the bake output
     put the ground ~7 m too low and inflated water depth from 0.90 m to 7.60 m,
     which is how it was caught -- 7.60 m contradicts the 0.6 m in
     water-wet-country-2026-08-05.md. The tool's docstring ("S1 after B3 thermal
     relaxation (= the bake)") was true when written and is now STALE.

  3. HAND-WALKING A TRUNK TO GET FLOW DIRECTION. See Part 2. The bake already
     has an instrument that takes the perpendicular from each cell's own
     receiver; use it. This is the third time in this file that reimplementing
     something the bake already does produced a wrong answer.
