The fork UNDER MOTION -- the criterion the plan actually sets for Wave D.

Every other fork measurement is cold fill. The plan's Wave D verdict is
"per-ring dispatch rate x per-ring p50 under motion", and residency under motion
is the thing the ring-gap symptom is about.

-VoxelPerfRun=60 -VoxelPerfFlight=surface -VoxelPerfSpeed=20, 64 m cascade,
2 legs per config, first two samples dropped so this is motion and not startup.

  config      meanPending   meanLoaded   staleResults   markTO   gpuLatencyTO
  CPU leg 1        373.3      13,669.7              0        0              0
  CPU leg 2        390.8      13,631.2              0        0              0
  fork leg 1        64.3      14,444.6              4        0              0
  fork leg 2        53.2      14,444.4              9        0              0

  pending backlog  -85%      (382 mean -> 59 mean)
  resident chunks  +6%       (13,650 mean -> 14,444 mean)

Within-config spreads are 17.5 (CPU) and 11.1 (fork) against a separation of
~320, so this is an order of magnitude outside the noise floor.

Final ring snapshot, one leg each:
  CPU   R0 2193 p0 | R1 2403 p89 | R2 2568 p149 | R3 2625 p110 | R4 2443 p57 | R5 1843 p132
  fork  R0 2134 p0 | R1 2459 p0  | R2 2752 p2   | R3 2706 p94  | R4 2473 p57 | R5 1895 p82

THE PREDICTED COST SHOWED UP AND IS NEGLIGIBLE. Wave D's design pass said stale
results would rise, because GenerationId is snapshotted at dispatch and compared
at drain, and multi-frame latency widens that window. It did: 4 and 9 against
the CPU's 0. Against ~59,500 chunks forked per leg that is 0.015%, and a stale
result costs a job rather than a slot -- LevelJobsInFlight[] is decremented
before the stale `continue`.

markTimeouts=0 and gpuLatencyTimeouts=0 on every leg, so nothing stranded.
submitToDeliver max 571 ms and 546 ms, well under kBlindJobMarkTimeoutSeconds.

WHAT IS STILL NOT MEASURED: frame time under motion. -VoxelPerfRun samples the
world delta, which the engine clamps at 400 ms, so it cannot be trusted here
(ground rule 10) -- that number needs a human reading stat unit, and it is on
the manual checklist. Nothing here says the fork makes FRAMES better; it says it
keeps residency ahead of the camera, which is a different and prior claim.
