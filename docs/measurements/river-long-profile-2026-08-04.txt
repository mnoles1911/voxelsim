THE 20.3 KM RIVER, WALKED FROM ITS HEAD TO ITS MOUTH
seed 20260719, tiles (-11,-4) (-11,-5) (-12,-5) (-11,-6), bake_ver 12
2026-08-04
================================================================================

The reach is the longest COMPOSED component of the bv12 corridor -- the river
plane unioned with the 255 lake sheets, which is what CompositeWaterSampler
draws -- identified in docs/measurements/river-seam-and-width-2026-08-04.txt
Part 4. This document walks it in order instead of summarising it.

REPRODUCING IT, and no re-bake was needed. The bv12 corridor was baked with
--diagnostic so there is no .vxtl to read, but the npz dumps are still on disk
at D:/tmp/seamwidth-npz. The basin registry is NOT in the npz; it was taken
from the shipped tiles, which is sound because `survey_basins` reads only
z_final, basin_depth, accumulation_m2 (MFD, untouched by bv12) and climate, so
it is bit-identical across the change. The check that it is: 255 basins, 246
overflowing and 9 terminal, which is exactly what the bv12 record reports.

    python tools/river_long_profile.py compose \
        --npz-dir D:/tmp/seamwidth-npz --basins D:/tmp/basins-corridor.json \
        --tiles="-11,-4 -11,-5 -12,-5 -11,-6" --out D:/tmp/composed-bv12.npz
    python tools/river_long_profile.py walk \
        --composed D:/tmp/composed-bv12.npz --interval 500 \
        --csv-out D:/tmp/longprofile-bv12.csv

The composition reproduces the record's totals exactly: 2,679,897 composed
cells, 317,665 river, 258 components, longest span 20,269 m, 5.0% lake. The
lake fill differs by 5 cells in 2,362,237 (0.0002%) because it runs on the
unquantised npz elevation rather than the control lattice.

2. HOW WIDE AT ITS WIDEST?

   Measured PERPENDICULAR to the local flow direction: the tangent is the chord
   over +-45 m of the extracted thalweg, the ray is cast on its normal in
   half-pixel steps, and the width is the contiguous wet run. Not a raster row
   -- a row measurement inflates a diagonal by up to sqrt(2).

   A perpendicular can point DOWNSTREAM at a tight bend and return tens of
   metres on a 4 m river, so every headline below is guarded twice: the local
   (+-9 m) and smoothed (+-45 m) tangents must agree to within 11 degrees, AND
   the perpendicular must agree with the bend-immune four-axis ribbon width
   (corridor_water_report.ribbon_width_m) to within two pixels. 7,135 of the
   13,897 path nodes pass. The guard matters: the unguarded maximum is 22.50 m
   at 15.2 km, where the straightness is 0.38 -- a bend, and a false number.

     widest RIVER cross-section          13.12 m  at 24.6 km (submarine)
     widest SUBAERIAL river section      11.25 m  at 12.1 km
     widest COMPOSED section             93.75 m  at 23.0 km -- 100% lake sheet
     four-axis ribbon over ALL reach cells   river max 10.61 m, p90 5.62 m
                                             composed max 100.76 m
     guarded perpendicular p50 7.50, p90 7.50, p99 9.38 m

   THE ANSWER TO GIVE: the river is 13.1 m across at its widest and typically
   7.5 m. Where it crosses the coastal lake at km 23 the drawn water is 93.8 m
   across, but that is a lake, not the river.

================================================================================
LENGTH: THE 20.3 KM IS A SPAN, NOT A LENGTH
================================================================================
This corrects an implication in the brief rather than a statement in it.

  20,269 m   the EUCLIDEAN span -- `max_pairwise_m`, the distance between the
             two furthest cells of the component via the convex hull. This is
             the number the seam-and-width record quotes and it is correct as
             what it is.
  30,577 m   the ALONG-CHANNEL length of the thalweg from head to mouth.
             Sinuosity 1.51.
  24,046 m   the along-channel length of the SUBAERIAL river, head to shore.

A river is longer than its own span. Every distance in this document is
along-channel unless it says otherwise.

HOW THE THALWEG WAS EXTRACTED, and the two ways the first attempt was wrong.

  * THE MOUTH IS THE LOWEST CELL, NOT THE FURTHEST. Taking the cell at maximum
    geodesic distance from the head gives a point 33,620 m away whose surface
    stands at 252.3 m -- 727 m ABOVE the outlet. It is up a tributary that is
    longer than the trunk's remaining length. The first run of this measurement
    did exactly that and produced a profile that climbed for its whole second
    half. Both endpoints are reported by the tool for this reason.

  * THE PATH IS COST-WEIGHTED TOWARD THE CENTRELINE. After the width law the
    ribbon is 1-5 px wide, and an unweighted shortest path hugs the inside of
    every bend, spending long stretches on widened bank cells whose own Q is a
    hillside trickle. Geometrically that is within two pixels and harmless; as
    a longitudinal profile it is not, because Q, bed and depth are then read
    off the flank. Weighting a non-centreline cell 6x puts 99.6% of the path on
    centreline or lake cells. Before the weighting the reported Q oscillated
    between 3e6 and 1e1 along a single kilometre.

================================================================================
THE SHAPE, IN ORDER
================================================================================

THE HEAD, km 0. A foothill, not a mountain: bed 389.0 m, water surface 389.4 m,
0.42 m deep, 3.75 m wide (one raster pixel each side of nothing -- the law
wants 3.04 m and the raster can hold 2 px). It already carries 1.86e6 m3/yr,
which is above the drawable cut of 1.53e6, so it is a drawn channel from its
first cell rather than a trickle that thickens.

THE STEEP UPPER 3 KM. This is the steepest kilometre on the whole river: 34.6
m/km over km 0-1, 30.0 over km 1-2. The river drops 86 m in the first 2.5 km
and does not widen at all -- 3.75 m the entire way. A tributary at 1.6 km adds
60% to the discharge (1.93e6 -> 3.09e6) and the law width steps 3.08 -> 3.72 m,
which is still under two pixels, so nothing changes on the raster. Depth steps
0.42 -> 0.50 m, and that is the only visible effect.

THE FIRST WIDENING, km 3.0-4.5. The drawn ribbon reaches 7.50 m for the first
time. The gradient has eased to 15-23 m/km.

THE CONTRACTION AT km 4.5-5.0, AND IT IS A TILE SEAM. The drawn width falls
7.50 -> 3.75 m and stays there for 1.5 km. It is not a bend and not a basin:
the discharge itself falls, 3.354e6 -> 2.865e6 m3/yr, a 14.6% loss, at the one
cell where the path crosses from tile (-11,-5) into tile (-11,-6). This is the
ONLY step in 13,896 where Q falls between two river cells; the other four Q
falls are the path entering a lake sheet, where Q is not defined.

  This is the residual mechanism the seam-and-width record named and did NOT
  fix. PR #209's single-receiver pyramid fixed the MAGNITUDE crossing a seam;
  it cannot fix the POSITION. The injected discharge enters at the lowest fine
  cell inside the 30 m crossing footprint, not at the fine tier's own thalweg,
  and where those disagree the trunk restarts slightly short. Here it costs
  14.6% of the discharge and 1.5 km of ribbon. The named fix -- place the
  entry cell by the fine surface's own accumulation maximum -- would remove it.

  IT IS THE ONLY SEAM THIS REACH CROSSES. The walk runs 5,219 m in (-11,-5)
  and 25,358 m in (-11,-6); it never enters (-11,-4) or (-12,-5).

THE LONG MIDDLE, km 5 to 19. Fourteen kilometres of a steady, slightly
flattening river. The gradient decays from 15 m/km to 8-12 m/km and bottoms at
5.1 m/km around km 15.5. Discharge climbs smoothly from 3.0e6 to 5.0e6 through
three modest tributaries (+11% at km 7.0, +8% at km 10.2, +16% at km 14.9). The
law width goes 3.67 -> 4.52 m -- less than half a pixel of growth over
fourteen kilometres -- so the drawn ribbon holds at 5.62-7.50 m and flickers
between them. Depth grows 0.50 -> 0.60 m, monotonically, and that is where the
growth of this stretch is actually visible.

THE KNICKPOINT AND THE BIG CONFLUENCE, km 19-20. The gradient jumps from 13 to
34.8 m/km at km 19.0 and stays above 28 through km 19.5: the river drops 32 m
in 1.4 km. At km 19.86, at the foot of it, the discharge DOUBLES -- 5.06e6 ->
1.013e7, the largest single tributary on the river -- and the law width steps
4.53 -> 5.97 m in one cell. Depth steps 0.60 -> 0.77 m across the same
kilometre. This is the transition from an upland stream to the trunk.

THE LOWER TRUNK, km 20 to 24. Full width the whole way: 7.50 m drawn against a
law width of 6.04-6.20 m, depth flat at 0.78 m, discharge 1.04e7 climbing to
1.11e7. The gradient is irregular -- 35 m/km at km 20.5, 11 at km 21, 55 at
km 22, 12 at km 22.5 -- a stepped descent, not a smooth one.

THE COASTAL LAKE, km 23.0. The only lake on the reach, and it is the reason the
composed network reaches the sea while the raster-only one stops 0.79 km short.
The river enters a registered basin at 22,999 m at a surface of 11.7 m and
leaves it about 130 m later. Inside it the drawn water is 93.75 m across
(four-axis 90.16 m -- the two measures agree, so this is a real sheet and not a
bend artefact) and up to 3.43 m deep, the deepest water on the whole reach. The
lake is 0.35% of the thalweg's nodes and 5.0% of the reach's cells. Everything
either side of it is river.

THE SHORELINE, km 24.0. The bed reaches 0 m at 23,987 m along, 78% of the way
through the walk. The gradient is 0.04 m/km through this bin -- the flattest
subaerial point on the river, a 500 m stretch essentially at sea level. Q
1.11e7, depth 0.79 m, width 5.6-7.5 m.

THE SUBMARINE CANYON, km 24 to 30.6. The drawn river does not stop at the
shore. It continues 6,589 m across the shelf and down the slope, descending
476 m -- more than half the river's total descent, in a fifth of its length.
The gradient reaches 216 m/km over km 27-28 and 160 m/km over km 28-29, an
order of magnitude steeper than anything above the shoreline. Two further
discharge steps (+11% at km 25.8, +4% at km 27.9) take it to 1.307e7. Depth
grows from 0.79 to 1.01 m, and the deepest CHANNEL water on the river is here,
at km 28.2. It is also the widest measured river cross-section, 13.12 m at
km 24.6.

THE MOUTH, km 29 to 30.6. It ends flat. The last 1.6 km falls 0.07 m/km --
essentially level -- at a bed of -476.0 m and a surface of -475.0 m, 1.01 m
deep and 7.50 m wide. The river terminates on a flat seafloor, not at a delta.


================================================================================
THE SANITY CHECKS THE BRIEF ASKED FOR
================================================================================

DOES THE SURFACE EVER RISE DOWNSTREAM? NO, not at any scale the profile has.

  The raw per-cell diff shows 230 rising steps of 13,896 (1.66%), largest
  1,528 mm, 56.0 m of rise against 920.4 m of fall. That number is NOT a
  defect and reporting it alone would be misleading. The bake's guarantee is
  that the surface descends along the D8 RECEIVER CHAIN. The path measured
  here is a least-cost route through the drawn mask, which is a different
  object: inside a 4 px ribbon it can step sideways from one thread onto a
  neighbouring one standing a few hundred mm higher.

  The test that discriminates is scale.

    resampled every  25 m (1,069 samples):  11 rises, largest 1,573 mm
    resampled every 100 m (  263 samples):   0 rises, largest  -1 mm
    resampled every 250 m (  105 samples):   0 rises, largest  -8 mm

  Above 100 m the profile is strictly monotone. The largest excursion above
  the running minimum anywhere on the walk is 2.60 m, at 26.6 km; excursions
  above 0.5 m cover 0.76% of the path in 37 stretches, none longer than a few
  metres. Nothing here needs fixing.

DOES WIDTH GROW MONOTONICALLY DOWNSTREAM? NO -- and the reason splits cleanly
into one real defect and one raster limit.

    Spearman(law width,      distance along)  +0.993
    Spearman(discharge,      distance along)  +0.993
    Spearman(drawn width,    distance along)  +0.542
    Spearman(drawn width, discharge)          +0.568

  THE HYDROLOGY IS MONOTONE. Q rises downstream on 13,891 of 13,896 steps, and
  the law width with it, from 3.04 m at the head to 6.61 m at the mouth. There
  is exactly one real exception, and it is the tile seam at km 5.2 described
  above: a 14.6% discharge loss that costs 1.5 km of ribbon width.

  THE DRAWN WIDTH IS NOT MONOTONE, and mostly that is the raster. The km-block
  median falls at 8 of 30 steps. Seven of the eight are one-pixel flickers
  (7.50 -> 6.56 or 6.56 -> 5.62 m) at places where the DISCHARGE ROSE -- the
  law width there is growing by hundredths of a metre against a 1.875 m pixel,
  so which side of a pixel boundary a given cell lands on is decided by the
  terrain clamp, not by Q. Only the km 4 -> 5 step (5.62 -> 3.75 m) has a
  falling Q behind it.

  So: the river the bake COMPUTES grows monotonically downstream except at one
  tile seam. The river it DRAWS grows in 1.875 m steps and flickers by one
  pixel either side of each step, which no rule can remove at this pitch.


================================================================================
WHAT THIS MEASUREMENT WOULD CHANGE IF IT WERE WRONG
================================================================================
Three things here contradict or refine what was carried in:

  * "the 20.3 km river" is a 20,269 m SPAN. The river is 30,577 m long along
    its own channel, 24,046 m of that above sea level.

  * "how deep at its deepest" has no single answer. 3.43 m is a lake, 3.28 m is
    a cut bank, 1.01 m is the channel. Only the third is the river's depth.
    None of the 91 corridor-wide >5 m cells fall on this reach.

  * The reach is not one continuous descent. 55% of its drop happens in the
    last 22% of its length, below sea level, on the continental slope.

The instrument is tools/river_long_profile.py. Its two known failure modes are
both guarded and both fired during this measurement before they were: taking
the furthest cell as the mouth walks up a tributary, and a perpendicular cast
at a bend can point downstream (the unguarded width maximum, 22.50 m, is a
bend at straightness 0.38, against a guarded 13.12 m).
