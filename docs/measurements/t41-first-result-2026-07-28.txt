THE LEAD TIME IS NOT A TUNING KNOB. 1 s already captures the entire effect and
8 s is no better; the differences between 1, 2, 4 and 8 are inside the pass-to-
pass spread of a single arm. This was not the expected shape -- the plan sweeps
lead precisely because it assumed more lead would buy more coverage -- and it
matters practically, because the cheapest setting is also the best one. Anything
that scales with lead (candidates enumerated, in-flight population, parked
residency) should be sized for ~1-2 s, not for 8.

Why it saturates, most likely: the speculative queue is bounded by
SpeculativeMaxInFlight*4 and the in-flight population by SpeculativeMaxInFlight
(32), so beyond a second of lead the binding constraint stops being how far
ahead the cone reaches and becomes how much work is allowed outstanding. That is
a hypothesis; the sweep does not test it.

Recommended default if the feature is switched on: voxel.Stream.VelocityLeadSec
2.0. Not 1.0 (the p90 is slightly worse and it sits at the edge of where the
effect appears), not 4.0+ (no benefit, strictly more work in flight).

wrong.)

3. THE MECHANISM, FROM THE FORK CENSUS (mid-flight window, both legs)
============================================================================

  arm       dispatched delivered pending queued gpuInFlight submitToDeliver
  lead 0.0         270       269     253    234          19       1863.9 ms
  lead 4.0         257       449     251    188          63       1110.5 ms

Three things, in the order they cause each other:

  a. gpuInFlight 19 -> 63. The GPU is doing work it was previously idle for.
     This is what the whole wave was for and it is the first leg on which it
     actually happened.

  b. The demand queue SHRANK, 234 -> 188. Speculation satisfies chunks before
     admission asks for them, so fewer demand jobs are ever queued.

  c. Demand latency HALVED, 1863.9 ms -> 1110.5 ms submit-to-deliver. This
     falls out of (b): a shorter queue is a shorter wait. It is a demand-path
     improvement produced by adding non-demand work, which is counterintuitive
     enough to be worth stating plainly.

Delivered per window 269 -> 449 (+67%) while dispatched stayed flat, because
"delivered" now includes speculative results.

4a. THE REAL COST IS GAME-THREAD TICK, NOT MEMORY (+45%)
============================================================================

Tick budget, mid-flight window, both legs:

  arm       tickMs/5s  % of wall  recompute  dispatch  apply  unload  perTick
  lead 0.0      485.0      9.70%      133.0      10.3   56.6   271.3    7.133
  lead 4.0      703.2     14.06%      131.6     274.6   51.0   242.6   11.162

The dispatch phase went from 10.3 ms to 274.6 ms per 5 s window -- a 27x
increase, and essentially all of T4-1's cost. Voxel tick work is 45% more of
wall clock (9.70% -> 14.06%) and the per-tick figure rose 7.13 ms -> 11.16 ms.

WHERE IT IS *NOT*: EnumerateSpeculativeCandidates() is called BEFORE T0, so the
annulus walk is outside the dispatch timer entirely. It lands in tickMs without
a bucket of its own. The first guess written here was that enumeration explained
the +264 ms; the timer boundaries rule that out.

So the whole increase is inside DispatchJobs, where DispatchSpeculativeJobs()
runs. The arithmetic there does not obviously add up either:

  lead 0.0:  270 jobs dispatched,  10.3 ms  =  0.038 ms/job
  lead 4.0:  257 demand + ~185 spec = ~442 jobs, 274.6 ms = 0.62 ms/job

A speculative submit calls the SAME SubmitGpuMeshJob as a demand submit, so a
16x per-job cost difference is not explicable by the submit itself. Something in
the speculative path costs per TICK rather than per job -- candidates are the
linear scan of GpuJobsPending (~252 entries) counting SpecOutstanding on every
call, and whatever EnsureGpuMeshJobs does per call -- but this is a hypothesis
with no measurement behind it and it should not be repeated as a finding.

The timer cannot answer it: dispatch= is one bucket covering demand dispatch,
speculative dispatch and the second post-drain pass. Splitting it is task #21
and is the prerequisite for any optimisation here, not an optional refinement.

NOT AN ABORT, BUT NOT FREE EITHER. renderMs is unchanged (flight median 30.1 ->
29.2, max 33.8 -> 35.0), so the doubled per-tick promotion budget did not cost
render-thread pass setup, which was the risk of giving speculation its own
allowance on top of MeshBatchCap. chunks/s is unchanged. The bill is paid on the
game thread, and it is the obvious next optimisation: split the dispatch timer,
then either cache the annulus walk across ticks or run it on a crossing rather
than every tick, the way RecomputeDesiredSet already does.

4b. MEMORY COST: ESSENTIALLY NONE, AND CAPACITY STEP 2 IS NOT JUSTIFIED
============================================================================

poolPct 86.2 -> 86.8. Peak SPECULATIVE parked population was 172 against a
SpeculativeMaxParked of 4000 -- the cap is nowhere near binding, because parked
speculative chunks are adopted almost as fast as they arrive (~86 parked and
~88 adopted per 5s window, in equilibrium). evictedUnused was 0 for the entire
leg: not one speculative chunk was meshed, parked, and then thrown away.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE OWNER'S 104M DECISION. The plan's capacity step 2 (64M ->
104M quads, table 81,920 -> 131,072) was sized on the assumption that
speculation would hold a large parked population. It holds ~172 chunks. NOTHING
MEASURED SO FAR JUSTIFIES THE SECOND CAPACITY STEP. It should not be taken on
this evidence.

What IS worth watching: poolPct is 86.2% with speculation OFF. That is the S2-3
abort threshold (~90%) getting close on its own, and it is a pre-existing
condition of the shipped parking cap, not something T4-1 caused. Flagged, not
fixed here.

============================================================================
5. THE HONEST DENOMINATOR: 46%, NOT 96%
============================================================================

The in-log "hit %" is adopted/parked and read 96%. Against DISPATCHED it is
4,678 / 10,093 = 46%. Both are true and they answer different questions:

  96% -- of the speculative chunks we kept, almost all got used. The velocity
         cone is aimed correctly. This is the number Wave S4 called specHitRate
         and set a >50% bar for.
  46% -- of the GPU work speculation SPENT, this much reached the screen. This
         is the cost number, and it is the one to improve.

Roughly half of speculative dispatches never became a park. Three silent early
returns in ParkSpeculativeResult could each account for it (all-air column,
demand overtook the job in flight, pool refused). They were uncounted, so the
split was unknown; drop-reason counters (dropOvertaken / dropEmpty /
dropPoolFull) are now in the 5s speculation line and the sweep will attribute
it.

ATTRIBUTED 2026-07-28 FROM THE SWEEP, AND THE HYPOTHESIS WAS WRONG.

Mid-flight windows, sweep legs t41s-lead1-1 and t41s-lead2-1:

  dropOvertaken=0   dropEmpty=100   dropPoolFull=0    (lead 1.0)
  dropOvertaken=0   dropEmpty=90    dropPoolFull=0    (lead 2.0)

Every non-parking dispatch is an ALL-AIR COLUMN. Not one was overtaken by
demand, and the pool never refused one.

The suspected cause written here first was "demand overtook it" -- nothing
consults SpeculativeInFlight in AddCandidate, so admission reaching a key with a
speculative job in flight would queue a SECOND job and mesh the same chunk
twice. That is a real gap in the code and it is measurably NOT HAPPENING: by the
time demand arrives, speculation has already parked the chunk and the adopt path
takes it. The fix that was designed for it -- routing speculative results to
ApplyMeshResult on arrival -- would have optimised an empty bucket.

WHAT THE REAL FINDING IS: roughly half of all speculative GPU work meshes air.
The speculative enumerator is not applying the predicted-empty and buried-skip
filters that admission applies (the fork's own enable log advertises "not
already predicted empty" as a condition of the demand path). Fixing that is
worth ~50% of speculation's GPU cost AND a proportional share of its
game-thread dispatch cost, since each one is a submit that need not happen.

Note this is not waste in the damaging sense -- an air column parks nothing,
holds no pool range, and evicts nothing (evictedUnused=0 on every leg). It is
spent capacity, not corruption. But it is the single largest lever on T4-1's
cost and it is cheap to take.

6. CORRECTION TO AN EARLIER READING IN THIS SESSION
============================================================================

The first read of this leg reported "inFlight 155 -- the GPU is now running 155
concurrent jobs." That was wrong. It read the speculation census's inFlight
field, which is SpeculativeInFlight.Num() -- the enumerated-but-unresolved set,
including ~125 keys still queued and never dispatched. True speculative
outstanding is capped at 32 and the GPU's real concurrency is the fork census's
gpuInFlight, which is 63.

The field is now labelled tracked= and a separate gpuInFlight= prints the real
count. Same class of mistake as the phase-blind window sampling in
tools/voxel-leg-summary.ps1's footer: a plausible number read off the wrong
counter, with no error to signal it.

============================================================================
7. IMPLEMENTATION NOTE -- THE PART THAT WAS WRONG TWICE
============================================================================

Speculation only works because the manager gained a SECOND queue with its OWN
per-tick promotion allowance (voxel.GPU.MeshSpeculativeBatchCap, default 4), on
top of demand's voxel.GPU.MeshBatchCap.

The two designs that came first both made speculation a dead path, and both
looked like prudence:

  - dispatch floor "GpuJobsPending.Num() >= GpuCap - SpecCap" -- pipeline
    OCCUPANCY, ~252 of a 256 cap in steady state, true on every tick.
  - promote low-priority "only when the demand queue is empty" -- the demand
    queue carries ~236 jobs for the whole flight, so also true on every tick.

Both measured how BUSY the pipeline was and called it full. MeshBatchCap is a
per-tick burst limiter protecting render-thread pass setup; the actual capacity
bound is MaxInFlight, and the GPU sat at 19 of 256. Demand is protected
STRUCTURALLY -- speculative jobs live in their own queue and promote only after
demand has taken its full allowance -- so no second, weaker copy of that
guarantee is needed at the submission site.

8. THE GAME-THREAD COST WAS 97% ONE THING, AND IT IS GONE
============================================================================

Section 4a recorded a +45% game-thread tick cost and refused to name a cause
without a measurement. The split timer named it in one leg.

  window                dispatch   specEnum  specDispatch  specPark
  before (t41-cost2)      343.9        0.8        0.6         334.7
  after  (t41-batchfix)    10.2        0.8        0.7           0.9

specPark was 97% of the dispatch bucket. Speculation's OWN code -- enumerating
the cone, submitting the jobs -- costs 1.4 ms per 5 s window combined, which is
nothing. The cost was ParkSpeculativeResult.

WHY. GpuMeshJobs->Tick() calls OnGpuMeshJobComplete, and a speculative
completion runs ParkSpeculativeResult INLINE: AddChunkFromGpu plus ParkChunk,
both pool mutations. That call sits at T0..T1, and S1-1's FScopedBatch did not
open until after T1. Its comment said the scope deliberately excluded the first
DispatchJobs because that "submits jobs and touches no pool state" -- true when
written, and made false by T4-1.

So every speculative park published the whole chunk table on its own. That is
precisely the per-mutation publication S1-1 removed for demand, and speculation
was paying it ~185 times per window because it entered the pool through a door
the scope did not cover. Not a new cost: an old one, re-incurred.

FIX: open the batch before GpuMeshJobs->Tick(). The hazard argument is
unchanged -- AddChunkFromGpu still does the UnmarkQuadsDirty interval subtract
that guards the free-then-reallocate-within-one-frame race, which is what makes
widening this scope safe.

RESULT (mid-flight windows; the fix is replicated on two legs):

                       tickMs   % of wall   perTick   dispatch
  speculation OFF       485.0      9.70%      7.133      10.4
  ON, before fix        742.0     14.84%     10.600     343.9
  ON, after fix         469.5      9.39%      7.335      10.0
  ON, after fix (leg 2) 459.4      9.19%      7.178      10.4

T4-1's game-thread cost is now INSIDE THE NOISE of having it switched off --
9.19-9.39% of wall against 9.70% with the feature off. specPark reads 0.9 ms on
both legs.

Nothing else moved. Both batch-fix legs against the lead-2.0 sweep leg:

  leg              chunks/s  flight holes med (p90)  poolPct  park hit  spec
  t41s-lead2-1       1070.9        56 (94)             86.8      89%    46%
  t41-batchfix       1070.3        57 (92)             86.8      89%    46%
  t41-batchfix-2     1070.4        58 (102)            86.7      89%    46%

allocFail=0 and holes(final)=0 on all three.

PARKING WAS NOT ZEROED. Worth stating explicitly, because task #17 records that
wrapping RecomputeDesiredSet in an FScopedBatch took parking from 28,117 to
exactly zero and was reverted undiagnosed. Widening the scope over the manager
tick does NOT reproduce that: parked=44,727 adopted=39,727 hit=89%, identical to
the unwidened leg. Whatever #17 is, it is specific to RecomputeDesiredSet.

byte-identical PASS, not this.
