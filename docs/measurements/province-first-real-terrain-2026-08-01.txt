Landform provinces on real terrain -- first measurement
========================================================
2026-08-01

WHY THIS DID NOT EXIST BEFORE
------------------------------
Wave 4 implemented province Tier 1 but could not validate it: no coarse world
existed on the dev box, so separation was measured on a random-fBm domain
whose within-scene sigma was flagged as probably a PESSIMISTIC denominator.
Separately, two of four provinces were unreachable on the shipped world --
its climate never dropped below 3,900 mm/yr of precipitation anywhere.

Both blockers are gone: the conditioning rebuild produced a world with real
climate contrast, and 121 tiles of it are on disk.

A BUG FOUND BEFORE ANY RESULT WAS TRUSTED
------------------------------------------
bake_real_tile.py -- the tool the plan names as the verification reference --
never passed climate. bake_tile's climate_fetch defaults to None and None does
NOT raise: the province partition silently falls back to relief alone.

Tile (-3,-3), which is 98.9% arid by precipitation, was reported as 100%
FLUVIAL. Nothing in the output said climate was absent; province_climate 0.0000
sat among 24 other stat lines. A validation tool that cannot exercise the
feature it validates is worse than none, because its clean output reads as
confirmation. Fixed in 36ed0fb.

THE MEASUREMENT
---------------
Two tiles at comparable relief and opposite climate, seed 0x135276f, full
8192^2 interior + 960 m apron, production geometry, bake_ver 7.

  tile        coarse relief   arid%(precip<400)   precip median
  (-3,-3)          3058 m            98.9%             94 mm
  (-2,-4)          2801 m             0.0%           1741 mm

                        (-2,-4) WET     (-3,-3) ARID
  province_climate         1.0000          1.0000
  province_fluvial_frac    0.9418          0.0880
  province_glacial_frac    0.0578          0.7962
  province_arid_frac       0.0000          0.1157
  province_lowland_frac    0.0004          0.0000
  relief_m               4,239.8442      4,560.5742
  interior_dead_ends       0.0000          0.0000
  padded_border_basin_frac 0.0000          0.0000

WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES
---------------------
1. The partition DISCRIMINATES, decisively, on the wet/dry axis. A wet massif
   is 94% fluvial; a dry one of the same relief is 9%. On the old world every
   tile was fluvial 0.918 / arid 0.0000 regardless of appearance.
2. The per-cell fields REACH THE EROSION KERNELS rather than being inert:
   relief_m on (-3,-3) moved 4560.1348 -> 4560.5742 between the no-climate and
   with-climate bakes of identical terrain.
3. Drainage invariants hold IN BOTH PROVINCES -- 0 interior sinks, 0.0%
   stranded. That is Wave 4's stated acceptance criterion, now met on real
   terrain instead of a synthetic domain.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
----------------------------
That the MULTIPLIER TABLE is right. Wave 4 measured the best separation at
0.52x of three within-scene sigmas and deliberately declined to tune the table
upward to clear the bar. Two tiles are a data point, not a verdict; confirming
the constants needs geomorph_validate.py --sweep bake across many tiles, and
the next anchor is channel_head_area_m2, which spans 866-8,660 m2 (10x) across
five real classes against the 2.5-4x the table currently uses.

THE OPEN QUESTION, NARROWED
---------------------------
The wet/dry axis works. What is untested is the ARID-vs-GLACIAL split WITHIN
dry terrain: a 98.9%-arid tile reads 79.6% GLACIAL and only 11.6% ARID, so the
discriminant weights cold over dry. That is plausible for a 4.5 km-relief cold
desert (Karakoram, Tibetan margin) and may be entirely correct -- but nobody
has checked it.

The check is cheap and specific. tools/earth_reference.py's corpus maps almost
1:1 onto the provinces: Teton/Alps -> GLACIAL (Teton has 1 m 3DEP bare-earth),
Smokies/Nepal -> FLUVIAL, Badlands -> ARID, Iowa/Llano/Illinois -> LOWLAND.
Classify those real windows through province.py and see whether the labels come
back right. If the Badlands classify GLACIAL, the DISCRIMINANT is wrong, not
the multipliers -- and that is a different and prior problem.
