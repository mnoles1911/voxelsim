--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1a IS CLOSED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The premise of the whole T4-1 re-sequencing was that the CONSUMER is the wall.
S0 measured the apply loop exiting on its 6 ms wall clock in every window and
NEVER on an empty queue. After S1-1:

    exit queueEmpty=88  wallClock=0  countCap=0
    stale=0 (0.0%)

The drain now empties the queue, and the stale-result fraction that was 42% is
zero. The consumer is no longer the wall. That is the S1 goal, discharged.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT ACTUALLY FIXED THE FRAGMENTATION -- NOT WHAT IT LOOKED LIKE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batching alone refused 77,290 allocations (96.9 M quads) while the free list
shattered: 16,903 free runs, largest run 68,326, against 8,407 / 4,904,120 on
the unbatched control at the same total free. That reads exactly like first-fit
pathology, and the obvious response is the idle defrag (T3-6).

IT WAS NOT FRAGMENTATION-AS-A-CAUSE. It was residency ballooning:

    unload=212.8 ms of a 219.6 ms tick        pendingUnload = 20,926
    peak live chunks = 80,716                 tracked = 92,875

voxel.Stream.MaxUnloadsPerFrame defaults to 24, tuned when the pipeline applied
~260 chunks/s. At 631/s with ~28 ticks/s that is ~684 unloads/s against ~630
loads/s -- marginally under water, so the unload queue grew without bound and
residency with it. A pool held chronically over-full fragments; first-fit was
the messenger.

Raising the budget to 256: peak residency 80,716 -> 50,930, peak pendingUnload
75,925 -> 19,873, allocFail 26,763 -> 0, holes 257 -> 0.

  NO IDLE DEFRAG IS NEEDED. T3-6 stays deferred. One leg's worth of diagnosis
  separated "build a compacting allocator" from "raise a cvar that was sized for
  a slower pipeline".

  AND THE CAPACITY RAISE IS PARTLY REDUNDANT. It is NOT free to keep: 64M costs
  512 MB VRAM in quads, the same again in chunk ids, and 12 B/quad of CPU shadow
  (docs/speculative-generation-plan.md §2.6). Peak residency with the unload fix
  is ~50,900 chunks ~= 46M quads, so 44M would still be marginal and the 49,152
  table floor is genuinely too low -- but 64M may be more than required. OPEN:
  run a control at 48M before treating that memory as necessary, because T4-1's
  speculative budget is sized off whatever is left over.

MaxAppliesPerFrame: UNDECIDED. THE LEG THAT "DECIDED" IT WAS CONTENDED.

countCap binds at the winning config (countCap=79 in busy windows), which reads
like a ceiling worth raising. A leg at 192 came back 749.5 chunks/s / 179 holes
against 797.0 / 0, and that was written up here as REJECTED with a mechanical
explanation attached (the drain breaks on a 6 ms wall clock, so a higher count
cap only lets each frame do more work against the same budget and lengthens
frames).

THAT LEG WAS CONTAMINATED AND IS VOID. Its last 86 seconds -- the flight tail,
where the numbers are made -- ran concurrently with another editor, because a
confirmation sweep was launched before the apply sweep's loop had finished:

    s24-apply192   00:19:29 -> 00:24:13
    s1close-1      00:22:47 -> 00:27:20      <-- 86 s of overlap
    s24-apply512   00:24:19 -> 00:27:20      <-- never completed

A second process on the GPU produces "this setting made it worse" for free. The
explanation offered above is plausible and may well be right; it is not
supported by this leg, and a plausible mechanism attached to a contended number
is exactly how a wrong result survives review.

  STATUS: MaxAppliesPerFrame stays at 64 because nothing has validly moved it,
  NOT because 192 was shown to be worse. Re-run the sweep through
  tools/voxel-run-flight-leg.ps1, which now refuses to start while another

  HOW IT WAS CAUGHT: the owner noticed two game windows on screen. That is not

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE BOTTLENECK MOVED, AND IT MOVED OUT OF WAVE S1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

At the winning config:

    tickMs=806.5 over 79 ticks (10.2 ms/tick)
      recompute = 530.5 ms   (66%)
      unload    = 223.4 ms   (28%, and most of that is the batch flush -- the
                              scope closes before T4, so the tick's single
                              publication is attributed to the unload bucket)
      apply     =  42.5 ms   (5%)

    recordsAdded = 44,604 per 2 s window     tracked = 92,875
    dispatched   =  4,422                    stale   = 0%

RecomputeDesiredSet is now two thirds of the streaming tick, and the cause is a
feedback loop that S1 CREATED:

    RecomputeDesiredSet relaxes every LevelAdmissionCutoffDistSq to DBL_MAX when
    PendingJobNum() * 4 < Cap * 3 -- i.e. when the pending queue is short.

The queue is now always short, because the consumer stopped being the wall. So
the admission cutoff is permanently disabled, admission floods (22,300
records/s against ~800 chunks/s actually loading), tracked balloons to 92,875,
and the O(tracked) exit scan gets expensive.

**Making the consumer fast made the admission cap stop working.** That is
lesson 5 -- "the bottleneck migrates; re-baseline after every fix" -- for the
sixth time in this programme, and it is the seventh.

This is Wave S2 / T4-2 territory (GPU-resident residency, and the entry-scan
quantisation T2-1/T2-2 work around). It is NOT more S1.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AGAINST THE S1 CLOSE CRITERION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The criterion was: pooled arm at or past 1,082 chunks/s with holes(final)=0.

    holes(final) = 0        MET
    chunks/s     = 797      NOT MET (74% of target)

Read honestly: the holes criterion is the one that describes what a player sees,
and it is met for the first time. The throughput criterion is not, and the
reason is no longer anything S1 was aimed at -- the apply path is no longer the
constraint by any measure taken here.

The 1,082 figure is also the COMPONENT arm's number, and that arm was never
converging holes at this cascade (it posted 1,082 with holes=0 at the OLD
scale). A pooled arm at 797 with holes=0 and a component arm at 1,082 with
holes=0 are not the same world; the head-to-head at the adopted cascade has to
be re-run before the comparison means anything.

  DECISION OWED (the plan's hard stop): whether to keep pushing throughput --
  which now means the admission/recompute path, i.e. T4-2 -- or to accept
  holes=0 at 797 and move to Wave S2 (parking) on the way to T4-1. The plan
  says stop and re-decide here, and the ground for that decision has changed:
  the item that would raise throughput further is no longer a Wave S1 item.

S2-3 PARKING -- MEASURED AND NOT JUSTIFIED AS INTEGRATED (2026-07-27)
================================================================================

Keep an evicted chunk's pool range, hidden, so re-admission is a chunk-table
write instead of a re-mesh round trip. Also T4-1's intended substrate.

  profile   config          chunks/s   holes   allocFail   parked/adopted (hit)
  line      off             1040.6         0           0   --
  line      off             1040.5         0           0   --
  line      park 4,000       850.3         0           0   28,185 / 21,428 (76%)
  line      park 12,000      855.3         2           0   28,117 / 23,709 (84%)
  line      park 12,000      850.1         0           0   27,577 / 22,971 (83%)
  line      park 12,000      847.6         0           0   27,377 / 23,118 (84%)
  circle    off              893.7         0           0   --
  circle    park 12,000      596.7         0           0   46,866 / 41,726 (89%)

THE CACHE WORKS AND STILL LOSES. On the circle profile -- continuous revisiting,
parking's BEST case -- 89% of parked geometry is adopted and throughput is 33%
LOWER. On line it is 83-84% hit and 18% lower. Higher hit rate, bigger loss.

WHY: each adopt calls UnparkChunk, which calls PushUpdatesToProxy, and the adopt
path lives in RecomputeDesiredSet -- which S1-1's FScopedBatch does NOT cover
(that scope wraps DrainResults/DrainGameThreadMesh/DrainUnloads). So parking
trades one meshing round trip for one FULL POOL PUBLICATION, and after S1-1 a
publication is the expensive thing. 41,726 adopts is 41,726 publications.

This is S1-1's own finding pointing back at itself: the per-item publication cost
is what made the original plateau, and parking reintroduced it on a path S1-1's
scope does not reach.

  FIRST ATTEMPT AT THE FIX IS REVERTED AND UNDIAGNOSED. Wrapping
  RecomputeDesiredSet in an FScopedBatch took parking from 28,117 parks / 84% hit
  to ZERO parks and chunks/s 1,040 -> 540, with the world still churning at the
  end of the linger. Confirmed not to be variance: three legs after the revert
  reproduce 847.6-855.3 at 83-84%. The scope is the cause; the mechanism is not
  understood. See the note at the RecomputeDesiredSet call site.

VERDICT: voxel.Stream.PoolParkMax stays 0. The mechanism is sound (89% hit, zero
allocFail, zero holes, frame time flat at ~22.5 ms p50 throughout) and the
integration is not.

  ALSO AN EARLIER, LARGER FAILURE, kept because it is the more useful finding:
  parking EVERY eviction scored 8% hit and cost allocFail 0 -> 45,633, holes
  0 -> 1,804. On a traverse everything evicted by the outer edge is behind the
  camera and never returns. Restricting to RetainDir_Finer -- inner-boundary
  oscillation, the only eviction cause that comes back -- took the hit rate
  8% -> 84% with no other change. WHERE you cache matters far more than HOW MUCH.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS CHANGES FOR T4-1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

T4-1's adopt path IS THIS PATH: AddCandidate finds parked geometry and unparks
it. Speculation would inherit the same per-adopt publication, at higher volume,
and would lose for the same reason -- while looking like a hit-rate success.

So, promoted onto T4-1's critical path:

  1. BATCH THE ADOPT PATH. Not an optimisation any more. Requires first
     diagnosing why FScopedBatch around RecomputeDesiredSet zeroed parking.
  2. specHitRate IS NOT SUFFICIENT as T4-1's deciding metric. Parking scored 89%
     and lost. The metric has to be throughput and holes AT a given hit rate,
     because a cache can be almost perfectly effective and still cost more than
     it saves.

That second point is the one worth carrying: the original plan named specHitRate
as the number that decides T4-1, and this measurement shows a 89%-effective
cache losing 33% throughput. Hit rate measures whether the PREDICTION was right.
It says nothing about whether the mechanism is cheap enough to be worth using.
