WHY IT EXISTS: three independent estimates of the 10 cm / 4 km resident brick
set disagreed by 4x (150 / 300 / 657 MB). The disagreement was entirely in the
mixed-brick fraction and the palette size. Both are now measured.

1. MIXED-BRICK FRACTION -- AND THE 4x SPREAD WAS A DEFINITION PROBLEM
   Two legitimate readings, and a census that reports one without naming which
   RECREATES the spread:

   reading                                slots      all-air  all-solid  MIXED
   volume (12.8 m of ground below shell)  3,025,821    2.6%     67.1%    30.3%
   surface shell (what the mesher meshes)   806,884    9.2%      0.7%    90.1%

   The 20-30% assumption is a VOLUME assumption and lands at the top of its
   band. The meshed set is 90% mixed but 3.8x fewer bricks. The probe prints
   which reading it used; --depth-m is an explicit input, not a hidden default.

THREE FINDINGS THE PLAN DID NOT HAVE
 a) tryCollapse is worth ~250 MiB of the 404 -- 62% of the total. 67.1% of
    slots collapse to an 8 B descriptor. "Load-bearing" is now a number.
 b) THE FLAT INDEX IS ALREADY 48% OF THE 10 cm TOTAL (193 of 404 MiB), and at
    2.5 cm it is 12,080 MiB -- 30x an entire 10 cm world. The plan defers the
    sparse-hash/octree decision to 2.5 cm. It is worth taking at 10 cm.
 c) 5 cm IS A WORLDGEN CHANGE, NOT A LATTICE CHANGE. Amplifier::column is
    addressed in integer LEVEL-0 voxels -- there is no sub-100 mm horizontal
    query -- so a sub-lattice is exact in z and SATURATED in xy. Halving the
    voxel size does not by itself buy horizontal detail. This is the terrain-side
    twin of the asset-side note that detail assets carry their own 5 cm pitch.

================================================================
CORRECTION 2026-08-19 (later): THE 404 MiB USED A VARIABLE PALETTE
================================================================
The census's `palette 3.3 MiB` line implies ~3.77 B per mixed brick, i.e. it was
computed with a VARIABLE-SIZE palette (ceil(paletteCount/4) dwords).

The format contract specifies a FIXED 16 B local palette, and both Phase 1
implementations adopted it after the contradiction was found -- four bits index
it exactly, it is one 16 B load at a known offset, and it keeps arithmetic out
of the marcher's inner loop.

  fixed palette cost   916,901 mixed bricks x 16 B = 14.0 MiB
  census counted                                      3.3 MiB
  difference                                        +10.7 MiB

  => THE 10 cm / 4 km CASCADE IS ~415 MiB, NOT 404 MiB (+2.6%).

  Inside P2's +/-20% gate with room, and it does not move any conclusion: bricks
  are still ~1.7x denser than the 702 MiB of quads off the same walk. But the
  404 figure is quoted in the plan and in a memory, so it is corrected here
  rather than left to be re-derived.

  The alternative -- adopt the variable palette and keep 404 -- was considered
  and rejected: it saves 10.7 MiB of a ~415 MiB budget and costs a shift and a
  mask on every brick entry in the hottest loop in the renderer.

================================================================
P2 DERIVATION (2026-08-19, later still): WHAT THE IMPLEMENTED POOL
ACTUALLY ALLOCATES -- AND WHY IT IS ~223 MiB, NOT ~415
================================================================
FVoxelBrickPool (P1-C / P2) took finding (b) above: it does NOT allocate a
dense flat brick index. Chunks are addressed through a 32 B FVoxelMarchChunk
record per RESIDENT chunk plus BrickBase, not through one uint32 per slot of a
dense 3D array over each ring's footprint and z envelope.

So the census's five lines do not all apply to it, and the ~415 MiB headline is
the wrong number to hold this implementation to:

  cells (adaptive)                       128.5 MiB   allocated (material arena)
  palette, fixed 16 B                     14.0 MiB   allocated (material arena)
  occupancy                               56.0 MiB   allocated (occupancy arena)
  descriptors                             23.1 MiB   allocated (descriptor arena)
  chunk records  3,025,821 / 64 x 32 B     1.4 MiB   allocated (chunk table)
  ---------------------------------------------------
  PREDICTED RESIDENT SET                 223.0 MiB

  flat index                             193.3 MiB   NOT ALLOCATED
  ---------------------------------------------------
  census headline                        415.0 MiB (with the fixed-palette
                                                    correction above)

Two consequences, both of which have to be carried to whoever reads a settle
measurement:

 1. P2's gate is "real VRAM at settle within +/-20% of the census". Measured
    against 415 MiB this design is 46% LOW and would fail the gate for being
    CHEAPER than a structure it does not build. The figure to hold it to is
    223.0 MiB +/- 20%, i.e. 178-268 MiB of USED bytes (FVoxelBrickPool::
    GetResidentBytes, printed by voxel.Brick.Stats).

 2. A VRAM tool sees the CAPACITY, not the used prefix: the arenas are committed
    once at Init and never grow. At the shipped cvar defaults that is 314 MiB
    (desc 32, occ 80, mat 200, table 2) -- ~1.4x the predicted resident set,
    which is the churn headroom. voxel.Brick.PoolOccMiB / PoolMatMiB /
    PoolChunks move it.

The sparse-vs-dense choice is not free and the trade is stated rather than
implied: a dense index answers "which brick is at this coordinate" with one
load, and the chunk table answers it with a lookup the marcher has to carry.
At 10 cm the index costs 193.3 MiB against 1.4 MiB of records -- 138x -- and at
2.5 cm it is 12,080 MiB against ~90 MiB. Finding (b) said the decision was worth
taking at 10 cm; P2 took it.
