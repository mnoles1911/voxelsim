THE CONTOUR-TERRACING ARTIFACT: MEASURED, AND WHY NOTHING HAS FIXED IT
======================================================================
2026-07-30. Written because the artifact has survived ten attempts and the useful
output of that is a sharp statement of the constraint, not another guess.

THE ARTIFACT, QUANTIFIED WITHOUT ANYONE'S EYE. terrainprobe now measures the
box-counting dimension of the voxel level field's terrace boundaries, with controls
computed every run:

  tilted plane (control)        D = 1.000
  our carrier                   D = 1.006
  our amplified surface (v17)   D = 1.014
  REAL topographic contours     D = 1.15 - 1.25   (published, external)
  white noise (control)         D = 1.872

So the shipped surface's contours are ~1% of the way from a machined plane toward
natural. That is the owner's complaint, as a number.

NOTE THE TOP OF THE SCALE. D is bounded above by NOISE, not by realism. A term that
degenerates into per-voxel static raises D toward 1.87. D alone therefore cannot
distinguish "crooked like real ground" from "noisy", and must be read alongside the
drainage ladder and the terrace-run statistics.

WHAT IT IS NOT. Established by ablation with the shader mirrored, so the GPU
actually saw each change: the 1.6 m octave, the rill term, the bedding term, the
entire detail ladder at 1/16, and the fine tier itself. All left it intact. Removing
detail makes it WORSE. A sun-angle control (08:00 / 12:00 / 16:00) confirms geometry
rather than shading. A periodicity scan finds no periodic component at 1-2 m, which
is correct and was the wrong question: terracing is a THRESHOLD phenomenon in
floor(h / 100 mm), not a spectral one in h.

THE CONSTRAINT, which is the useful part. Crooking a contour needs of order one
voxel of vertical deviation at sub-metre wavelength. At 0.4 m that IS a gradient of
0.25. The v14/v15 drainage cap permits max(0.05, 0.5 x carrierGradient). So:
  * below ~50% grade the two requirements are arithmetically incompatible;
  * and the CARRIER alone measures D = 1.006, so it sets a floor no client term can
    lift -- the smoothness is upstream of the client entirely.

Two experiments confirm the trade is real and unprofitable:
  * doubling the cap's k to 1.0 cost 53.8% of the tile's drainage and moved D by
    -0.001. Amplitude is not the lever.
  * reshaping the micro band to a Hurst-0.8 ladder (v17: 400:90 -> 165, 200:25 ->
    95) moved local H at 0.4 m from 1.352 to 0.979, INTO the plan's [0.6, 1.0]
    target band, at zero drainage cost -- and moved D only 1.010 -> 1.014. Spectral
    shape is not the lever either.

THE ONE MECHANISM THAT SHOULD WORK, AND WHY THE SHIPPABLE FORM DOES NOT. A
displacement varying ONLY across the slope has a gradient perpendicular to the fall
line, so it cannot change the downhill component and cannot close a basin at any
amplitude -- exempt from the cap by construction. A prototype using the EXACT
gradient direction measured, on the fine tier at a 35% site:

  150 mm  D = 1.021   0.0% stranded
  400 mm  D = 1.060   0.0%
  800 mm  D = 1.155   0.0%      <- in the real band
 1600 mm  D = 1.307   42.9%     <- breaks

Promoted to a shippable form -- the rill's 16-direction table with two-frame
blending, which is what makes the rill free of the lever-arm defect documented at
length in detail_rill.h -- it collapses:

  amp 0     D = 1.014
  amp 3200  D = 1.020

The blend is the reason. At a 1.2 m across-slope wavelength the two fields either
side of the measured aspect are effectively INDEPENDENT, so averaging them destroys
the coherence that crooks a contour. The rill survives this because its features are
12.8 m along-slope, where the blend mostly affects orientation rather than phase.

AND THE PROTOTYPE'S RESULT IS PROBABLY A FALSE POSITIVE, which is why nothing was
shipped. detail_rill.h records that the exact-direction construction has a noise
phase whose derivative grows with distance from the world origin, degenerating into
per-voxel static -- measured there at 54 km. The sites used here sit at 69 km
(x = -69120 m). So the prototype's D = 1.155 is consistent with the metric climbing
toward the 1.872 NOISE control rather than toward real terrain. Its drainage held at
0.0%, which argues against full degeneration, but the two explanations are not
separated by the evidence in hand.

WHAT WOULD SEPARATE THEM, and it is cheap: run the prototype at a near-origin site
and at 69 km and compare D. If D collapses near the origin, it was the lever arm and
the direction is dead. If it holds, the construction is real and the task is to make
it lever-arm-free without the decorrelating blend -- more sectors is the lever
detail_rill.h itself names as costing nothing.
