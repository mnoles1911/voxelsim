THE CONTOUR-TERRACING ARTIFACT: MEASURED, AND WHY NOTHING HAS FIXED IT
======================================================================
2026-07-30. Written because the artifact has survived ten attempts and the useful
output of that is a sharp statement of the constraint, not another guess.

THE ARTIFACT, QUANTIFIED WITHOUT ANYONE'S EYE. terrainprobe now measures the
box-counting dimension of the voxel level field's terrace boundaries, with controls
computed every run:

  tilted plane (control)        D = 1.000
  our carrier                   D = 1.006
  our amplified surface (v17)   D = 1.014
  REAL topographic contours     D = 1.15 - 1.25   (published, external)
  white noise (control)         D = 1.872

So the shipped surface's contours are ~1% of the way from a machined plane toward
natural. That is the owner's complaint, as a number.

NOTE THE TOP OF THE SCALE. D is bounded above by NOISE, not by realism. A term that
degenerates into per-voxel static raises D toward 1.87. D alone therefore cannot
distinguish "crooked like real ground" from "noisy", and must be read alongside the
drainage ladder and the terrace-run statistics.

WHAT IT IS NOT. Established by ablation with the shader mirrored, so the GPU
actually saw each change: the 1.6 m octave, the rill term, the bedding term, the
entire detail ladder at 1/16, and the fine tier itself. All left it intact. Removing
detail makes it WORSE. A sun-angle control (08:00 / 12:00 / 16:00) confirms geometry
rather than shading. A periodicity scan finds no periodic component at 1-2 m, which
is correct and was the wrong question: terracing is a THRESHOLD phenomenon in
floor(h / 100 mm), not a spectral one in h.

THE CONSTRAINT, which is the useful part. Crooking a contour needs of order one
voxel of vertical deviation at sub-metre wavelength. At 0.4 m that IS a gradient of
0.25. The v14/v15 drainage cap permits max(0.05, 0.5 x carrierGradient). So:
  * below ~50% grade the two requirements are arithmetically incompatible;
  * and the CARRIER alone measures D = 1.006, so it sets a floor no client term can
    lift -- the smoothness is upstream of the client entirely.

Two experiments confirm the trade is real and unprofitable:
  * doubling the cap's k to 1.0 cost 53.8% of the tile's drainage and moved D by
    -0.001. Amplitude is not the lever.
  * reshaping the micro band to a Hurst-0.8 ladder (v17: 400:90 -> 165, 200:25 ->
    95) moved local H at 0.4 m from 1.352 to 0.979, INTO the plan's [0.6, 1.0]
    target band, at zero drainage cost -- and moved D only 1.010 -> 1.014. Spectral
    shape is not the lever either.

THE ONE MECHANISM THAT SHOULD WORK, AND WHY THE SHIPPABLE FORM DOES NOT. A
displacement varying ONLY across the slope has a gradient perpendicular to the fall
line, so it cannot change the downhill component and cannot close a basin at any
amplitude -- exempt from the cap by construction. A prototype using the EXACT
gradient direction measured, on the fine tier at a 35% site:

  150 mm  D = 1.021   0.0% stranded
  400 mm  D = 1.060   0.0%
  800 mm  D = 1.155   0.0%      <- in the real band
 1600 mm  D = 1.307   42.9%     <- breaks

Promoted to a shippable form -- the rill's 16-direction table with two-frame
blending, which is what makes the rill free of the lever-arm defect documented at
length in detail_rill.h -- it collapses:

  amp 0     D = 1.014
  amp 3200  D = 1.020

The blend is the reason. At a 1.2 m across-slope wavelength the two fields either
side of the measured aspect are effectively INDEPENDENT, so averaging them destroys
the coherence that crooks a contour. The rill survives this because its features are
12.8 m along-slope, where the blend mostly affects orientation rather than phase.

AND THE PROTOTYPE'S RESULT IS PROBABLY A FALSE POSITIVE, which is why nothing was
shipped. detail_rill.h records that the exact-direction construction has a noise
phase whose derivative grows with distance from the world origin, degenerating into
per-voxel static -- measured there at 54 km. The sites used here sit at 69 km
(x = -69120 m). So the prototype's D = 1.155 is consistent with the metric climbing
toward the 1.872 NOISE control rather than toward real terrain. Its drainage held at
0.0%, which argues against full degeneration, but the two explanations are not
separated by the evidence in hand.

WHAT WOULD SEPARATE THEM, and it is cheap: run the prototype at a near-origin site
and at 69 km and compare D. If D collapses near the origin, it was the lever arm and
the direction is dead. If it holds, the construction is real and the task is to make
it lever-arm-free without the decorrelating blend -- more sectors is the lever
detail_rill.h itself names as costing nothing.

STATE: reverted to v17, tree green -- ctest 2/2, vxc_gpu bit-exact. Nothing from
this investigation ships.

=============================================================================
THE LEVER-ARM TEST, RUN. THE ACROSS-SLOPE DIRECTION IS A PROVEN DEAD END.
=============================================================================
The prototype was re-instated and measured on SYNTHETIC terrain, which is
statistically homogeneous, so any systematic change with position is the
construction and not the ground:

  distance from origin        D        boundary cells
        0 m                 0.976            4,623
        5 km                1.443           46,192
       69 km                1.412           47,712
      500 km                1.433           48,845

At the origin the field is coherent -- D just under 1.0, a modest boundary set.
Everywhere beyond, D is ~1.43 (PAST real terrain's 1.15-1.25, heading for the
1.872 noise control) and the boundary-cell count is 10x higher. That is
per-voxel static, exactly the degeneration detail_rill.h documents and measures
at 54 km. The real exemplar sites sit at 69 km.

SO THE PROTOTYPE'S D = 1.155 WAS DEGENERATION, NOT STRUCTURE. It is retracted.

This also supplies the companion signal the metric needed. D alone can be raised
by making a surface noisy; a 10x jump in BOUNDARY CELL COUNT at constant terrain
is how noise is told from crookedness. Quote both, always.

AND THE BLENDED FORM FAILS FOR THE SAME REASON FROM THE OTHER SIDE, which
explains its measured collapse to D = 1.020 at four times the amplitude. The two
frames' across-coordinates differ by about |p| * dTheta. At 69 km with 16 sectors
(11.25 deg) that is 13.5 km -- roughly 11,000 wavelengths of a 1.2 m field, so
the two blended fields are independent and averaging them destroys the phase.
Closing that gap needs dTheta < lambda / (4|p|) = 4.3e-6 rad, i.e. ~700,000
sectors. Raising kRillSectors, which detail_rill.h names as the free lever, does
not reach: 256 sectors still leaves ~845 wavelengths of separation at this range.

Anchoring the coordinate to a local lattice bounds |p| but trades the problem for
a seam: a 32 m anchor with 256 sectors gets separation down to ~0.5 wavelength,
which is coherent, at the cost of a visible discontinuity every 32 m -- the exact
artifact class this project exists to remove.

THE GENERAL STATEMENT, which is detail_rill.h's own theorem and now has a second
measurement behind it: ANY construction whose noise phase is a position-dependent
transform of the GLOBAL coordinate carries this defect. Either the transform is
exact and the phase derivative grows with |p| (static), or it is quantised and
blended and the phase decorrelates with |p| (vanishing). The rill term escapes
because its field is 2-D -- rotation mostly REORIENTS its features -- whereas for
a 1-D across-slope field the phase IS the content.

WHAT IS LEFT, honestly. Client-side terms cannot fix this: the CARRIER alone
measures D = 1.006, so the smoothness is upstream. The remaining candidates are
all upstream or out-of-band:
  * give the BAKE sub-metre roughness so the carrier itself is crooked. It
    currently stops at 1.875 m/px, so this means a finer baked tier or a
    bake-side roughness pass below its own Nyquist;
  * relax the drainage bar, which is stricter than real ground at 10 cm and is
    what forbids vertical roughness -- this needs a real decimetre DTM to set
    honestly, and none is available on this machine;
  * accept it as a property of 10 cm voxels on smooth ground and address the
    LOOK through material and shading variation, which costs no geometry and so
    no drainage.

=============================================================================
THE MOUND ATTEMPT, AND THE GEOMETRIC REASON THE WHOLE CLASS CANNOT WORK
=============================================================================
Twelfth attempt, and the one that finally produces a proof rather than another
measurement.

THE IDEA. Every previous attempt perturbed a RAMP. The level sets of a monotone
surface are nested parallel curves, so roughening or shortening them leaves them
parallel -- and parallel, evenly spaced bands ARE the artifact. Only CLOSED
contours break that. Closed contours need local extrema. Pits were ruled out by
the drainage cap, but MOUNDS are not pits: water flows around a mound, and on a
slope a mound only dams if it is taller than the hill drops across its half-width.
So: rectified (positive-only) noise, amplitude self-limited to slope/4, which is
half the damming bound.

RESULT: rib continuity essentially unmoved (p50 1.3 m, p90 4.0 vs 4.1 m), drainage
untouched at 0.0% stranded, and the in-engine raking capture shows the ribbing
unchanged. Reverted.

WHY, AND THIS IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING. The reason is not the amplitude or the
lattice; it is geometry, and it kills every variant of this idea:

  A closed contour on a slope requires the surface to be locally flat or reversed.
  That requires the perturbation's gradient to REACH the carrier's own gradient.
  A perturbation whose gradient reaches the slope's gradient is, by the definition
  the drainage cap uses, exactly the thing that creates pits.

So on steep ground "closed contours" and "no pits" are the same constraint pulled
in opposite directions. The self-limiting amplitude that makes mounds safe
(slope/4) is precisely what stops them from ever closing a contour. Safe and
ineffective are the same setting.

WHICH MEANS: for a HEIGHTFIELD on a smooth steep slope, parallel contour banding
at the voxel scale is not a defect to be tuned out. It is forced. Any single-valued
h(x, y) that is monotone over a few metres has nested parallel level sets, and
quantising it produces parallel bands whose spacing is voxel / grade. No client-side
term can escape this, which is consistent with every measurement in this file: the
carrier alone sits at D = 1.006 and rib p50 13.7 m, and twelve different terms moved
those numbers around without changing what the surface IS.

THE TWO ESCAPES, both structural rather than parametric:

  1. STOP BEING A SMOOTH RAMP. Real steep ground is not monotone over metres -- it
     has benches, ledges, cliff bands and outcrops, which are metre-scale STRUCTURE
     with near-vertical faces. Those break banding because the slope itself changes,
     not because noise was added to it. This is bake-side: the bake currently stops
     at 1.875 m/px and its thermal relaxation actively smooths toward the angle of
     repose, which is the opposite of what produces benches.
  2. STOP BEING A HEIGHTFIELD. Overhangs and undercuts are not expressible as
     h(x, y) at all. density3 already exists for this and is heavily gated; a
     genuine cliff with a recessed base reads as rock in a way no heightfield can.

RAISING THE BEDDING TERM was tried as a proxy for (1) -- it is the one existing term
that makes metre-scale ledges, at 120 mm (1.2 voxels). At 400 mm rib p99 went
15.6 -> 5.4 m and at 900 mm to 2.8 m, drainage untouched at 0.0% throughout. The
metric improved 5x and the picture did not, because shorter ribs are still PARALLEL
ribs. Reverted, but recorded: it is the cheapest existing lever if (1) is pursued,
and the reason it failed alone is that bedding modulates the surface rather than
replacing the ramp with facets.
