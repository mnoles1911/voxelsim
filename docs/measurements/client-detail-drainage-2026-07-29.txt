CLIENT DETAIL DESTROYS THE BAKE'S DRAINAGE — measured 2026-07-29, worldgen v13
==============================================================================

HOW THIS WAS FOUND, because the route matters more than the number. v13 was
accepted on mean slope by scale (plains 8.116 -> 2.936 deg against a real
Illinois till plain's 2.134, alpine preserved at 28.168) and on the geomorphon
class fractions. Both are AREA statistics: they ask how rough the ground is and
what shape its cells are. Neither can see whether the ground is CONNECTED, and
connectivity is what makes terrain read as water-shaped rather than as texture.

An in-engine capture of the alpine exemplar (tile -5,15, spawn -69120,238080,
1600x900, 150 s settle, visiblePreCap L0-only so these are true 10 cm voxels)
showed a field of isolated one-voxel pillars rather than a mountainside. The
metric below is what that picture is, in numbers.

  vxc_terrainprobe <s1 tiledir> 20260719 <x> <y> 512 --baseline

               alpine (-5,15)          plains (-55,20)
               carrier -> amplified    carrier -> amplified
  interior sinks       0 -> 1625            98 -> 9525
  sinks per km2        0 -> 11020          665 -> 64596
  stranded area      0.0% -> 87.9%       75.2% -> 97.4%
  mean flow path   223.6 m -> 28.9 m    39.3 m -> 4.3 m
  path ratio            0.129                0.109

READ THE ALPINE COLUMN FIRST. The carrier -- which is the baked tier, carrying
routed drainage, incised channels and talus at repose -- has ZERO interior sinks
and moves water 224 m across the window. After the client's detail band is
composited on top, 87.9% of a 40.6%-grade mountainside cannot drain and the mean
flow path is 28.9 m. Water does not pond on a 40% slope. This is not a tuning
disagreement; it is the surface no longer being a surface water can run down.

The plains column says the same thing with less contrast because its carrier is
already poorly drained at this scale (75.2%), which is itself expected on a 1.7%
grade at 10 cm postings and is not the finding here.

WHY EVERY EXISTING GATE PASSED THIS. Mean slope by scale, the geomorphon
histogram, curvature asymmetry, Hurst and the spectrum-matched surrogate control
are all computed on the height field's LOCAL statistics. A field of uncorrelated
decimetre pits has correct local statistics by construction -- that is what fBm
IS -- so it passes all of them. Only a non-local pass (routing) sees it. This is
the same lesson as the surrogate result recorded in
docs/terrain-validation-2026-07.md, where all four realism gates passed a plain
whose frac_flat was 0.014 against a real 0.48-0.67: internally consistent, and
the wrong kind of landscape.

THE MECHANISM, and it is arithmetic rather than mysterious. Each octave
contributes a local gradient of about amplitude/lattice (the amplifier's own v11
comment states this and used it to cut two octaves). At v13 the coarse table is
{25600,2600} {6400,1100} {1600,500} {400,90} {200,25}, and the shaping and metre
bands take the relief gate (up to 2.0x) times a curvature gate (up to 1.75x on
crests, 1.375x in the halved micro form):

  25.6 m : 2600 x 3.50 = 9100 mm  -> gradient 0.36
   6.4 m : 1100 x 3.50 = 3850 mm  -> gradient 0.60
   1.6 m :  500 x 2.75 = 1375 mm  -> gradient 0.86
   0.4 m :   90 x 1.375 =  124 mm -> gradient 0.31
   0.2 m :   25 x 1.375 =   34 mm -> gradient 0.17
                                     ------------
                                     up to ~2.3

A detail gradient of 2.3 on ground whose own gradient is 0.4 reverses the
downhill direction almost everywhere, and a reversed downhill direction is a
closed basin. Note the sign of the coupling: the relief gate scales detail UP
where relief is high, so it is loudest exactly where the physical argument says
it must be quietest. v13 fixed WHICH CLASS gets how much amplitude and left the
gradient unconstrained in all of them.

A NOTE ON THE METRIC I FIRST REACHED FOR, so nobody repeats it. Mean voxel
terrace run looked like the natural readout and is not usable across classes: on
a 40.6% grade the carrier alone steps one voxel every 2.5 columns, so the run
length has a geometric floor set by base slope. Alpine measures 1.81 against a
gentle site's 2.62 not because it is rougher but because it is steeper. Plateau
COMPONENT COUNT is slope-sensitive too but far less so, and it did respond to an
ablation (halving the 6.4 m and 1.6 m amplitudes took alpine from 19,576
components to 9,572 while barely moving the mean run from 1.81 to 1.92) -- which
is what pointed at the gradient rather than at any single octave.

THE ACCEPTANCE BAR THIS IMPLIES, stated so it can be checked rather than
argued. Detail must not reverse the carrier's downhill direction. If the total
post-gate detail gradient stays strictly below the carrier's own gradient, no
new closed basin can form, because the sum's downhill direction is the carrier's.
That is a provable statement about the composite and it is the right shape of
constraint, because it is scale-free and class-free.

It cannot be applied unconditionally: on flat ground the carrier's gradient is
~0.017 and requiring detail below that would delete the decimetre roughness that
exists precisely to break up terrace runs, which is the artifact the microrelief
band was added for. So the cap has a floor. The proposed form is

  detailGradient <= max(gradFloor, k * carrierGradient)

with k and gradFloor set by measurement against these drainage numbers, applied
as ONE normalisation over the whole post-gate ladder rather than as per-octave
retuning. As a scale-DOWN it can only tighten the surface bound, so
surfaceUpperBoundMm stays sound without re-derivation.

WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED HERE. That some stranding is wrong: real ground has closed
decimetre depressions (stones, tussocks, hoofprints) and a real 10 cm DTM would
show a few percent. The finding is 87.9% on a 40% slope against a carrier's 0%,
and the 100x jump in sinks per km2. Nor is the plains capture called a defect --
79.3% of its area in flat plateaus of 4 m or more is close to what a 1.7% grade
voxelised at 10 cm must produce, and its step edges are ragged rather than clean
contours, which is the microrelief band doing its job.
