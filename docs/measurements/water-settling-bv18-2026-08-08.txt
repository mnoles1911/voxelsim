WATER FINDS ITS LEVEL -- bake_ver 18, wet alpine block, seed 20260719.
Namespace terrain-diffusion-unlabeled-80b9ca451a23eae4-ba6ff22d6. 2026-08-08.

THE OWNER'S COMPLAINT: "the water blocks are not being placed correctly.
Gravity and physics would naturally cause the water to spread out to fill the
terrain and level out." He was right, and the measurement I first offered
against it was wrong -- it flooded UPHILL ONLY, so it measured bank above the
waterline rather than floor below it. See river-width-is-bed-limited for that
retraction; do not use its conclusion.

THE DEFECT, on the bake's own elevation_m at bv17:
  dry cells touching water            285,518
  ...whose ground is BELOW that water 169,759  (59.5%),  p50 0.90 m below
  in the 0.1-5 m band: 168,708 cells = 59 hectares of channel floor, ONE tile,
  standing dry with water 90 cm above it in the next cell.

CAUSE: `_fill_levels` carries a level only UP a drawn cell's own donors, so a
bed cell upstream of nothing drawn terminates at a root and gets NaN -- "dry for
want of a number rather than for want of room", in the code's own words. One
path lookup, so it can never notice water standing beside it.

WHAT SHIPPED, in the order the measurements forced:

 1. SPREAD. A dry cell joins at its highest adjacent water surface when its
    ground is min_depth below it. Alone this has NO equilibrium: 8 sweeps add
    705k cells, 24 add 1,510k, still growing at the cap, depth and reach both
    creeping up. That is the documented 209x flood -- a level carried away from
    the water that owns it. max_iter was not a safety bound, it was the only
    thing stopping it.
 2. DRAIN. An added cell may not hold water perched above dry ground it could
    run off onto. Drawn cells exempt: a river drains by definition and its
    escape is the wet cell downstream.
 3. RELAX 1+2 TO A FIXED POINT. Converges in 3 rounds, spreading 2,398,366
    cells and draining 2,327,675 of them -- 97% of everything it tries runs
    back off. This is the answer to "how do we stop it spreading at one z
    level": not a cap, a rule.
 4. DISCHARGE BUDGET. width(Q) x depth(Q) x cell_m per channel cell, so the
    allowance GROWS DOWN THE RIVER as the watershed does. Verified on a
    synthetic flat trough: at headwater Q it keeps 0 of 120 spread cells, at
    trunk Q all 120. Same terrain, discharge decides.
 5. SECTION SOLVE, because a budget must REDISTRIBUTE not delete. L_k =
    (budget/area + S_k)/k over cells sorted by ground; the answer is the
    largest k whose ground is still under L_k. On a flat bench: headwater 2
    cells wide at 0.18 m, trunk 20 cells wide at 0.35 m.
 6. CAPPED LEVEL RELAXATION over the whole surface INCLUDING the channel. The
    step below is why: pinning the channel locked in the roughness, because the
    section solve lowers each drawn cell independently and that is where the
    discontinuity is born.

Every variant before the last traded one of the owner's complaints for the
other. The last moves both: dry ground below adjacent water -32%, and the step
between neighbouring water cells -43% at p90, BELOW the baseline it started
from. Fewer smoothing iterations keep more water and are worse on both quality
metrics, so 12 is measured, not cautious.

THE COST: 10.7-16.5% less wet area per tile, and the owner has said more water
looked better. The mechanism is an asymmetry rather than physics -- the
relaxation may raise a level only to its ceiling but may dry a cell
permanently, so iterations drift the surface down. water_level_smooth_iters is
the dial. Bake time roughly doubles, ~790 s/tile.

CONFIGURATION SWEEP AFTER THE CEILING FIX. Tile (-4,-4), 2026-08-08.

The ceiling bug (settled cells had no ceiling, so relaxation walked them up
banks) is fixed: the cap is now the level each cell SETTLED at. That changed the
trade materially, so all three candidate configurations were re-measured.

                              wet   vs base   dry-below   >1vox    p90
  baseline bv17           364,354      --      161,082    17.4%   338 mm
  lean: settle+drain only 419,967   +15.3%     123,717    21.2%   421 mm
  budget + smooth 4       316,926   -13.0%     121,693    23.7%   285 mm
  budget + smooth 12      261,803   -28.1%      97,518    13.8%   147 mm  <- default

SMOOTH 4 IS WORSE THAN BOTH ENDS on roughness (23.7% against 21.2% and 13.8%).
The trade is NOT monotonic, so "split the difference" is not available and the
choice is genuinely binary: lean for volume, smooth 12 for quality.

WHY SMOOTH 12 IS THE DEFAULT DESPITE BEING THE DRIEST, and this is the part the
raw wet count hides. The owner's complaint was not "there is too little water",
it was "the water is not filling the lower spots where we expect it, while
climbing the bank on the other side". The metric for THAT is dry-below -- ground
lying under the water beside it -- and smooth 12 has the best figure by a wide
margin: 97,518 against a 161,082 baseline, down 39%. It carries less water
overall because it stops putting water where water would not stand, and fills
more of the places that should be wet. Roughness is also 13.8% against a 17.4%
baseline with p90 down from 338 to 147 mm, so the staircase improves at the same
time.

The -28% wet is still a real cost and remains the open risk: it is the one thing
made worse, and the owner has previously said more water looked better. If it
reads as too dry in the editor, `lean` is the fallback and it is one constant
away (water_settle_discharge_budget=False, water_level_smooth_iters=0).
