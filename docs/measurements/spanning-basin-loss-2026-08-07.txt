TILE-SPANNING LAKES: WHAT IS DROPPED, AND WHAT HAPPENS TO THE HOLE
2026-08-07, wet alpine block, bake_ver 15. Wave 2 of the water plan.

THE QUESTION
------------
`basin_exclude_spanning` defaults TRUE, so a basin whose extent touches the
tile's interior edge is dropped from the registry. A world survey found 1322
spanning components against 997 kept -- and MORE LAKE AREA excluded (4609 ha)
than retained (3596 ha). If that generalised it would be the largest open water
defect in the project.

Two things had to be established: what happens to a dropped basin's HOLE, and
whether the 57% figure survives at the current bake version.

ANSWER 1: THE HOLE IS NOT DUG. THE LAKE SIMPLY DOES NOT EXIST.
--------------------------------------------------------------
Read out of the code, pipeline.py B5.reopen_basins:

    out["z"][keep] -= out["basin_depth"][keep]

`keep = survey.keep_mask()`, and basins.py:671-684 confirms keep_mask is built
from `kept_labels` -- REGISTERED basins only. A dropped basin is not in it.

So the depression that B2a filled for routing STAYS FILLED in the shipped
ground. The failure mode is NOT a dry pit where a lake should be (which is what
I expected and said out loud before checking). It is:

  * no lake registered, so nothing fills it;
  * the ground artificially FLAT at the fill level, because the fill is never
    subtracted back out.

A large tile-spanning lake therefore ships as a flat plain. That is less
alarming than a pit and still wrong: the terrain is not the terrain, and the
lake is missing.

ANSWER 2: ON THIS BLOCK THE LOSS IS ~9% BY AREA, NOT 57%
---------------------------------------------------------
`pregen`'s per-tile line now prints `span_drop=<count>/<area>ha@<maxdepth>m`
(added 2026-08-06) so this stops being a stale citation. Six tiles, bake_ver 15:

    tile        kept basin      dropped (spanning)
    (-5,-4)     1.1% / 22 m     29 basins /  25 ha @ 21 m
    (-4,-4)     0.5% / 34 m     12 basins /  18 ha @ 40 m
    (-3,-4)     0.9% / 46 m     18 basins /  24 ha @ 41 m
    (-5,-5)     1.7% / 44 m     29 basins /  35 ha @ 34 m
    (-4,-5)     2.4% / 36 m     16 basins /  22 ha @ 40 m
    (-3,-5)     1.5% / 40 m     19 basins /  22 ha @ 20 m

    total dropped: 123 basins, 146 ha
    tile area 15.36 km square = 23,593 ha; kept basin coverage 0.5-2.4%
    => kept area is roughly 118-566 ha per tile, ~1,500 ha over the block

So dropped area is about **9% of lake area on this block**, not the 56% the
world survey implied. The dropped basins are also SHALLOWER on average than the
kept maxima on four of six tiles.

WHY THE WORLD FIGURE DOES NOT GENERALISE. The survey (docs/lake-survey/) is 12
tiles at bake_ver 7 -- eight versions stale -- and its own text warns the
numbers do not survive a bump, citing a measured -5.3% component change across
7->8 alone. Its area total is also dominated by ONE tile: (-13,-6) contributes
2358 of the 4609 ha from 69 components, i.e. **51% of the world's excluded lake
area comes from a single tile**. That tile is not in the wet alpine block.

VERDICT
-------
Real defect, not an emergency, and NOT the thing to fix first on this block.
Two consequences worth carrying:

  * the fix (global lake id + BASIN_TABLE_VERSION 2, stop excluding spanning
    basins) is still correct and still wanted -- a lake crossing a seam is
    registered independently by each side "and the two need not agree"
    (lake-survey.md:56), with nothing in the codec to reconcile them;
  * but it should be scheduled on the evidence of a WORLD survey at bake_ver 15,
    not on this block, because the loss is concentrated in tiles this block does
    not contain. Re-running the survey is the prerequisite, and it is cheap
    relative to acting on a stale number.

CORRECTION ON THE RECORD
------------------------
I flagged this to the owner as possibly "the largest open water defect in the
project" before measuring it, on the strength of the 57% figure and a guess that
the hole was being dug. Both halves were wrong: the hole is not dug, and on the
block we actually look at, the loss is ~9%. The 57% is real but is a
component count on 12 stale tiles, half of whose excluded area is one tile.
