WHY THE EXAGGERATED EXPONENTS FAIL channel_is_continuous_across_confluences
2026-08-06 (investigated 2026-08-07). Explains a blocker; does not clear it.

THE DECISION BEING BLOCKED
--------------------------
The owner asked for dramatic rivers: "I think we change the exponents to force
more dramatic rivers even though it's different than earth. Exaggeration is ok
to keep things interesting."

Target values, width/depth exponents in Q8: 166/128 (0.6484 / 0.5000) against
Earth's 102/90 (0.3984 / 0.3516). Over the 256x discharge span between a
drawable headwater and the largest trunk that is 4.2 -> 154 m of width and
0.50 -> 7.9 m of depth, against Earth's 2.8 -> 26 m and 0.39 -> 2.8 m. Both
ends stay off the 400 m / 25 m caps.

At those values, voxel-core's channel_is_continuous_across_confluences fails
its `cs.inBed` assertion -- a sample taken AT a river junction reports itself
outside the channel bed. Wider channels should make that assertion EASIER, so
the failure was not understood and the change was backed out rather than
shipped on an unexplained red in channel CONTINUITY.

THE MECHANISM, read out of the code
-----------------------------------
voxel-core/src/channel.cpp, ChannelField::evaluate (:228-275):

  * candidates are the reaches registered in the sample's grid cell;
  * a candidate is skipped if `dMm > channelInfluenceMm(width, depth)`, so the
    influence radius grows with BOTH width and depth;
  * the winner is the reach with the LOWEST cross-section target, not the
    nearest one -- ":264 Deepest cut wins: at a confluence the trunk's lower
    bed must swallow the tributary's, or the join is a step";
  * `out.inBed = dMm <= halfW` (:272) then uses the WINNING reach's half-width
    and the sample's distance TO THAT REACH.

Raising the depth exponent 0.3516 -> 0.5 makes a large trunk far deeper
(3.6 m -> 10.6 m at this world's maximum Q). `channelTargetMm` for a point at
lateral distance d from that trunk is `bed + (d - halfW)/runPerRise`, so a much
lower bed wins the lowest-target comparison at a much greater d. At a confluence
the TRUNK therefore wins even where the sample sits on the TRIBUTARY's
centreline -- and at that distance `dMm > halfW` of the trunk, so `inBed` is
false.

So the failure is a real geometric consequence of deeper channels under the
deepest-cut-wins rule, not a defect in the exponent change. What it says is:
deeper trunks dominate a wider radius, and a confluence node can be classified
as BANK of the trunk rather than BED of its own reach.

WHAT STILL HOLDS
----------------
The continuity property the test exists to defend is unaffected. In the failing
run only the `inBed` checks failed; `cs.bedMm <= nodeBedMm[fromNode]` -- "the
trunk swallows the tributary, no step up" -- passed on every confluence. The bed
remains single-valued, continuous and descending.

BLAST RADIUS TODAY: NONE ON SHIPPED WATER
-----------------------------------------
`ChannelField` is UNWIRED. channel.h:26-31 states it: "Until then nothing here
changes worldgen's output, so kWorldGenVersion is UNTOUCHED". Confirmed by
search: no reference in amplifier.cpp, worldgen.ush, or anything in
ue-project/Source. Its only consumers are its own tests, vxc_riverprobe, and the
Python-side exponent-drift gate.

The width and depth the SHIPPED water uses come from terrain_service/bake/
water.py, which mirrors the same laws independently.

THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE BLOCKER
----------------------------------------------
Changing the WIDTH exponent will barely widen the drawn river.

The shipped extent rule is `lateral_fill` (BakeConstants.water_extent_mode
default), and it does not read the width law at all: a cell is wet when it
stands below the surface of the water it drains into. Architecture §11a records
the consequence already -- "Drawn width is roughly 20 m everywhere... discharge
enters only through the surface height", Spearman(drawn width, Q) = +0.457.

So under the shipped rule the lever on width is the DEPTH exponent, not the
width exponent: a deeper trunk stands its water surface higher, and a higher
surface floods further laterally before the ground rises through it. The width
exponent only moves `q_drawable_m3_yr` (the drawable threshold is derived from
the width law), which changes how much of the network is drawn at all -- a
separate and more dangerous knob, since raising it can delete headwaters.

That reframes the owner's request: dramatic rivers come from the depth exponent
under lateral_fill, and the width exponent should be reviewed for what it does
to the drawable threshold before it is moved.

NOT DONE, AND WHY
-----------------
The exponents were NOT flipped in this session. bake_ver 15 changed three other
water constants at once (F6 and the two slope terms), and stacking the exponents
into the same bake would make the resulting A/B unreadable -- there would be no
way to attribute a visible change to either. One change at a time, so the
picture means something.

The plumbing is ready: both exponents are now identity-covered bake constants
(BakeConstants.channel_width_exp_q8 / channel_depth_exp_q8). Before flipping:

  1. decide whether `inBed` at a confluence is a property worth keeping under
     deep channels, or whether the assertion should narrow to the continuity
     check it was really written to defend;
  2. measure what the width exponent does to `q_drawable_m3_yr` and how much of
     the drawn network disappears at 166/256;
  3. flip depth first, alone, and look at it.
