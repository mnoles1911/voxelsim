THE WORLD'S LARGEST RIVER: WHERE IT IS, AND WHAT THE BAKE DOES WITH IT
seed 20260719, whole world (289 coarse tiles, 8704^2 @ 30 m)
bake_ver 14, TERRAIN_VERSION 8 unchanged, water_min_width_px 1.5 -> 1.0
2026-08-05
================================================================================

THE TASK WAS "bake the world's largest trunk, reported by survey_world_water.py
at 82.5 m^3/s, which through the shipped laws is 55 m wide and 5.4 m deep."

82.5 m^3/s REPRODUCES EXACTLY and is not a river. The largest trunk that is on
land is 14.2 m^3/s; the largest that the bake will actually draw is 9.87. The
three numbers differ for two independent reasons and both are measured below.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE 82.5 m^3/s CELL IS ON THE ABYSSAL FLOOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    python tools/survey_world_water.py accumulate \
        --coarse-dir .../80b9ca451a23eae4/000000000135276f/s1 \
        --out D:/tmp/worldwater-trunk
    -> trunk max 2.6035e+09 m3/yr = 82.5 m3/s          (reproduced, exactly)

Where that cell is was never asked before:

    row 3821, col 0    tile (-16,-9)    elevation -3,132 m    catchment 24,440 km2

It is below sea level by three kilometres, it is against the grid's west edge,
and its "catchment" is 82% of every land cell in the world. `fill_depressions`
floods the ocean basin exactly as readily as a valley, so the whole planet's
runoff converges into one submarine pit and is counted once, there. Tracing D8
downstream from it terminates in a single step: it is a sink, not a reach.

Every one of the top twelve whole-grid maxima is below sea level; the shallowest
is at -179 m. There is no land cell anywhere near 82.5 m^3/s.

    LAND cells only, D8 and MFD agreeing to 0.2%:

      largest land trunk        14.18 m3/s   tile (-8,-5), z = 3 m
      its catchment              1,960 km2 = 6.6% of the world's land
      law at that Q              27.0 m wide, 2.89 m deep

The world's land is 29,911 km2 of a 68,184 km2 extent (43.9%). The task brief's
57,600 km2 is not this world's land area; at 84% land it could not be, and the
Thames comparison scales with it. A 1,960 km2 catchment is a River Wear or a
Ribble, not a Thames, and 14.2 m^3/s over 1,960 km2 is 228 mm/yr of runoff,
which is a perfectly ordinary temperate yield. The architecture is right. The
world is simply small and its largest basin is small.

4. THE DEFECT THAT MATTERS: THE TRUNK DOES NOT CROSS THE TILE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Interior discharge maximum, from the npz dump, against the coarse trunk the
same tile should be carrying:

      tile      coarse trunk   bake qmax (padded)   INTERIOR max      ratio
      (-6,-3)      9.86 m3/s       3.11e+08          9.868 m3/s       1.00

      (-6,-2)      9.98            3.13e+08          0.120            0.012

      (-6,-1)      9.98            3.16e+08          0.005            0.0005
      (-7,-5)     14.17            3.59e+08          0.041            0.003

    for contrast, the wet-country tiles, whose interiors DO carry it:
      (-3,-4)      5.30            1.78e+08          5.316            1.00
      (-4,-5)      4.11            1.30e+08          4.113            1.00

On four of the six tiles the pyramid delivered the trunk to the padded domain
and the interior never saw it. This is the failure `drawnpad` was added to make
visible (pregen._inflow_note: "the water is in the APRON and leaves without
crossing the tile") and HYDROLOGY_RESIDUALS #7 names. It is not rare and it is
not confined to odd tiles: it took out the mouth of the river that was baked and
the whole of the nominally-largest river in the world.

(-6,-1) is the clean case: injected 6.33e8 m^3/yr, drawnpad 20 cells,
water=0.000%wet. A tile carrying 10 m^3/s baked completely dry.

THE MECHANISM IS NOT ISOLATED. Two candidates were checked and NEITHER
separates the tiles cleanly, so neither is being claimed:

  a) the apron blind spot. The bake's own warning,
     "contains a flat/basin NNNN m across, wider than the 960 m apron":

        warned and LOST   (-7,-5) 4239 m   (-7,-1) 4288 m   (-6,-1) 1942 m
        warned and KEPT   (-6,-3) 1592 m
        silent and LOST   (-6,-2)
        silent and KEPT   the five wet-country tiles

     It fires on the largest flats, which are also the tiles that lose it most
     badly, but (-6,-3) carries a 9.868 m^3/s trunk straight across its
     interior with a 1,592 m flat in it and (-6,-2) loses one without a flat
     at all. The warning is a hint, not the discriminator.

  b) basin capture. Registered-basin share is 20.9 / 23.2 / 4.0 / 2.4 / 0.7%
     on (-7,-1)(-6,-1)(-6,-2)(-6,-3)(-5,-3). It orders the two worst tiles
     right and then fails: (-6,-2) at 4.0% loses 99% of the trunk while
     (-6,-3) at 2.4% loses none. Basins also cannot explain a low
     `discharge_m3_yr`, which is written before the basin mask is applied.

What IS clean is that all six tiles are EXOTIC -- the river is imported, not
local. Local land runoff, mm/yr:

      (-6,-2) 0.1    (-6,-1) 0.3    (-6,-3) 1.2    (-7,-5) 3.4
      (-5,-3) 70.0   (-7,-1) 18.0
      wet-country five: 608 - 1,262

Every tile that loses the trunk gets essentially ALL of its discharge through
`inject_edge_inflow`, and `APRON_BLIND_SPOT` records that injection "runs after
the fill". The wet-country tiles make their own rivers locally and never
exercise that path hard. That is where to look; it is not proof.

5. WIDTH AND DEPTH, MEASURED, AGAINST THE LAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

On (-6,-3), the tile that carries the trunk:

      Q at the trunk                        9.868 m3/s
      law: channel_width_m                  23.4 m
      law: water_depth_m                     2.54 m

      DRAWN depth,  p50 over 208,946 wet px  2.51 m
      DRAWN depth,  p90                      2.54 m
      raster ribbon width, p50              31.9 m
      raster ribbon width, p90             100.8 m

DEPTH IS NOT A DEFECT AND CANNOT BE ONE. `graded_water_surface` writes
`water_depth_m(Q)` and nothing else; the drawn depth IS the law evaluated at the
cell's own Q, so it agrees to the quantisation by construction. It agreed on the
alpine block too (drawn max 2.052 m against a law 2.044 m at that block's
5.32 m^3/s). The ONLY way to draw a deeper river is to route more water to it.
"The law says 5.4 m and the bake draws 2 m" would therefore never have been a
depth bug; it was the 82.5 figure being wrong about Q.

WIDTH runs WIDER than the law, not narrower: 31.9 m drawn against 23.4 m
predicted at p50, 100.8 m at p90. `lateral_fill` decides width from the terrain,
so a floodplain reach spreads well past the hydraulic-geometry channel. Along
the composed thalweg the guarded perpendicular river width is p50 6.56 m,
p90 31.88 m, max 120.94 m -- the ribbon statistic and the perpendicular
statistic disagree by the amount `river_long_profile.py`'s docstring predicts.

9. THE THRESHOLD CHANGE, AND THE IDENTITY GATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

water_min_width_px 1.5 -> 1.0, on the owner's call:

      q_drawable_m3_yr   1.5286e+06 -> 5.5250e+05 m3/yr
                         48.4 -> 17.5 l/s      (factor 2.77)
      marginal channel   2.812 m -> 1.875 m wide

0.75 px is the real floor and it is a floor in the law, not a judgement:
`channel_width_m` is anchored at 1.5 m for `water_q_perennial_m3_yr`, so
`q_drawable_m3_yr` SATURATES -- 0.75 px and 0.50 px both return 3.1558e5 m^3/yr
(10.0 l/s), which is the perennial anchor itself.

MEASURED EFFECT on these five tiles. Both bakes were run in full, 1.5 and 1.0,
into their own namespaces, and the wet counts compared off the shipped .vxtl:

      tile      wet @1.5      wet @1.0     change   local runoff mm/yr
      (-7,-1)    586,790     2,116,042    +260.6%          18.0
      (-5,-3)     78,746       114,416     +45.3%          70.0
      (-6,-3)    208,946       208,946      +0.0%           1.2
      (-6,-2)     75,876        75,876      +0.0%           0.1
      (-6,-1)          0             0      +0.0%           0.3

THE "ROUGHLY 2.8x MORE NETWORK" DOES NOT ARRIVE HERE, and the reason is
specific rather than disappointing. The threshold is a cut in Q, and a cell only
lands in the newly-admitted band (5.5e5 to 1.53e6 m^3/yr) if enough LOCAL runoff
accumulates on it. On (-6,-2) local runoff is 0.1 mm/yr: at 1.875 m pixels that
is 3.5e-4 m^3/yr per cell, so reaching 5.5e5 would take 1.5e9 cells and the
padded domain has 8.5e7. There is no intermediate-Q water on that tile AT ALL --
the discharge field is bimodal, near-zero where it is local and ~3e8 where the
pyramid injected it, with nothing in between for a threshold to catch.

So the change is worth exactly as much as a tile has rain. It bought 3.6x on the
one tile with 18 mm/yr and 1.45x on the one with 70, and nothing on the three
deserts the big river crosses. On the wet-country block (608-1,262 mm/yr) it
would be expected to pay in full; that was not re-baked here.

11. WHAT SHOULD HAPPEN NEXT, in the order the numbers support
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. THE APRON HANDOFF (section 4) outranks everything else here. Four of six
   tiles dropped a 10 m^3/s river between the pyramid and the interior. Until
   that is fixed no river in this world reaches its own mouth, and every
   "rivers are too shallow" report is really this. The three tiles that lost it
   all carry the flat-wider-than-the-apron warning; that is the lead.

2. RIVER WATER ON THE SEAFLOOR (section 7) is now 66% of the wet pixels in any
   block that touches a coast, and every large river in this world ends at a
   coast. It is no longer a cosmetic defect on the edge of a corridor.

3. DO NOT chase depth. It is the law evaluated at Q, exactly, and it always has
   been. Chase Q -- which means 1 and 2.
