FOR SCREENSHOTS
---------------
The "best tile" column is where to point a capture. A tile is 15.36 km across,
so its world coordinates are (tile_x * 15360, tile_y * 15360) metres from the
origin of the tile grid. Prefer the ones at or near 100%: -4_-2 (tundra),
2_-8 (savanna), -2_-7 (beach), -6_-2 (grassland), -3_-11 (desert).

RAINFOREST and TEMPERATE_FOREST have no dominant tile -- their best are 59.5%
and 56.6% -- so a capture there will show a mixed scene. That is a real
property of the world, not a sampling problem.

TWO IMBALANCES, BOTH CLIENT-SIDE AND RETUNABLE
-----------------------------------------------
1. TUNDRA_ALPINE at 26.34% is high. This is a DIRECT CONSEQUENCE of Wave 2b:
   elev_gain 1.6 raised the terrain, and the treeline is a fixed
   kBiomeTreelineBaseMm 900 m + 150 m per degree C, so more land now sits
   above it. The trade was made deliberately for bigger mountains; it can be
   unwound by raising the treeline rather than by lowering elev_gain, which
   would cost the peaks.

2. TEMPERATE_FOREST at 2.43% is low against Earth's ~10-15%. Savanna takes the
   warm-seasonal band ahead of it in classifyBiome's gate order, and savanna
   is over-firing (18.69% here, and up to 55% on other seeds) because the
   conditioning channels are quantile-matched ONE AT A TIME -- every marginal
   is correct while the joint is not. See biome-gates-2026-08-01.txt.

Neither requires regenerating tiles: biome.h classifies from the tiles' climate
channels at runtime.

EVERY SEED HAS DESERTS (6.8-19.0%) and a non-zero hot-arid corner. That is the
question this was run to answer, and it passes: the calibration is not
overfitted to one seed.

DO NOT read the spread as seed character. Nine tiles is 2,124 km2, and climate
decorrelates at ~20 km while these samples sit 184 km apart -- so each seed
gets about nine independent draws, and the per-seed mean is noisy. The proof is
in the table itself: seed 20260719 reads peak_m 1471 here, while the full
121-tile set of THE SAME SEED peaks at 6,021 m. Same world, 4x apart, purely
from sample size. Separating true seed variation from sampling noise needs far
more tiles per seed than this.

BIOME TRANSITION DISTANCE -- the owner's actual stated goal
================================================================

The ask was: "richer, more varied world in which the terrain character
transition and occur in CLOSE PROXIMITY to one another so the world feels more
alive", and "jump from Iceland like rugged terrain to eastern European dense
temperate forests in relatively short order", explicitly FASTER than Earth.

Nothing had measured that. This does, on the stitched 289-tile world
(1088x1088 at 240 m/px = 261 km across, 50.0% land).

BIOME RUN LENGTH along E-W land transects, n = 30,301
  p25   0.5 km
  MEDIAN 1.4 km
  p75   5.5 km
  p95  19.9 km

DISTINCT BIOMES WITHIN A RADIUS
   5 km   mean 2.59   median 3   max 5
  10 km   mean 3.84   median 4   max 7
  25 km   mean 6.40   median 7   max 8
  50 km   mean 7.88   median 8   max 8

Within 25 km a player typically encounters SEVEN of the eight biomes; within
50 km, all eight. On Earth that traverse is hundreds to thousands of km, so
this is roughly 10-50x faster -- which is the stated design goal, not an
accident.

THE CAVEAT, AND IT IS A REAL DESIGN QUESTION
A median run of 1.4 km with p25 at 0.5 km means A QUARTER OF BIOME PATCHES ARE
UNDER 500 m ACROSS. That can read two ways in game: alive, or SPECKLED, with
biomes flickering as the player walks. The distribution is heavily skewed
(median 1.4 km against p95 19.9 km), so both tiny patches and large coherent
regions exist; which dominates the FEEL is a visual judgement and not
answerable from these numbers.

If it reads as speckled, the fix is CLIENT-SIDE -- biome hysteresis, or a
minimum-patch filter in classifyBiome's consumer -- not a regeneration. The
tiles carry climate; the classifier decides patches.
