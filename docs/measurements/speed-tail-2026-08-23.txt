================================================================================
THE FRAME TAIL AT SPEED IS THE ANCHOR RECOMPUTE, AND IT IS ADMIT/TRUNCATE CHURN
2026-08-23 -- read from Saved/st-all.log, Saved/spd60.log, Saved/spd120.log.
No new legs run; every number is quoted from an instrument already in the tree,
with the line that produced it named.
================================================================================

THE DEFECT: p95 frame time nearly triples from 30 to 120 m/s while throughput
falls.  p50 is flat, so it is entirely a tail problem.

    speed     p50      p95      hitches   frames
    30 m/s    9.71    17.06        351    24,826   (st-all.log)
    60 m/s   11.16    38.94      1,579    16,405   (spd60.log)
   120 m/s    9.94    47.36      1,892    17,090   (spd120.log)

"VoxelPerfRun complete", one binary, one flight (`line`, spawn -61440,-61440),
varying only -VoxelPerfSpeed.  (st-all additionally carries
-VoxelGpuWorklistAssetStamp=1, which the spd legs drop.  Noted so nobody
reports the 30 m/s arm as switch-identical: it is not.  It does not affect any
conclusion below, all of which rest on within-leg ratios.)

THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE: the streaming recompute costs the same per call at
every speed and is CALLED four times as often at speed, and what it spends
that time on at speed is admitting half a million records per two seconds and
throwing 498,025 of them straight back out again.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. IT IS NOT THE RENDER THREAD.  IT IS THE SUBSYSTEM TICK.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first thing to rule out, because if it were true none of the rest would
matter.  Aggregated over every "Hitch frame:" line in each leg (mean; the
attribution counts each hitch once):

              subsystemTickMs   renderWaitMs   hitches whose frame is
    speed        mean   max        mean        >50% subsystem tick
     30 m/s      4.26   69.5       37.4          1 of   351  (0.3%)
     60 m/s     19.33   91.5       33.5        697 of 1,579  (44%)
    120 m/s     32.77  775.1       43.3      1,021 of 1,892  (54%)

renderWaitMs is FLAT across the sweep.  subsystemTickMs on a hitch frame goes
up 7.7x.  At 30 m/s a hitch is essentially never a streaming hitch; at 120 m/s
the majority are.

And it is not dispatch and not apply -- those are flat:

                dispatchMs   applyMs   remeshMs   unloadMs
     30 m/s        0.62       2.49       0.00       0.40
    120 m/s        2.03       2.30       0.00       0.77

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. INSIDE THE TICK IT IS RecomputeDesiredSet, AND THE COST IS PER CALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Voxel recompute (max since last log)" carries calls=; "(sum since last log)"
carries the stage split.  Both aggregated over windows 50..100 (mid-flight;
zero-total windows dropped -- see tools/leg-summary.sh for why the last window
of a log is never read).  The log interval is 2 s, so a window is 2 s of wall.

    speed    calls/2s    ms per call    total ms/2s    share of wall
     30        12.5         25.91          324.7          16%
     60        54.7         22.07        1,207.6          60%
    120        47.8         29.90        1,428.9          71%

** ms PER CALL IS FLAT.  THE CALL COUNT IS WHAT EXPLODES. **

That single fact decides the shape of any fix.  The recompute's cost does not
scale with how far the anchor moved between calls -- it scales with the number
of calls.  So calling it less often is a straight saving, not a deferral of
work into a later, bigger call.

WHY the call count explodes: the trigger is a LEVEL-0 chunk crossing
(VoxelWorldSubsystem.cpp, `AnchorChunk.X != LastAnchorChunk.X || ...`).  A
level-0 chunk is 3.2 m.  At 120 m/s that is 37.5 crossings/s.  The 3.2 m
trigger was sized for walking speed; at flight speed it is a 37.5 Hz timer on
a 25-30 ms function.  (Measured 23.9 calls/s, not 37.5, because the recompute
is by then slow enough that several crossings collapse into one tick -- the
cadence is self-limiting only because it is already eating the frame.)

At 120 m/s the recompute occupies 71% of the game thread.  p50 stays at 9.94 ms
because roughly half the frames carry no recompute at all; p95 is 47.36 ms
because the ones that do carry a 25-30 ms blob (worst single call in the leg:
109.8 ms).

Stage split, ms per 2 s window, windows 50..100 (the parts sum to the total):

    stage           30 m/s    60 m/s    120 m/s    ratio 120/30
    entry R0          94.5     434.7      353.3        3.7x
    entry R1          41.8     147.1      286.4        6.9x
    entry R2          22.0      85.5      161.8        7.4x
    entry R3-R5       24.8      80.9      158.7        6.4x
    sort               1.3     154.7      249.8      192x
    exitScan         139.1     260.0      176.3        1.3x
    queueFilter        0.5      41.6       37.3       75x
    fine               0.4       1.4        1.3
    ---------------------------------------------------------
    TOTAL            324.7   1,207.6    1,428.9        4.4x

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT THE TIME IS ACTUALLY SPENT ON: ADMIT/TRUNCATE CHURN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Voxel admission (5s window)", aggregated over ALL 51 flight windows
(50..100), not a single window -- the churn is a steady state, not a spike:

    speed   mean cap   capSrc over 51 windows   mean drainEMA   mean dropped
     30      32,768    max x51                      7,820              0
     60      32,448    max x50, ema x1              7,528        206,479
    120      32,768    max x51                      6,203        470,376

dropped= is ZERO IN EVERY ONE of the 51 windows at 30 m/s and never below six
figures at 120.  This is a phase change, not a gradient.

`dropped=` is the counter this file's own comment (AdmissionCapMax, and again
at LeadHorizonSec) names as THE NUMBER THAT MUST BE WATCHED, with the failing
reading written out in advance: "dropped= balloons while dispatched/s stays
flat: truncate churn."  It balloons from ZERO to 498,025 per 2 s while
dispatched FALLS.  The predicted failure mode is the one that is happening.

"Voxel job flow (5s window)", same windows:

    speed   dispatched   drained    stale      recordsAdded  recordsEvicted
     30       15,734     15,461     0 (0.0%)        16,973        15,900
     60       17,529     12,539   443 (3.5%)       210,889        12,190
    120       12,659     12,661 3,637 (28.7%)      503,424        15,144

At 30 m/s recordsAdded (16,973) tracks dispatched (15,734): every record
admitted is a record used.  At 120 m/s the system admits 503,424 records per
2 s, dispatches 12,659 and evicts 15,144.  The other ~475,000 were added and
then removed again by truncation inside the same or the next call.  That is
~250,000 TMap insert/erase pairs PER SECOND on the game thread producing
nothing, and it is 10,532 admitted per recompute call against 265 dispatched
per call -- a 40:1 ratio.

DropFarthestOverCap's own comment states the design intent it is missing by
that 40x: "the band admits only chunks that are clearly nearer, sized so a
call's admissions are on the order of what a call's dispatches drain, which is
the whole point of the exercise: make production track drain."

Stale results are the same waste seen from the other end: 28.7% of completed
mesh jobs at 120 m/s are discarded because the chunk was un-admitted before
the result landed.  0.0% at 30 m/s.

WHY THE PHASE CHANGE.  Queue depth ("pendingJob=" on the max line):

    speed    pendingJob    cap     tracked    entry scans/2s  R0/R1/R2/R3/R4/R5
     30        1,436     32,768   250,692    12.5  6.3 3.1 1.6 0.8 0.4
     60       29,389     32,768   109,385    54.7 18.8 9.5 4.7 2.4 1.2
    120       32,336     32,768    60,441    47.8 34.5 18.8 9.5 4.8 2.4

At 30 m/s the queue holds 1,436 against a 32,768 cap: subcritical, truncation
never fires (dropped=0), no cutoff (cutoffM=-1), no rejections.  At 60 m/s and
above the queue PINS at the cap, the cutoff collapses to 67 m, and every one
of the 24 recomputes per second re-keys and re-sorts all 32,336 entries and
then truncates them.  That is the sort's 192x: 1,436 entries x 12.5 calls
versus 32,336 x 47.8 calls, n log n, which predicts ~123x against 192x
measured -- same mechanism, same order.

And the sorted output is almost entirely unused: dispatch pops ~265 entries
between two sorts.  0.8% of a 32,336-entry sort is ever read before the whole
array is re-keyed and re-sorted from scratch.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE CAP CEILING IS DECIDING EVERYTHING, AND THE SWEEP THAT WAS RUN COULD NOT
   HAVE SEEN IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`capSrc=max` in every window at every speed.  The drain-sized cap
(-VoxelAdmissionCapDrainSec=8, drainEMA ~6,425/s -> wanted ~51,400) is clamped
to the 32,768 ceiling and never binds.  The cap in force is the CEILING, at
every rung of the drainSec sweep that was run.

worker-tap-2026-08-23.txt already records the confound -- "at drainSec>=8 the
EMA crosses the 32,768 ceiling and every rung runs the same effective cap" --
and the drainSec sweep that was run was 8 / 16 / 32.  All three are above the
crossing.  ** THE SWEEP WAS THREE IDENTICAL ARMS. **  It read
2,841-2,842 chunks/s "identical to the digit" because it WAS the same arm three
times.  The only untested direction is DOWN, and down is the direction this
churn analysis points.

AND IT IS WORSE THAN THAT.  Every -VoxelAdmissionCapDrainSec value ever put on
a command line, across every log in Saved/:

    drainSec=4  : 6 legs   (b3b-off/on, cut-drain, p3-a/b/c)
    drainSec=8  : 23 legs
    drainSec=16 : 1 leg
    drainSec=32 : 1 leg

All six drainSec=4 legs carry NO -VoxelPerfSpeed, i.e. they ran at 30 m/s --
the speed at which dropped= is zero in every window and the cap never binds at
all.  They are also from a build predating the instrument: their admission
line has no drainEMA=/capSrc= columns and reads cap=2048 throughout, the
static floor.  So the drain-sized cap has NEVER been swept at speed, on any
build that could report which bound decided.  The one arm that could have
distinguished the ceiling from the controller was run six times, at the one
speed where neither is in force.

7. WHAT TO RUN NEXT, CHEAPEST FIRST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All at -VoxelPerfSpeed=120, against spd120.log as control.  Judge on per-ring
residency, never aggregate throughput.

READ THESE FOUR ON EVERY ARM, because the gate is all four and not just p95:
  * p50 and p95        -- "VoxelPerfRun complete" (p50 must NOT rise)
  * per-ring residency -- the per-ring resident counts, never the aggregate;
                          level-primary ordering once read "R3/R4 at 0 loaded
                          chunks for 90 s" while the aggregate looked healthy
  * holes / uncovered  -- tools/read-holes.sh
  * time-to-settle     -- "Voxel cold settle: SETTLED t=..s ..." (the probe
                          that landed on main 2026-08-23; see
                          docs/measurements/cold-fill-diagnosis-2026-08-23.txt).
                          Every experiment below changes admission, and (1) in
                          particular changes how much the cold fill may have
                          in flight, so settle is the reading most likely to
                          regress quietly while p95 improves.

(1) NO CODE AT ALL -- shrink the cap.  -VoxelAdmissionCapDrainSec=1 and =2.
    At the measured drainEMA of 6,203/s those give Wanted = 6,203 and 12,406,
    both inside [StaticCap 2048, ceiling 32,768], so capSrc= must read `ema`
    and not `max`.  ** capSrc=max on either rung means the arm did not take
    and the leg measured the control twice ** -- check it before reading
    anything else.  This is the untested half of section 4's sweep.
      PREDICTED: dropped= collapses toward 0, pendingJob falls ~5x, sortMs
      falls ~10x, stale% falls from 28.7%, recompute share falls from 71%,
      p95 falls.
      FAILING READING that says the cap was already right: per-ring residency
      loses R3/R4/R5 (they are the rings a smaller shared budget starves
      first -- this is exactly the failure the ring quota exists for, so read
      per-ring, and read `holes`/`uncovered` with tools/read-holes.sh), or
      dispatched/s falls with dropped= unchanged.

(2) NO CODE -- arm -VoxelIncrementalAdmission (section 6).  Read
    "Voxel incremental admission": incr R# close to the `scans` field is the
    fast path carrying traffic.
      FAILING READING: incr ~0, or incr high with entryMs unmoved -- the
      switch is on and doing nothing.

(3) The rate bound authored alongside this note (-VoxelRecomputeDutyPct=25),
    which attacks the call COUNT rather than the per-call cost, and is
    therefore orthogonal to (1) and (2).  Its own readings are on the
    accessor's comment, including the magnitude it can and cannot deliver:
    p95 47 -> high twenties, NOT to the 17 ms of the 30 m/s arm, because p95
    is a percentile over FRAMES and 8.4 calls/s is still ~9% of them.  Do not
    read a partial result as a dead feature; read defer= first.

    NOTE ON COMPOSING (1) AND (3): (1) makes each call cheaper, and the rate
    bound is expressed as a duty cycle, so a cheaper call automatically buys
    MORE calls at the same duty.  The two do not fight, and (1)+(3) together
    is the arm that should clear the gate -- but run them apart first, because
    a duty bound sitting on top of an already-cheap recompute may never engage
    (defer=0) and would then be carried forward as "measured, works" when it
    was never tested.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. WHAT THIS NOTE DOES NOT CLAIM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* It does not explain why the producer is too slow to keep up at 60 m/s in the
  first place.  It explains what the CONSEQUENCE of falling behind costs on
  the game thread, which is a positive feedback loop, not the root.
* exitScanMs is the one stage that barely moves (139 -> 176 ms/window) and
  that is because `tracked` FALLS with speed (250,692 -> 60,441).  Per call it
  goes 11.1 ms -> 3.7 ms.  It is O(tracked) and behaving exactly as designed;
  it is not a speed defect.
* The 30 m/s arm carries one extra switch (section head).  Every conclusion
  here is a within-leg ratio or a stage split that closes against its own
  total, so no conclusion rests on that difference -- but a re-run of the
  30 m/s arm on the exact spd command line would remove the caveat for free.

9. 240 m/s CANNOT CURRENTLY BE MEASURED AT ALL -- ALL SIX LEGS DIED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 240 m/s row of tonight's table has a blank p95 and this is why.  Every leg
run at -VoxelPerfSpeed=240 crashed before "VoxelPerfRun complete":

    spd240.log  arena-240.log  wide-on-240.log  wide-ctl-240.log
    lead-off-240.log  lead-on-240.log

All six on the SAME fatal, VoxelFineTileStreamer.cpp:944:

    FINE TIER GATE LEAK (fatal, unattended run): elevation query at fine pixel
    (-16384,-35200) needs tile (-2,-5), which is not resident and could not be
    loaded ... The streaming anchor is in tile (-3,-4), so this query is
    1 tile(s) (~15 km) away from the player.

This is NOT a streaming defect and NOT something this note's fixes address.
The gate is working exactly as designed: at 240 m/s the `line` flight runs off
the edge of the baked fine-tile coverage, and the gate refuses to answer with
sea level rather than produce an unreproducible artifact.  The whole 240 m/s
column of any sweep is therefore vacuous until one of:

  * bake the missing tile -- tools/bake_tiles_from_cache.py --tiles="-2,-5"
    (the gate message names it, and names the seed and namespace it checked);
  * -VoxelFineTileGateFatal=0 to downgrade it to an Error -- but then the leg
    is reading sea level over the unbaked ground and its terrain-shaped numbers
    (holes, residency, dispatched) are measuring a different world than the
    30/60/120 arms.  Use it only for frame-time-only questions, and say so;
  * shorten the flight or move the spawn so 240 m/s stays inside coverage.

RECORDED SO IT IS NOT RE-DISCOVERED: a 240 m/s leg that "produced no p95" has
not measured a slow arm.  It crashed at ~2 minutes.  Check the tail of the log
for `FINE TIER GATE LEAK` before drawing any conclusion from a missing number.
