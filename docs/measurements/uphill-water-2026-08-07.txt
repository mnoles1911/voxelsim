WATER THAT CLIMBS: CONFIRMED, LOCATED, AND ONE CANDIDATE FIX PRICED
2026-08-07, wet alpine block tile (-4,-4), bake_ver 14 and 15.

THE REPORT
----------
The owner, flying the magenta marker at bake_ver 15: "possible I see a spot or
two where the magenta blocks actually seem to flow slightly up hill when they
should be descending down path of least resistance in a mountain river valleys."

CONFIRMED, AND IT IS NOT NEW
----------------------------
vxc_riverribbonprobe, along ORIENTED reaches, surface = reconstructed ground +
baked depth (ground #2, resolved through RiverSampler):

    bake_ver 14   46,726 downstream steps   8,469 RISE (18.12%)   3,437 > 1 voxel
                  p50 33 mm   p90 527 mm   max 4,775 mm
    bake_ver 15   81,983 downstream steps  11,142 RISE (13.59%)   4,479 > 1 voxel
                  p50 40 mm   p90 627 mm   max 3,996 mm

bake_ver 15 is better as a FRACTION and worse in absolute count, which is what
more drawn water should do. p90 627 mm against a 100 mm wire LSB: this is
metre-scale, visible, and not quantisation.

IT IS NOT A TRACING ARTEFACT
-----------------------------
Split by whether the two points are 8-CONNECTED (a genuine channel walk) or
farther apart (the tracer stitching a confluence, or a centreline hopping
between two adjacent water bodies):

    adjacent steps    81,983  = 100.0% of all steps
    of which RISE     11,142  = every single rise

There are no non-adjacent steps at all, so tracing explains NONE of it.

WHY THE INVARIANT DID NOT CATCH IT
-----------------------------------
`enforce_descent` guarantees a non-increasing surface along the D8 RECEIVER
FOREST, and it does. Two pixels can sit side by side on the drawn channel and
drain to DIFFERENT receivers -- the sweep never compares them to each other.
`fill_to_local_surface` then hands each cell the level of the reach IT drains
into, so neighbours legitimately inherit different levels and the seam between
them is a step the eye walks straight over.

The guarantee is real. It just does not cover the pairs a player traverses.

A CANDIDATE FIX, PROTOTYPED OFFLINE AND NOT SHIPPED
----------------------------------------------------
Rule A -- "water finds its level": adjacent wet cells whose water is connected
are one body, take the lower, iterate.

    rises      415,005 -> 8,405   (-98.0%)
    wet cells  unchanged
    COST       surface lowered on 363,843 of 364,354 cells, p50 925 mm

REJECTED. "Water finds its level" is true of STANDING water and false of a
river: a flowing channel has a gradient by definition, so min-propagation is
transitive and chains the global minimum up the whole network. It flattens the
long profile. The measurement is what caught it -- the rule looked right.

Rule B -- non-increasing DOWNSTREAM: for a cell with an upstream wet neighbour
(one standing on higher ground), the surface may not exceed that neighbour's.
Water cannot be higher than the water feeding it. Only ever lowers.

    rises      415,005 -> 22      (-100.0%)
    wet cells  unchanged (+0.00%)
    COST       surface lowered on 219,410 cells (60% of wet), p50 547 mm,
               p90 1,643 mm, max 5,405 mm

NOT SHIPPED, for two reasons that are the honest state rather than caution:

  1. IT DID NOT CONVERGE. The relaxation hit its 60-iteration cap at 59, so the
     figures above are an INCOMPLETE relaxation and the true cost is unbounded
     so far. A fix whose price is still moving is not priced.
  2. The prototype uses `elevation_cp` -- the CONTROL LATTICE -- as ground, to
     decide which neighbour is upstream. The lattice stands up to 5.6 m off the
     surface it interpolates, which is the exact proxy error this file criticises
     in its own first paragraph. Before/after under one consistent definition is
     still informative about the DELTA; the absolute cost is not trustworthy.

That 60% of cells violate "not higher than your feeder" by a median half metre
is itself a finding: the level assignment is not merely seamed at rare
junctions, it is systematically inconsistent between neighbours. That points at
the composition -- per-reach levels stitched together -- rather than at a bug in
any one rule, and it is the same thing water-system-architecture §11b already
names as the intended fix: ONE surface field from ONE solve, instead of two
authorities composed at runtime with nothing forcing them to agree.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT
--------------------
  * re-run Rule B to true convergence with a higher cap, and on the
    RECONSTRUCTED surface rather than the lattice;
  * check the long profile before/after with river_long_profile.py, since the
    failure mode of any lowering rule is a flattened profile and the rise count
    alone cannot see that;
  * only then decide whether it belongs in the bake behind a constant, or
    whether it is a patch over a composition that should be replaced.
