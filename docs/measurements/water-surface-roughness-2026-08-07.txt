THE RESULT: the ground under the rivers is smooth to within a millimetre --
ZERO percent of neighbouring cells differ by even one voxel. The water surface
laid on that ground jumps more than THREE voxels between neighbours a sixth of
the time. The owner's "manmade magenta staircase, not a natural water flow" is
the WATER surface, not the terrain. Every fix so far has adjusted depth, and
|d SURFACE| tracks |d depth| almost exactly (22 vs 20, 496 vs 500) because the
ground contributes nothing: surface = ground + depth, and ground is flat here,
so the surface simply inherits every discontinuity the depth law produces.

WHY DEPTH IS DISCONTINUOUS: depth is assigned per cell from a local law
(Q, slope) and neighbouring cells get materially different values. The
construction is backwards. A river surface is smooth and monotone by physics
and the BED is what varies; deriving surface = ground + f(local Q) inverts that
and hands the surface the bed's noise plus the law's.

RETRACTED IN THE SAME SESSION -- two metrics I reported that do not hold up:

1. "62% of downhill wet steps rise" (uphill_check.py, all three bake versions).
   Real number, misleading quantity. It recomputes steepest descent on the
   shipped ground, and on these reaches the ground drop is p50 4 mm -- ONE
   quantisation step. The descent direction is therefore chosen by rounding
   noise among near-ties, so "downhill" is close to a random direction and a
   surface that varies +-270 mm rises about half the time by construction.
   61.66% is consistent with noise. Do not read it as "62% of the river flows
   uphill". The >1 m tail (9,860 cells) is still meaningful; the headline is not.

2. The downstream/upstream split of those rises (80.6% rising downstream vs
   24.5% upstream, which looked like a physics inversion). It assumed the low 5
   bits of `flow` are log2 discharge. They are not clean discharge:
   corr(q, ground) = +0.108 over channel cells (a discharge field should be
   NEGATIVE -- big rivers sit low), and depth is non-monotone in q
   (q=6 -> 1260mm, q=18 -> 720mm, q=24 -> 2330mm). Discarded. Identify the
   field before splitting anything by it again.

What does survive from that dead end, because it does not depend on direction:
on the steps examined, ground drop was p50 4 mm while depth changed p50 270 mm,
and depth change exceeded ground drop in 100.0% of cases. The height comes from
the depth field, not from the terrain.
