THE STRAIGHT-LINE RIDGES: THREE ABLATIONS, THREE NEGATIVES, CAUSE STILL UNKNOWN
==============================================================================
2026-07-30, worldgen v15. Frame under test: g35 (tile -5,2, p50 grade 35.0%,
complete 3x3 coarse ring, real climate), the frame where the owner called the
artifact clearest: "a clear pattern of jump ridges in a straight line", with the
spacing on other frames estimated as "every metre or two".

HYPOTHESES TESTED AND KILLED

  arm B  fine-table 1600 mm microrelief octave, 500 -> 50 mm amplitude.
         Rationale: plain value noise on an AXIS-ALIGNED world lattice at exactly
         1.6 m, which should read as straight lines along world x/y -- the best
         match for the word "straight".
         RESULT: pattern unchanged. Not the cause.

  arm A  rill/flute term, kRillAmplitudeMm 300 -> 30.
         Rationale: also 1.6 m across-slope (kRillAcrossMm), and on a UNIFORM
         slope a gradient-aligned term produces straight parallel lines down the
         fall line, so "straight" does not exonerate it.
         RESULT: pattern unchanged. Not the cause.

  arm C  drop the fine tier entirely; same site on the 30 m coarse tier.
         Rationale: the fine raster's own 1.875 m pitch, with control points
         quantised to a 100 mm LSB and a measured reconstruction error up to
         50 mm, is half a voxel of ripple at exactly the pixel spacing and
         axis-aligned.
         RESULT: pattern still present, if anything more pronounced. Not the
         fine tier.

AND THE FALLBACK EXPLANATION IS ALSO DEAD. Contour terracing -- the geometric
consequence of voxelising any smooth slope -- would show up as terrace runs of the
reported length. Measured at g35:

  voxel terrace runs: count=2138  mean=2.39 voxels (0.24 m)  median=2  p90=4  max=19
  (local grade in the probe window 81.2%)

0.24 m is an order of magnitude short of "a metre or two", so the visible ridges
are not contour steps at that spacing.

WHAT I GOT WRONG, twice, and why it is recorded. When the frames were sent I named
the rill term as the suspect, on the strength of chevrons I thought I saw. On the
owner's word "straight line" I switched to the 1600 mm axis-aligned octave. Both
were guesses from the picture, and both are now falsified by ablation. Three
one-at-a-time term ablations have cost more than a direct measurement would have.

THE NEXT STEP IS TO MEASURE THE PERIODICITY, NOT TO GUESS ANOTHER TERM. What is
needed is the wavelength AND orientation of the artifact, from the height field
itself -- a 2-D autocorrelation or power spectrum of the amplified surface over a
window where the artifact is visible, reported as peak wavelength and peak
direction relative to world x/y and to the local fall line. That distinguishes
in ONE measurement between:
  * an axis-aligned lattice term (peak along world x/y),
  * a fall-line-aligned term (peak rotates with local aspect),
  * a raster-pitch artifact (peak at 1.875 m or 30 m exactly), and
  * quantisation (peak at a wavelength that scales with 1/local slope).
terrainprobe already computes directional roughness and a structure function, so
this is an addition to an existing instrument rather than a new one.

RESOLVED ALONG THE WAY: THE BLUE SPECKS ARE WATER. In the arm-B frame they form a
CONNECTED LINEAR CHAIN following a valley line rather than scattered specks -- a
river, rendered thin and discontinuous. That retires the MAT_PERMAFROST /
MAT_SNOW cold-biome-flip hypothesis. The remaining question is why it renders
discontinuously, which is a water/rendering question and not a terrain-shape one.

TREE STATE: all three arms reverted. kWorldGenVersion 15, kRillAmplitudeMm 300,
both tables' 1600 mm octave at 500. Nothing from this investigation ships.
