IT ALSO FIXED THE GPU POOL, which was filed separately as its own defect and
deliberately NOT worked on first. Same site, same view, alpine fine tier:

                        v13              v14
  quad high-water       79,999,988       52,008,094
  live chunks           71,541           51,947
  pool capacity         93.9%            65.0%
  free runs             18,217           1
  alloc failures        7,290            0
  alloc-failed quads    12,846,450       0
  CHUNKS LEFT UNDRAWN   7,178            0

Those 7,178 undrawn chunks are the black gaps in the v13 fine-tier capture. The
reasoning that deferred this was: the micro-texture is what emits the quads, so
sizing the pool before capping the gradient would size it for a count about to
drop by a third. That held -- a 35% cut in quads took the pool from 18,217
fragments and 7,178 dropped chunks to a single free run and none.

Recorded because the opposite call was tempting and would have wasted the work,
and because an earlier reading of this same defect was WRONG in the other
direction: from alpine's `largest run 642` the conclusion drawn was "fragmented,
wants compaction". It wanted fewer quads.

WHAT v14 DOES NOT BUY, and the picture says it plainly.

An in-engine capture at alpine on the fine tier at v14 shows a coherent slope
with organised contour terracing and visible channel structure, where v13 showed
per-voxel static. Structurally that is the fix. Aesthetically it trades one
artifact for another: voxel-scale dithering for CONTOUR RIBBONS. The mechanism is
arithmetic -- on 40% ground the cap allows a detail gradient of 0.5 x 0.4 = 0.2,
so the 200 mm octave gets ~40 mm of amplitude, which is sub-voxel and therefore
cannot dither a step edge.

NOTE THE TWO ALPINE FINE CAPTURES ARE NOT A LIKE-FOR-LIKE BEFORE AND AFTER: v13's
was taken at pitch -3.0 (a vista) and v14's at pitch -40.0 (looking down). They
show different things about the same world and must not be presented as one
comparison.

THE PRINCIPLED WAY OUT is already in the plan and is not more amplitude. An
ISOTROPIC detail term cannot add visible texture without risking a reversed
downhill direction, which is exactly what the cap now forbids. An ANISOTROPIC one
can: a rill/flute term whose domain is scaled along the local gradient frame has
high ACROSS-slope gradient and low ALONG-slope gradient, so it is visible without
closing basins. The plan specifies precisely that (~1.6 m across, ~13 m along, its
gate zeroed below ~10% grade). Under v14's cap that term is the right place to
spend the remaining texture budget, and it should be measured against the same
drainage ladder rather than by eye.

STILL OPEN: the 20-25% transition band (moderate 83.4%, third 63.4% coarse) where
the relief ramp only partially engages; the fine-plains drainage-vs-terracing
trade (terrace runs 0.60 -> 1.95 m) which has no column-local discriminator
because the bake supplies no decimetre texture there; alpine's ridge deficit,
unrelated to this and needing a steep-ground process term; and NVIDIA parity,
which remains owed -- every parity claim here is AMD RX 7800 XT only.
