Those 7,178 undrawn chunks are the black gaps in the v13 fine-tier capture. The
reasoning that deferred this was: the micro-texture is what emits the quads, so
sizing the pool before capping the gradient would size it for a count about to
drop by a third. That held -- a 35% cut in quads took the pool from 18,217
fragments and 7,178 dropped chunks to a single free run and none.

Recorded because the opposite call was tempting and would have wasted the work,
and because an earlier reading of this same defect was WRONG in the other
direction: from alpine's `largest run 642` the conclusion drawn was "fragmented,
wants compaction". It wanted fewer quads.

WHAT v14 DOES NOT BUY, and the picture says it plainly.

An in-engine capture at alpine on the fine tier at v14 shows a coherent slope
with organised contour terracing and visible channel structure, where v13 showed
per-voxel static. Structurally that is the fix. Aesthetically it trades one
artifact for another: voxel-scale dithering for CONTOUR RIBBONS. The mechanism is
arithmetic -- on 40% ground the cap allows a detail gradient of 0.5 x 0.4 = 0.2,
so the 200 mm octave gets ~40 mm of amplitude, which is sub-voxel and therefore
cannot dither a step edge.

NOTE THE TWO ALPINE FINE CAPTURES ARE NOT A LIKE-FOR-LIKE BEFORE AND AFTER: v13's
was taken at pitch -3.0 (a vista) and v14's at pitch -40.0 (looking down). They
show different things about the same world and must not be presented as one
comparison.

THE WAY OUT I PROPOSED HERE IS FALSIFIED. Measured, not argued, the same day.

The claim was: an ISOTROPIC detail term cannot add texture without risking a
reversed downhill, but the plan's ANISOTROPIC rill/flute term can, because it is
warped 8:1 along the fall line (kRillAcrossMm 1600, kRillAlongMm 12800) so its
gradient is almost entirely ACROSS the slope where it cannot close a basin. That
reasoning predicted the rill term was free texture under the cap.

It is not. Three arms, alpine fine tier, carrier 0 sinks / 0.0% / 216.6 m:

  arm                                       sinks  stranded  path
  v14 as shipped (rill inside capped sum)       0      0.0%   218.8 m
  rill exempt from the cap, not budgeted       12      9.2%   185.9 m
  rill given first claim on the budget          2      7.0%   187.6 m

Giving the rill its full amplitude costs 7-9% of the tile's drainage however the
accounting is arranged. The anisotropy reduces the damage per unit of visible
texture -- 7.0% with the octaves absorbing the reduction is better than 9.2%
without -- but it does not remove it. At 300 mm over a 1.6 m across-slope
wavelength, doubled by the relief gate, the rill's own gradient is ~0.375 against
an alpine allowance of ~0.2, and the octagonal-norm frame carries up to 12%
direction error, so a real fraction of that lands along-slope.

ONE REAL BUG WAS FOUND ON THE WAY and is worth fixing on its own terms, separately
from any texture ambition. detailGradMmPerM sums only the three OCTAVE bands, yet
the scale it produces is applied to a detailMm that already includes the rill and
bedding terms. So the post-cap gradient is (octaves + rill) * allowed/octaves --
ABOVE the allowance by the rill's share -- while the comment above the block
asserts the estimate and the thing it estimates cannot drift apart. The guarantee
leaks in the unsafe direction. It does not currently show up as stranded area
because scaling the rill down along with the octaves happens to compensate, i.e.
the code is right by accident. Either count the additive terms in the budget or
say in the comment that they are outside it; do not leave the assertion false.

CONCLUSION FOR THE TEXTURE QUESTION: under a drainage constraint there is no
cheap isotropic OR anisotropic client-side term. Sub-voxel-dithering texture and
preserved drainage are in genuine tension at 10 cm voxels, and the remaining
budget is small. The honest next places to look are not amplitude at all: surface
MATERIAL variation and shading, which cost no geometry and therefore no drainage,
and the bake supplying decimetre structure that the client can then follow rather
than invent.

STILL OPEN: the 20-25% transition band (moderate 83.4%, third 63.4% coarse) where
the relief ramp only partially engages; the fine-plains drainage-vs-terracing
trade (terrace runs 0.60 -> 1.95 m) which has no column-local discriminator
because the bake supplies no decimetre texture there; alpine's ridge deficit,
unrelated to this and needing a steep-ground process term; and NVIDIA parity,
