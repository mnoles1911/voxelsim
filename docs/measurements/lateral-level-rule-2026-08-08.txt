THE LATERAL EQUAL-LEVEL RULE -- why water climbed one bank while the other side
stood dry. Tile (-4,-4), npz-lvl2 replayed offline, 2048 px window on the
wettest region: 63,466 wet cells, 214,901 adjacent wet pairs. 2026-08-08.

THE OWNER, in game: "on one side of the river, there are magenta blocks climbing
up and over the bank and hill - on the other side of the river water there is
open air and space that the river should fill to level the basin."

WHY NO EXISTING RULE CAUGHT IT. Every monotonicity rule in this module compares
a cell to its UPSTREAM neighbour along the D8 receiver forest.
`enforce_upstream_monotone` measures ZERO violations on real tiles and it is
telling the truth -- verified with a planted violation (before=1, after=0). But
two cells lying SIDE BY SIDE that drain to DIFFERENT receivers are never
compared by any of them. Each is perfectly monotone along its own chain while
standing at a different height a few metres across the same channel.

MEASURED, adjacent wet pairs, |difference in water surface|:
  p50 16.8   p90 189.7   p99 667.7   max 2,642 mm
  over 100 mm (one voxel): 44,622 = 20.76%
  over 300 mm            : 10,860 =  5.05%

ATTRIBUTION of those 44,622 steps:
  along-flow (one is the other's receiver)      38.6%
  same receiver, not along-flow                  9.0%
  DIFFERENT RECEIVERS                           52.4%
So 61.4% of every visible step is between cells no D8-forest rule compares.
By membership: 4.2% channel-channel, 14.4% channel-floodplain,
81.4% FLOODPLAIN-FLOODPLAIN -- the collar, not the river.

For scale: the along-flow drop between adjacent DRAWN cells is p50 14.5 mm, one
seventh of a voxel. The legitimate river gradient is far below the artifact,
which is what makes a one-voxel tolerance safe.

THE RULE. For every 8-connected adjacent wet pair, S[a] <= S[b] + 100 mm (the
wire LSB), enforced by LOWERING ONLY, 8 sweeps, EXCEPT along-channel pairs --
both cells drawn AND one is the other's receiver.

TWO FINDINGS THAT SHAPED IT:
 * A BARRIER BETWEEN TWO ADJACENT WET CELLS IS IMPOSSIBLE. The sill between
   neighbours is max(z[a],z[b]) and the higher water always clears it. So the
   barrier idea applies to DRY ground, and the rule gets it for free by only
   ever comparing two already-wet cells: it never crosses dry ground, so it
   cannot join pools the terrain keeps apart.
 * THE EXEMPTION MUST BE THE DRAWN CHANNEL, NOT THE FOREST. A perched bank
   cell's receiver IS the river beside it, so exempting all along-flow pairs
   protects the very defect being fixed: 3.91% residual against 0.87%, and
   9,548 dry-below against 8,367.

EFFECT (8 sweeps, tol one voxel):
  wet                63,466 -> 54,252   (-14.5%)
  steps over 100 mm   20.76% -> 0.87%
  steps over 300 mm    5.05% -> 0.21%
  p99 step            668 mm -> 100 mm
  dry-below-by-a-voxel 9,227 -> 8,367   (-9.3%)
  long profile        p50 14.5 -> 14.9 mm, p90 399 -> 255 mm
It never raises a level (asserted before return) and never wets a dry cell, so
the ceiling guarantee and the 209x flood bound both hold structurally rather
than by tuning.

CAVEATS, recorded rather than smoothed over:
 * Receivers are NOT in the npz -- `flow` is the packed accumulation/flag plane,
   not a direction. The attribution above recomputes a D8 forest on the shipped
   post-B5 elevation while the bake routes on the PRE-B5 surface. Direction and
   magnitude are not in doubt; the exact 61.4% could move a few points.
 * The 8-sweep bound is LIVE, not slack: 32 sweeps takes the residual from 1,580
   to 606 pairs for 0.6% fewer wet cells. 8 matches settle_to_adjacent_level's
   documented 15 m reach. A real trade, not a formality.
 * -14.5% wet is the owner's call. This rule only ever removes water, and he has
   said more water looked better.
 * Not yet measured end-to-end on a real bake -- everything above is the shipped
   plane replayed offline.
