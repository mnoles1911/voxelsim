WHAT WAS WRONG, in the owner's words after flying bake_ver 13:

    "the water still does not behave correctly especially on steeper down
    slopes. on a steeper downslope in the river, there will be several cubes of
    water placed in a general direction but disconnected going down the slope.
    it does not look like flowing water going down hill and there is obvious
    empty space and air all around the placed water."

"Several cubes" is not a figure of speech and "disconnected" is not a matter of
taste. Both are measurable and both were measured before anything was changed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE HYPOTHESIS THAT WAS ON THE TABLE, AND WHY IT IS ONLY HALF OF IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The stated suspicion was that `fill_to_local_surface` floods each cell to a
HORIZONTAL level -- right for ponded water, wrong for flowing water on a
gradient -- so that where the bed drops faster than the water is deep,
consecutive columns do not touch.

That mechanism is real and it is quantified in section 3. It is NOT the larger
one. The larger one is pure rasterisation and no amount of depth fixes it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. HOW THE CLIENT ACTUALLY DRAWS WATER, which decides what "touching" means
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Read off `voxel-core/include/voxelcore/lakes.h`, not assumed:

    RiverSampler::waterSurfaceMmAtVoxel(vx, vy)
        px = floorDiv(vx * kVoxelSizeMm, pxMm)      <- NEAREST fine pixel
        surfaceMm = reconstructedGroundMm(px, py) + depth * kWaterDepthLsbMm

    implicitWaterFill(vz, groundMm, surfaceMm)
        wet iff  vz*100 >= groundMm  and  vz*100 < surfaceMm

The lookup is NEAREST, so the water surface is constant across all 18.75 voxels
of a 1.875 m pixel. **What the client draws is one flat slab per fine pixel**,
spanning [ground, ground + depth]. Two consequences:

  (a) two plan-adjacent pixels share a VOXEL FACE iff their slabs overlap:

          surface(lower) > ground(upper)   <=>   depth(lower) > bed drop

  (b) a DIAGONAL neighbour shares an EDGE, not a face. Two slabs touching at a
      corner are two objects with air between them.

Every existing corridor instrument -- `measure_corridor_fragmentation`,
`river_break_probe`, `corridor_water_report` -- labels the WET MASK, 8-connected.
A mask cannot see either (a) or (b). That is why bv13 measured as a connected
river and flew as a row of cubes.

The new probe is `terrain-service/tools/river_column_contact.py` and it labels
the wet set under VOXEL 6-CONNECTIVITY, reporting the plan-8 number beside it so
the two can never be quoted apart.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`water.bridge_to_face_contact`, run after whichever `water_extent_mode` ran.
Two local steps, no iteration, no threshold:

    CORNER   for every diagonally-adjacent wet pair with neither corner wet,
             wet the LOWER-bedded corner at the HIGHER of the two surfaces,
             provided its ground stands at least min_depth below that -- the
             same submergence test the fill itself uses, applied to the corner
             the flow passes through.

    BRIDGE   raise every wet cell's surface to at least
             max over face-adjacent wet u of (ground(u) + min_depth).

WHY THIS IS THE BLEND BETWEEN THE TWO REGIMES AND NOT A SWITCH BETWEEN THEM.
In genuinely ponded water the BRIDGE is exactly a no-op, by construction rather
than by tuning: every cell in a pool shares one level and each is submerged, so
`surface >= ground(u) + min_depth` already holds for every wet neighbour and the
max finds nothing to raise. The raise is `max(0, drop - depth)`, which is zero on
level water and grows continuously with bed slope. So the surface is HORIZONTAL
where the water ponds and BED-PARALLEL where it runs, the transition is the
terrain's own, and there is no slope constant anywhere that could put a seam
along the river. `test_face_contact_bridge_is_exactly_a_no_op_on_ponded_water`
asserts it on a tilted bed under a flat pool, byte for byte, rather than taking
the argument's word.

WHY IT CANNOT FLOOD. `ground(u) + min_depth <= surface(u)` for every wet u, so a
raised surface never exceeds the surface of a cell one pixel away that was
already drawn at it. The maximum of the water surface over the plane does not
move at all, and every raised cell has a cell it touches standing at least as
high. Both are asserted.

WHY ONE PASS IS THE FIXED POINT. The bridge reads BEDS only, never the surfaces
it writes. There is no ordering dependence and nothing to iterate.

WHERE IT STOPS. A diagonal whose two corners both stand above the water is
refused rather than bridged -- wetting one would put water on a rise and the
bridge would then drag the reach up to meet it. Counted as
`bridge_corner_refused`.

100 m and 250 m and reads 0 rises across 30,577 m. It needs `--diagnostic` npz
dumps, and a `--diagnostic` bake writes no .vxtl, so it cannot be run against

the SAME path evaluated in both namespaces:

    (-11,-6)     100 m        0            0            0 mm
                 250 m        0            0            0 mm
    (-11,-5)     100 m        2            2           31 mm
                 250 m        0            0            0 mm
    (-12,-5)     100 m        4            4           61 mm
                 250 m        1            1           22 mm
    (-11,-4)     100 m        0            0            0 mm
                 250 m        0            0            0 mm

**Identical on every tile at both scales.** The bridge added no reversal that
the shipped gate would see. On (-11,-4) the same path carried 22 DRY cells in
bv13 and 0 in bv14, and its total fall grew 29.45 -> 30.81 m: the profile got
longer, not bumpier.

REPORTED HONESTLY, THE STRICTER FORM MOVES. Asked per adjacent pair over the
whole tile -- every ordered face pair, no resampling -- the share where the
lower-bedded cell stands higher goes:

    (-11,-6)  34.83% -> 34.79%
    (-11,-5)  30.13% -> 30.50%
    (-12,-5)  32.57% -> 33.26%
    (-11,-4)  15.11% -> 16.69%

bv13 is already at 15-35% by this measure and always was: the fill hands a bank
cell the level of the reach it drains to, and its neighbour one pixel across can
drain to a different reach at a different level, so a local step in either
direction is ordinary. This is why the shipped gate resamples in the first
place, and its own docstring says so ("a single-step rise inside a 4 px ribbon
is a lateral hop, a rise that survives 100 m of smoothing is a real reversal").
The number is recorded rather than suppressed because a future change that moved
it by tens of points would be worth catching.
