BAND PERIOD — what the parallel lines actually are
==================================================
2026-07-31. Site -69094,38426 (g35, the owner's repro pose), bake_ver 6, 51.2 m
window of the CONTINUOUS surface (VXC_PROBE_DUMP, pre-voxelisation) at 10 cm.
Tool: terrain-service/tools/band_period.py. Relief 52.1 m, median grade 95%.

Twenty-plus attempts had been made on this artifact without anyone measuring its
spacing. Three candidate causes predicted different spacings, so one measurement
discriminates. It did, and it falsified two of the three -- including the one I
had argued for hardest.


1. THE BANDING IS PURE VOXEL QUANTISATION OF A LOCALLY SMOOTH SURFACE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
A smooth ramp cut into 100 mm voxels produces a step every 0.1/slope metres.
Measured against that law, by grade bin:

    grade        observed band width   law predicts    n
    15- 30%            0.40 m            0.44 m       413
    30- 60%            0.20 m            0.22 m      6645
    60-120%            0.10 m            0.11 m     51258
    all (median)       0.10 m            0.10 m     41789   ratio 1.25

The law holds in every bin. Ratio 1.25 means the realised surface is only 25%
more irregular than a perfectly smooth ramp would be. There are no terraces in
the data; there is a smooth surface being sampled by a cubic lattice.

The slope dependence is the part that reconciles the screenshots with the
metrics. Shallow ground gives WIDE bands -- at 10% grade one voxel of rise takes
a full metre of run -- which is the metre-scale banding the owner photographs on
moderate slopes. On steep faces the band width collapses to 0.10 m, i.e. ONE
VOXEL: those faces are not banded so much as they are a regular staircase at
voxel pitch, whose regularity is what the eye integrates into lines.


2. THE CARRIER IS NOT PRINTING ITS LATTICE  (hypothesis dead)
-------------------------------------------------------------
If the fine control lattice (1.875 m) were imprinting, the continuous surface
would carry a discrete spectral spike at 1.875 / 3.75 / 7.5 m, independent of
slope -- and every detail-amplitude fix to date would have been aimed at the
wrong layer. It does not. High-passed spectra show power rising smoothly with
wavelength with no discrete peak anywhere in the sub-4 m band; the nominal
"peak" simply rides the largest wavelength the filter passes, which is red noise
behaving normally (and is the same filter-shoulder trap this project has now hit
four times -- the search limit is hp/3 for that reason).

Good news, and it retires a real worry: the fine tier is clean.


3. THE SURFACE IS NOT MONOTONE  (my own hypothesis, dead)
----------------------------------------------------------
I argued that the v14/v15 drainage cap (|grad detail| <= 0.5 |grad carrier|)
makes the surface strictly monotone downslope, and that monotone surfaces cannot
break a contour. The first half is false as implemented:

    fall line from a  2 m carrier:   4.2% of ground rises going downhill
    fall line from a  5 m carrier:  11.3%
    fall line from a 15 m carrier:  30.4%

There is counter-slope, and at landform scale there is plenty of it. The cap is
not the blocker I claimed. What IS true is the weaker, quantitative statement in
section 4, which the cap only partly bears on.


4. THE CRITERION THAT ACTUALLY GOVERNS  (and why 20 fixes failed)
------------------------------------------------------------------
A band of width W is broken only by relief of at least ONE VOXEL (100 mm) at a
wavelength no longer than W. Anything smoother than that leaves the level
crossings parallel, no matter how much relief is added at longer wavelengths.

Every fix attempted so far -- meso relief, folded strata, repose fields, ridge
composites, couloirs, and the amplitude ladders before them -- added relief at
metre-and-up wavelengths. That moves where the bands run. It cannot stop them
being a regular staircase, because at 0.2-1 m the surface stays smooth (ratio
1.25). This is why the metrics kept improving while the picture did not.

Consequences, by ground type:

  moderate ground (15-30% grade, W = 0.4 m): genuinely fixable. It needs
  10-20 cm of relief at wavelengths <= 40 cm. The gradient cap PERMITS this
  (0.5 x 0.22 = 0.11 gradient floor territory) -- the detail simply is not there.

  steep ground (>60%, W = 0.10 m): the band width is already one voxel. No
  sub-voxel change can alter a one-voxel staircase. The only geometric levers
  are the voxel size itself, discrete objects that stand proud of the surface by
  several voxels, or -- at distance, where LOD sets the effective voxel size --
  giving each LOD level its own roughness rather than decimating L0.


5. WHAT THIS RULES IN
----------------------
  * Sub-metre roughness with an ABSOLUTE floor of 1-2 voxels in the 0.2-0.8 m
    band, rather than a floor proportional to carrier slope (a slope-proportional
    floor vanishes exactly where bands are widest -- on gentle ground).
  * Discrete debris and blocks: relief of several voxels at sub-metre scale is
    precisely the missing term, and it is the mechanism nature uses.
  * LOD-own roughness for the distance percept, which no work to date has tried.

  Drainage exposure must be measured, not assumed, for the first two: sub-metre
  bumps of 10-20 cm will create micro-pits. They are physically real (puddles),
  but the stranded-area metric is measured at 10 cm and will see them.
