2. THE DISC IN THE SKY, MEASURED RATHER THAN LOOKED AT
================================================================================

The PR #222 failure put a disc of water about 527 m in the air, following the
camera, because the near-field half was amplifying the PROCEDURAL world while
the screen showed the BAKED one. The frame is the weak way to check that. The
strong way is the disc's own brick count, which is a direct measurement of the
quantity that bug moved:

    pose            camera z     implicit disc rebuild
    valley-width       373.1 m   0 candidate brick(s)
    ladder-200m        449.7 m   0 candidate brick(s)
    ladder-1km       1,249.7 m   0 candidate brick(s)
    dry-altitude     2,014.9 m   0 candidate brick(s)
    ladder-5km       5,249.7 m   0 candidate brick(s)

Zero at every altitude, across three different columns and a 4.9 km spread of
height. No water surface exists anywhere within +-25.6 m horizontally and
+-12.8 m vertically of any of those cameras.

AND THE ZEROS ARE NOT VACUOUS, which is the part that makes them worth
anything. The same mechanism at the 10 m rung reports 16,328 candidate bricks
and 4,082 meshed. So the disc is live, it does produce geometry when water is
near the camera, and it produces none at altitude. A frame with no water
anywhere would be consistent with the subsystem silently never running; these
numbers are not.


================================================================================
3. THE ONE SITE THAT WAS ASKED FOR AND NOT SHOT
================================================================================

The lateral-fill measurement site -- tile (-12,-5), row 850, col 2627, the
86.25 m cross-section -- WAS NOT PHOTOGRAPHED, deliberately.

Its world position is (-179393.44, -75205.31) m. Tile (-12,-5) spans
y [-76800, -61440], so the site sits 1,594.7 m from that tile's y-minimum edge,
and the tile beyond it, (-12,-6), IS NOT BAKED. Clearance is therefore 1,594.7 m
against a 4,500 m rule -- 2,905 m short. That is the condition that produced
2,898 gate leaks and one discarded capture last time.

The reading was verified rather than assumed, because the failure mode here is
an axis convention. `BakeGeometry.padded_origin_cells` has row tracking tile_y
and column tracking tile_x, both INCREASING, so interior row 0 is the tile's
MINIMUM y. Reading row 850 from the other end would put the site 13.8 km from
the edge instead of 1.6 km. The convention was confirmed by reproducing the
document's own published numbers from these coordinates:

    ground 348.0437 m (doc 348.044), water 349.1055 m (doc 349.105)

Both possible conventions put the site within 1,594.7 m of an unbaked edge
(the other end is 1,594.7 m from tile (-12,-4), also unbaked), so the site is
unshootable either way and the conclusion does not depend on the convention.

WHAT WAS SHOT INSTEAD. `width-86m` -- a different reach of the same bake whose
cross-section measures the SAME 86.25 m, at 6,001 m of clearance. The headline
number is reproduced at a legal pose rather than approximated at an illegal one.


================================================================================
4. TWO THINGS THAT ARE WRONG WITH THIS SET, STATED PLAINLY
================================================================================

(a) NO POSE IN THIS TILE SET CAN BE GENUINELY FREE OF RIVER. The driest column
    that also clears 4.5 km is 3,401 m from the nearest water, and the ribbon
    actor's scan radius is 4,000 m. So `dry-altitude` has 19 reaches in frame
    and could not have had zero. For the disc-in-the-sky question this is
    harmless -- arguably better, per section 2 -- but "over dry ground" here
    means "over ground with no water within 2.7 km", not "no river in frame".

(b) I FIRST MEASURED THAT DRYNESS WRONG, and it is worth recording because the
    error was invisible. The first search ran the distance transform PER TILE
    and reported 4,684 m for a column that sits 359 m from its own tile edge
    with a wet neighbour tile just past it; the true cross-tile answer is
    2,700 m. Every interesting column in a four-tile set is near a boundary, so
    this was the normal case, not a corner case. It was caught only because the
    ribbon actor built 19 reaches at a pose whose stated nearest water was
    beyond its 4,000 m scan radius -- two instruments disagreeing. The probe's
    `dry` subcommand now mosaics all baked tiles before transforming.
