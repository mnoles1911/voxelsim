FIRST IN-ENGINE SURVEY OF THE FINE TIER, THREE CLASSES — 2026-07-29
====================================================================

The .vxtl v2 fine tier had never been rendered before this. All three exemplar
classes baked at BAKE_VERSION 4, staged as s16 tiles, captured at 1600x900 with a
200 s settle, sun frozen at 12:00 on 03-20.

it frames a vista rather than looking down. Same site, different pose.

WHAT EACH CLASS LOOKS LIKE

alpine (-5,15), 40.6% grade. The first image in this project that reads as a
mountain: coherent ridgeline, real valleys, material banding from vegetated lower
slopes to bare rock above, a proper skyline. Compare the coarse tier at the same
site, which is a field of isolated one-voxel pillars. This is the step the plan
was aiming at, and it is worth saying plainly that the LANDFORM half of the design
works.

Overlaid on all of it, everywhere, is a uniform scaly micro-texture. That is the
detail band whose gradient strands 93.1% of this tile's drainage. It is not new
information -- it is what the drainage number looks like.

plains (-55,20), 1.7% grade. WORSE than the same site on the coarse tier: where
coarse plains gave broad plateaus with ragged edges, fine plains is dense isolated
single-voxel speckle. This agrees with the measurement rather than contradicting
it (fine amplified 97.3% stranded against its own carrier's 89.0%, where on the
coarse tier the detail IMPROVED a plain). The bake now supplies sub-metre swales
here and the client is fighting them.

third class (15,55), 24.6% grade. The most informative of the three, because the
bake's dendritic drainage is VISIBLE THROUGH the static -- branching sinuous
valleys organise the whole frame. The landform is there and the client's noise is
sitting on top of it rather than replacing it. That is the encouraging reading of
an otherwise bad picture.

TWO CORRECTIONS TO EARLIER REASONING IN THIS SESSION

1. THE GPU POOL IS NOT INTRINSICALLY FRAGMENTED, and I had this backwards. The
   alpine fine run reported `no room for 1633 quads (1385893 free, largest run
   642)` and chunks left undrawn, and by the code's own criterion (far apart =>
   fragmented => wants compaction) I leaned toward compaction over capacity. The
   third-class run says otherwise:

     liveChunks=49723 highWater=52332061 free=27667939 largestRun=27667939
     freeRuns=1 capacityPct=65.4 allocFail=0

   ONE free run, zero allocation failures, zero undrawn chunks. The pool is
   perfectly healthy at 65% capacity. Alpine's fragmentation appeared only under
   near-exhaustion (139,532 chunks and 130.7M quads produced, against 49,723 and
   52.3M here). So fragmentation is a SYMPTOM of running the pool near full, and
   capacity -- or reducing the quad count -- is the primary lever. Compaction is
   secondary at best.

   This also strengthens the case for sequencing: the micro-texture is what emits
   those quads, so the gradient cap should be measured before the pool is resized.

2. SMALL BLUE PATCHES, UNEXPLAINED, and deliberately left that way rather than
   given a story. They appear mid-slope on alpine and third class and NOT on
   plains. What has been ruled out:
     * not undrawn chunks -- the third-class run reports zero;
     * not the water subsystem -- it logged nothing; the only "Water" matches in
       the log are the pool's own `highWater`;
     * not implicit sea level -- core.h:134 makes water implicit only below z=0,
       and this tile's minimum elevation is +47 m;
     * not the flow plane painting a wet material -- the amplifier does not read
       the flow plane at all (no flowPlane/channelFlag consumer in voxel-core/src).
   A river system does exist (rivernet.cpp, rivercouple.h) and is the obvious next
   place to look. Filed rather than guessed.

NO TIMINGS ARE QUOTED FROM ANY OF THESE RUNS. The box's GPU was shared with a
game throughout, which does not affect geometry, materials or the pool's
occupancy counters but does affect anything measured in milliseconds.
