"implied" is Q_in/A_in, the catchment-mean runoff behind the imported water.
"Q_in vs proxy" is Q_in / (A_in x local runoff): how far the boundary condition
moved. Local runoff is c09ef43's figure for each tile.

THIS IS WHY THE FIX HAD LITTLE TO MOVE HERE, and it follows from how the
corridor was chosen. c09ef43 re-selected it on "runoff high the whole way",
precisely so the proxy would not zero it out. But a corridor whose local runoff
matches its catchment's runoff everywhere is exactly the corridor on which the
proxy was ALREADY nearly right: the correction lands between 0.50x and 1.40x
here, where at the (-14,*) mouth it was 19.7x. The two selection criteria are
in tension, and nobody noticed: "a corridor the proxy does not kill" and "a
corridor that shows what fixing the proxy buys" are close to disjoint.

It corrects downward on (-11,-4) -- catchment 100 mm/yr against 199 mm/yr of
local ground -- and that tile LOST 421 wet pixels.

(-12,-5) is the cleanest control in the set: 2.0 km2 of boundary inflow is
negligible against its own catchment, and its wet count is EXACTLY unchanged,
5,914 -> 5,914. A change that moved it would have meant something was wrong.

WHAT THE FRAGMENTATION IS NOT: IT IS NOT THE CURRENCY
------------------------------------------------------
The premise behind the prediction was that area accumulates monotonically while
the conversion to discharge multiplies by a spatially varying local runoff, so
the fragmentation must live in that conversion. Two measurements against it.

1. THE AREA NETWORK ON THE SAME STITCHED GRID, thresholded at the flow plane's
   log2 >= 23 (where wet cells begin), labelled by the same labeller:

     cells 73,575   components 3,615   largest 12.0%   wet share 36.1%
     top by size: 8,823px/4,216m  4,049px/2,207m  3,940px/2,068m
                  2,600px/1,887m  1,757px/1,085m

   Four AREA components span >= 1.8 km and the largest spans 4,216 m, against
   a wet-mask maximum of 1,113 m and nothing at all over 2 km. Only 36.1% of
   above-threshold-area cells are wet. So on identical ground the area network
   is ~3.8x longer in its best piece than the discharge network -- the gap the
   prediction was about is real, and carrying Q closed none of it.

2. THE CURRENCY IS NOT WHERE THE WEIGHTING HAPPENS. `acc` and `q_pad` are
   produced by the SAME `kernels.accumulate_mfd` over the SAME `filled`
   surface with the SAME `mfd_p`. They differ in the source term and in the
   boundary term, and task #49 changed ONLY the boundary term:
   `water.discharge_source` still seeds every interior cell with
   `runoff_m * cell_m^2`, the spatially varying local runoff, exactly as
   before. The per-cell runoff weighting that the prediction blamed is
   untouched by the fix; the fix replaced how the water ARRIVING AT THE EDGE
   is denominated.

   That is consistent with everything above: the tile whose edge delivers
   almost nothing ((-12,-5), 2.0 km2) is bit-for-bit unchanged, and the tile
   whose edge delivers most ((-11,-6), 487.3 km2) gained the most pixels --
   and still gained them as fragments, because the boundary is only ever a
   rim and HYDROLOGY_RESIDUALS #7 governs what happens after it.

Not established here: what DOES set the fragmentation. The candidates the
measurements above are consistent with, none of them tested, are the sharpness
of the `q_drawable` cut against a coarse log2 area bin (a log2 threshold
tolerates a factor of 2 before it breaks a component; a Q threshold does not),
MFD dispersion splitting Q below the cut and back over it, and B5 basin cells
being written dry -- (-11,-6) is 17.4% basin with 154 registered basins. That
needs the discharge raster along a traced channel, which needs a re-bake with
`--bake-npz-dir`, and is deliberately not guessed at here.
