================================================================================
T4-1 -- THE PREMISE IS INVALIDATED BY S1's OWN SUCCESS
================================================================================

Measured 2026-07-28 on the S1 configuration (batched publication, unload budget
256, applies 192, pool 64M, parking 12,000). Adopted 128 m / 4 km cascade, real
terrain, -VoxelPerfFlight=line at 20 m/s.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CLAIM T4-1 WAS BUILT ON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

docs/deep-review-streaming-perf-2026-07-27.md, T4-1:

  "Once T1 frees the consumer side, the GPU is essentially always idle from
   streaming's perspective. Spend it."

Supported by S0's own measurement: the fork sat at ~11 jobs in flight against a
cap of 256, finishing a chunk in ~12 ms and then waiting ~128 ms to be picked up
(docs/measurements/s0-apply-census-2026-07-27.txt).

That measurement was taken WHILE THE CONSUMER WAS THE WALL. It described a
starved producer, and it was read as spare capacity.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE FORK LOOKS LIKE AFTER S1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Flight-phase windows, GPU mesh fork census:

    dispatched=242  delivered=243  failed=0  pending=251  queued=235  inFlight=16
    dispatched=232  delivered=231  failed=0  pending=254  queued=236  inFlight=18
    dispatched=221  delivered=221  failed=0  pending=252  queued=233  inFlight=19

  pending  = submitted, not yet delivered  (GpuJobsPending)   ~252 of a 256 cap
  queued   = sitting in the manager's queue                    ~235
  inFlight = actually executing on the GPU                     ~16

THE SUBMISSION PIPELINE IS FULL OF DEMAND WORK. 235 demand jobs are already
queued and cannot get through. Speculation has nothing to scavenge: a
speculative job submitted here does not use idle GPU, it queues behind 235
demand jobs and is stale before it runs.

S1 did not reveal idle capacity. It CONSUMED the idle capacity, by removing the
consumer bottleneck that was starving the producer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AND inFlight=16 IS NOT THE GPU BEING BUSY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

16 concurrent against a 256 cap, with 235 waiting, is a PROMOTION limit:
FVoxelGpuMeshJobManager::Tick promotes at most voxel.GPU.MeshBatchCap (4) jobs
per tick into one FRDGBuilder.

So the GPU is underutilised because of a per-tick promotion cap, not for lack of
work -- and raising that cap helps DEMAND directly, with no speculation, no
cache, no hit rate and no new correctness surface.

  MeshBatchCap 4 / MeshHarvestCap 8 were measured on 2026-07-27 and the sweep
  that set them was monotone: 32/64 -> 367 hitches, 8/16 -> 8, 4/8 -> 8 with the
  best chunks/s. But those hitches were RENDER-THREAD cost from per-chunk
  publication, which S1-1 removed. The number was correct for a pipeline that no
  longer exists. Being re-swept.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATUS OF T4-1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BUILT, gated off (voxel.Stream.VelocityLeadSec 0), and CORRECTLY BLOCKED. Not
cancelled -- blocked on a precondition that is now measurable rather than
assumed: there must be GPU capacity demand cannot use.

Two bugs were found and fixed getting to this point, both of which made
speculation silently inert while looking configured:

  1. DispatchSpeculativeJobs() was inside DispatchJobs' cold-band re-queue
     branch, so it only ran on ticks with cold-band deferrals.
  2. The demand-protection floor (GpuJobsPending >= 256 - 32) breaks on every
     tick once demand saturates the fork -- which is now always.

Both are worth keeping. The second is the feature correctly declining to
compete with demand; it is only a problem because there is no slack at all.

WHAT WOULD MAKE T4-1 LIVE AGAIN, in order of directness:

  * Raise the promotion cap so the GPU runs more than 16 jobs at once. If demand
    then stops saturating the pipeline, the slack T4-1 wants is real.
  * Tile batching (T3-1), which multiplies chunks-per-dispatch rather than
    dispatches-per-tick. The occupancy census that sizes it already ships.
  * Any configuration where demand throughput is bounded by something OTHER than
    the fork -- e.g. a slower camera, or a settled world where speculation is the
    only work available.

Do NOT re-enable speculation on the strength of the original "GPU is idle"
sentence. That sentence was true of a starved producer and is not true now.
