THE RIVER FILLS THE VALLEY: EXTENT FROM THE TERRAIN, NOT FROM A FORMULA
seed 20260719, tiles (-11,-4) (-11,-5) (-12,-5) (-11,-6)
bake_ver 12 -> 13, TERRAIN_VERSION 8 unchanged
2026-08-04
================================================================================

WHAT WAS WRONG, in the owner's words after flying it:

    "the river water is only being placed over a narrow path rather than
    actually flooding and filling in the terrain to the left and right. the
    river cuts a straight path across a valley floor. the water has a depth of
    approximately 8 voxels. however, to the left and right of the water
    channel, there is empty air, not a river bank."

That is a true report and it is measurable. bake_ver 12 decided extent with
`channel_width_m(Q)` -- discharge in, metres of width out -- and painted the
result as a ribbon. Over the corridor that ribbon is 5.30 m wide at p50 and
7.50 m at p90: a formula's width, nearly constant, independent of the valley it
crosses. The ground beside it is already under water and the bake did not know.

bake_ver 13 replaces the rule. A cell is wet when it stands below the surface of
the water it drains into. Nothing about discharge enters; the terrain decides.

WHERE THE OUTPUT IS. Namespace
`terrain-diffusion-unlabeled-80b9ca451a23eae4-ba9c62170`, seed 000000000135276f,
s16 -- a FRESH namespace created by the BAKE_VERSION roll, holding four
loadable .vxtl tiles. Nothing in any existing namespace was touched; the bv12
tiles this document compares against are still in `-b52995abb`.

    python tools/bake_tiles_from_cache.py --seed 20260719 \
        --cache-dir D:/voxelsim/tile-cache \
        --provider-id terrain-diffusion-unlabeled-80b9ca451a23eae4 \
        "--tiles=-11,-4 -11,-5 -12,-5 -11,-6" --codec zstd \
        --npz-dir D:/tmp/latfill-npz

    282-307 CPU-s per tile (1,191 CPU-s for four), against ~300 before. The
    fill itself is one topological sweep over the D8 forest -- no heap, no
    search -- and costs about 3 CPU-s.

    Tile size barely moves, which is worth knowing before anyone worries about
    9x the wet cells: (-11,-4) 51,602,491 -> 51,654,204 bytes (+0.10%),
    (-11,-5) 47,721,389 -> 48,024,569 (+0.64%). The water sections are small
    next to the elevation control points.

================================================================================
2. THE RULE, AND THE THREE THAT WERE TRIED BEFORE IT
================================================================================

    level(c) = the water surface of the FIRST DRAWN CHANNEL CELL on c's own
               downstream D8 path        (NaN if there is none)
    wet(c)  <=>  z_ground(c) <= level(c) - 0.1 m

Height Above Nearest Drainage, inverted. `water.fill_to_local_surface`.
0.1 m is `WIDEN_MIN_DEPTH_M`, the codec's own representability floor, unchanged.

--- (a) THE FIRST VERSION ANCHORED ON THE NEAREST CHANNEL -- carry each reach's
surface outward, stop where the ground rises above it. It is the reading the
words invite and it is wrong by two orders of magnitude. Measured on these four
tiles, running on bv12's own plane and elevation:

                          bv12 ribbon    nearest-anchored flood
  wet cells                  317,665              66,546,420    x209
  share of tile wet        0.06-0.18%              9.3-39.7%
  added depth p50                  -                    7.8 m
  added depth max                  -                   78.0 m
  distance from channel p50        -              550 - 630 m

  per tile:  (-11,-4)  48,911 ->  6,217,059   x127   9.264%wet
             (-11,-5) 103,476 -> 18,599,065   x180  27.715%wet
             (-12,-5)  42,056 -> 15,065,567   x358  22.449%wet
             (-11,-6) 123,222 -> 26,664,729   x216  39.734%wet

Nothing is wrong with the flood. The level is simply never RE-ANCHORED: a cell
one step off a channel that runs along a ledge is below that channel's surface,
and so is the cell below it, and the cell below that. The nearest channel does
not change as you descend, so the fill runs down the hillside carrying a level
it left behind, and stands 8 m deep on a slope. This is the "getting it wrong
in the easy direction floods a continent" failure, found by running the rule
rather than by reasoning about it. Note that (-11,-5) has NO ground below sea
level at all and still floods 27.7% of itself, so this is not the coastline.

--- (b) A CONNECTED FLOOD SANDWICHED between the source reach's bed and its
surface (`bed(s) <= z(c) <= level(s) - 0.1`), which is the obvious repair: water
spreading out of a channel cannot stand deeper than that channel. It bounds the
DEPTH and not the extent. 9,339,092 cells overall (x29.4) and, on the coastal
tile (-11,-6), 7,041,016 at 10.5% wet with a median cell 306 m from any channel
and 0.38 m deep. A continent flooded at 40 cm.

--- (c) A PER-STATION PERPENDICULAR RAY-MARCH with the same sandwich, which is
level and local by construction because a ray cannot route around an obstacle.
It marches unobstructed across a plain instead: on (-11,-6), 2,088,938 cells at
a p50 drawn width of 610 m and a p90 of 1,782 m. It is also NARROWER than (d)
everywhere else, so it loses on both counts.

--- (d) THE FLOW PATH, which ships. It needed nothing added to bound it -- no
distance cap, no width term, no relief term. The hillside cell above a channel
at 460 m gets 460 m, not the 500 m reach it happens to be nearest, and is dry.
The first version had a 512-px (one apron) safety cap; it was DELETED once the
anchor was right, because a fill that needs a radius to stop is a formula
wearing a flood's clothes.

All four were measured on the same inputs. (a), (b) and (c) are recorded here so
the next person does not re-derive them.

================================================================================

--- THE SURFACE MUST NEVER RISE GOING DOWNSTREAM.

`tools/river_long_profile.py walk`, the longest composed component, head to
mouth, same argv as the bv12 record:

                                       bv12                 bv13
  raw path steps that rise      230 of 13,896 (1.66%)  231 of 13,042 (1.77%)
  resampled every  25 m            11 rises              15 rises
  resampled every 100 m             0 rises               0 rises
  resampled every 250 m             0 rises               0 rises
  descent, water surface        389.4 -> -475.0 m     389.4 -> -475.1 m

ZERO RISES AT 100 m AND AT 250 m, which is the property as it was stated. The
raw-step count is unchanged in character: the walk's own notes record that a
single-step rise inside a ribbon is a lateral hop across the section rather than
a defect, and 98.3% of the rising steps start on a centreline cell.

There is a stronger version of the same check, run over the WHOLE drawn plane
rather than one walked reach -- for every wet cell with a wet D8 receiver,
does the surface fall?

    tile      bv12 rising pairs        bv13 rising pairs
   (-11,-4)   778 of 39,977   max 4.821 m      3 of 140,998   max 0.284 m
   (-11,-5)   906 of 82,964   max 1.413 m     13 of 395,920   max 0.075 m
   (-12,-5)   367 of 27,616   max 0.620 m      2 of 168,609   max 0.111 m

The new plane is two orders of magnitude cleaner on this, and it is not a
coincidence: a filled cell and its receiver share one float by construction, so
the only steps that can rise are the ones bv12 had already.

--- THE WATER SURFACE ACROSS A CROSS-SECTION MUST BE LEVEL.

Exactly, for a bank cell that drains straight into the reach: it inherits the
reach cell's float, the same number. NOT exactly for a cell whose steepest
descent is diagonal -- it reaches the channel a row or two downstream and
inherits that row's surface, which is lower by the channel's own gradient. The
honest bound is therefore

    spread across a section  <=  gradient  x  lateral offset

and that is what the unit test asserts, at that bound rather than at a round
number. Measured on the corridor, level spread within a contiguous wet run:

                            bv12               bv13
  (-11,-5)   p90 / max    0.457 / 2.052 m   0.307 / 2.104 m
  (-11,-6)   p90 / max    1.086 / 19.391    0.321 / 20.238

  |step| between ADJACENT wet cells, metres:
  (-11,-5)   bv12  p50 0.0002  p90 0.1050  p99 0.6671
             bv13  p50 0.0000  p90 0.0408  p99 0.3458
  (-11,-6)   bv12  p50 0.0001  p90 0.0691  p99 0.7465
             bv13  p50 0.0000  p90 0.0020  p99 0.1346

THE FILL'S SURFACE IS FLATTER THAN THE ONE IT REPLACES at every percentile to
p99. The two sites in section 1 spread 0.137 m over 86 m and 0.006 m over 58 m.
The maxima are the same in both columns because they belong to the centreline,
which neither version touches.

--- AND THE CENTRELINE ITSELF IS BIT-IDENTICAL, which is why both properties
were safe to begin with. The drawable set is `q >= q_drawable = 1.528564e6`, and
`discharge_m3_yr` is in the npz, so this is directly checkable rather than
argued:

    (-11,-4)  centreline cells 23,389  all present and BIT-IDENTICAL: True
    (-12,-5)  centreline cells 28,974  all present and BIT-IDENTICAL: True

Every cell that changed value or disappeared is a bv12 WIDENED cell -- 9,016 of
25,522 on (-11,-4), 1,520 of 13,082 on (-12,-5) -- which is the formula's own
output being replaced, and is the entire point.


================================================================================
5. THE ONE THING THAT NEEDS A DECISION: THE SEA
================================================================================

Tile (-11,-6) holds the mouth. Its %wet goes 0.1836 -> 3.2134, far out of line
with the other three (which land at 0.22-0.61%), and the reason is a single
number:

    bv12   27,623 of   123,222 wet cells have ground <= 0 m   (22.4%)
    bv13  1,195,259 of 2,156,457                              (55.4%)

THE RIVER PLANE IS BEING PAINTED OVER THE SEAFLOOR, and the lateral fill
amplifies it 43x. Mechanically it is correct: the reach descends to -475 m, its
water surface at the mouth is about 0 m, and every submarine cell genuinely
drains to it and genuinely stands below it. The fill has no way to know that
something else already draws that water.

It is not a valley being drowned. Above sea level the corridor's width is
p50 30.00 m and p90 103.12 m and every edge sits on rising ground. Excluding the
submarine cells the change is 290,042 -> 1,696,228, a factor of 5.85, and the
depth distribution goes DOWN. The flooding is bounded on land.

But it makes the headline reach statistic meaningless: the longest composed
component is now 21,370 m with a four-axis ribbon width of p50 1,164 m, and that
number is the sea, not a river. THE CLEAN INLAND REACH TO QUOTE INSTEAD is
rank 1 -- 14,837 m span, 207,931 cells, 4.3% lake, 190.5 -> 978.4 m.

THE REMEDY IS ONE LINE AND IT IS NOT MINE TO TAKE. `fill_to_local_surface`
already accepts a barrier mask, and the argument for using it here is the one
this module's own docstring makes about lakes: a registered basin's surface is
on the wire and "writing it into the plane as well would be a second copy of a
shipped fact, free to disagree with the first". The ocean is the same case.
Passing `exclude |= (z_ground <= 0)` would stop the fill at the shoreline. It
was NOT done here, for two reasons:

  * it would also remove bv12's own 27,623 submarine cells, which is a change
    to existing deliberate behaviour and outside what this task was asked to
    change -- `docs/measurements/river-long-profile-2026-08-04.txt` uses "the
    composed reach runs out onto the seafloor" to identify the mouth;
  * the ocean surface is live work in another subsystem and the two should be
    decided together, not raced.

The number is here so the decision is made on it rather than on a screenshot.
