Timeline from first window (20:03:58) to first settle (~20:04:48, ~55 s):

  * Rings fill STRICTLY SERIALLY -- R0's queue drains to 0 by +4 s, then R1
    (+10 s), R2 (+14 s), R3 (+20 s), R4 (+28 s), R5 (+50 s). Nearest-first

  * 254,717 jobs / ~50 s = ~5,100 jobs/s average. That IS the settle time.

1. WHERE DOES COLD-FILL TIME GO? (the parent's question 2, answered)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Into worker-side generation, full stop. Same leg, first fill:

  * Streaming tick: 11-19% of wall after the first window (the first window's
    48% is the initial recompute burst, 1,810 ms once, ~3% of the fill).
    The earlier "0.25% of wall" reading was a steady-state window, not a
    fill window -- the tick-budget line is fine; the window was wrong.
  * recompute= falls to ~50-90 ms/window after window 1. Admission is cheap.
  * dispatch stages: perDispatch=0.002-0.006 ms. The 0.21 ms/chunk
    game-thread dispatch cost recorded in the dispatch-stages comment does
    NOT hold on this leg; the loop is ~30-100 ms/window at 8k+ launches/s.
  * apply= 125-290 ms/window; brickFlush= 5-11 ms/window. Neither binds.
  * Worker side: cpuJobSec pins at 15.1-15.4 per 2 s window = effConc
    7.5-7.6, every fill window, while launches run 8k/s at 0.72 ms/job.
    Little's law closes: ~7.6 effective workers / 0.72 ms = ~10.5k/s ceiling,
    and the observed launch rate rides just under it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE MECHANISM: WHY MORE THREADS DO NOT HELP (this is the keeper)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The -VoxelWorkerPool=16 arm (wk-pool.log) against control (wide-on-30.log),
same job mix, census perJob columns -- JobCycles is QueryThreadCycleTime, so
Kcyc is cycles the thread actually executed, not wall:

                    control (8-ish workers)    pool=16
  perJob wall            721-818 us          1,419-1,829 us
  perJob cycles          ~2,494-2,829 Kcyc   ~2,550-3,282 Kcyc   (SAME)
  effective GHz          3.39-3.52           1.68-2.05           (HALVED)
  effConc                7.5-7.6             15.6-16.2           (DOUBLED)
  launches/window        ~14-16k             ~14-17k             (FLAT)

Sixteen threads genuinely ran (effConc = pool size; the pool IS the tap
opener the worker-tap doc asked for). Each job executed the SAME number of
cycles -- no lock-spin appeared -- but the cycles came at half the clock.
Twice the threads, half the per-thread speed, product constant. The box
delivers ~10-11k jobs/s of this workload TOTAL regardless of thread count;
8 threads at ~3.5 GHz and 16 at ~1.8 GHz are the same machine. (Whether the
halving is turbo/power binning or P-core/E-core split on the 24-logical-core
box changes nothing downstream: the falsified lever stays falsified, and now
with its mechanism attached.) inflight96.log doubles the in-flight cap on
top (cap=768 held 720-760) -- launches identical to wk-pool.log's. Queue
depth past the pool was already dead as a lever; this is why.

CONSEQUENCE, arithmetic form: the default cascade is ~130k chunks, the wide
arm ~255k. At the hardware's ~10-11k jobs/s:

    130k / 10.5k/s = ~12 s   <- CPU best case, default rings, perfect tap
    255k / 10.5k/s = ~24 s   <- wide arm
    target                  = 2.6-5 s

NO CPU-side pacing, priority, cap, admission, or thread-count change can
reach the target. The routes that remain are exactly three:
  (a) fewer jobs, (b) cheaper jobs, (c) a second producer (the GPU worklist).
(c) is the only one with 10x headroom -- GPU compute sits at 1-2% through
the entire fill (gpuForked ~330/s = 4% of traffic, bounded by the per-chunk
round trip that section 9.5 of the handoff already condemned). The worklist
work in flight is therefore also the cold-start fix; cold start is its
FIRST customer, not an afterthought.

3. IS COLD FILL RATE-LIMITED BY DESIGN? (question 3: NO -- measured)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every pacing suspect was open during the fill:
  * exitEmpty=0, exitCap=all passes: dispatch never starved, never idled.
  * pendingJobs 690-10,474: admission kept the queue fed through all six
    ring hand-offs (the sawtooth is the per-ring batch admission, and its
    troughs never reach 0).
  * apply exits: queueEmpty-dominated, countCap=0 (drain8/drain16 evidence).
A cold-start "burst mode" that relaxes MaxAppliesPerFrame / ApplyBudgetMs /
the Cap/4 budget would relax gates that are not binding. Do not author it;
this paragraph exists so nobody re-runs that experiment.

4. FEWER JOBS / CHEAPER JOBS: WHAT THE SKIPS ALREADY DO, AND TWO OPEN LEADS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The skips are ON and honest where they apply -- the population above is net
of them:
  * Buried skip: 5,656 air skips in the first 6 s, then quiet (band coverage
    ends with R0's fill). solidKept=13,848 -- would-be solid skips VETOED
    because the marcher's brick volume needs solid bricks (volumeFed=1).
    That is ~5% of the fill paid to feed the volume. A "canonical solid
    brick" fast path inside the job (skip grid+mesh, emit uniform bricks)
    could reclaim most of those 13.8k jobs' cost, but it needs per-voxel
    material fidelity answered first -- flagged, not authored.
  * Sky skip: R0 34-55/window, R1 <30, R2-R5 ZERO all leg. The outer rings'
    populations (~41-44k each, same as R0's) are already Z-clamped to the
    surface band, so "outer rings are overwhelmingly sky" does not hold on
    this preset -- OR IsChunkProvablyAllAir's bound is too conservative at
    L>=2 and a working skip is reading as no-op. One leg with the verify arm
    (-VoxelVerifySkyBand) at R2+ would say which. Sixth-inert-feature
    candidate; cheap to check; do not assume either way.
  * Per-job cost: 2.5 Mcyc/job at L0, grid build 36-39% of it WITH the
    shared cache hitting (cycPerColumn 765-833 during fill -- the cache is
    working). The remaining ~60% is voxelize+mesh+CPU brick pack
    (TimedMeshChunkBricks). Whether the coarse-ring tail (jobs 3-4x L0
    cost) is grid-bound or pack-bound is readable TODAY from the census
    per-ring workerMs -- read it before optimising either half.
