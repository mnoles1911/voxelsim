THE DETAIL GRADIENT CAP — worldgen v14, measured 2026-07-29
===========================================================

The fix for docs/measurements/client-detail-drainage-2026-07-29.txt and the
ten-class ladder in drainage-ladder-v13-2026-07-29.txt: at v13 the post-gate
detail ladder summed to ~2.3 of nominal gradient on ground whose own gradient
is 0.4, so the client's texture reversed the carrier's downhill direction and
turned the bake's routed drainage into closed decimetre basins.

WHAT SHIPPED (amplifier.cpp "THE DETAIL GRADIENT CAP", mirrored bit-exactly in
worldgen.ush, kWorldGenVersion 13 -> 14):

  One scale-down factor over the whole summed post-gate detail so that its
  nominal gradient (per-band amplitude/lattice, through the same gates as the
  detail itself) obeys

      detailGradient <= max(gradFloor, k * carrierGradient)

  ENGAGED per column: everywhere on a fine-tier world; on a coarse world on a
  linear ramp over per-cell landform relief (off <= 3600 mm of relief30, full
  >= 7500 mm — the ladder's own 12%/25% grade thresholds in relief currency).
  Constants: k = 0.5 (q10 512), gradFloor = 50 mm/m on both tiers.

Relative octave weighting untouched. Scale-down only, one truncating divide
toward zero, so every v13 surface bound is still sound unchanged; the derived
tripwires (kLandformMaxMm trio, AbsMax trio, kDetailMaxAtMaxSlopeMm 15310 /
5513) keep their v13 values — checked by static_assert, not assumed. The
bound's WORST case is not tightened (the cap is taken at its 1.0 ceiling in
surfaceBoundsMm, the same trade couplings (5)/(6) already take); the typical
surface tightens, the bound does not claim the credit.

The bake supplies NO decimetre texture (the 100% row), so on the fine tier the
floor is a straight trade between the swales' drainage and the flats' anti-
terrace band, and no column-local discriminator separates a swale bottom from

50 is the measured knee: below it the drainage residual (1.3 points from
carrier) cannot shrink by more than it is, while the last of the texture goes.
IF FINE PLAINS READS AS TERRACED IN-ENGINE, kFineDetailGradFloorMmPerM is the
knob, and each +50 buys back roughly 0.4 m of terrace run for ~3 m of mean
flow path. This is the largest open cost in v14 and it is stated, not hidden.

FORMULATIONS REJECTED ON MEASUREMENT (kept so nobody retries them)

* CONSTANT FLOOR (the brief's formula taken literally): the required
  allowance is NON-MONOTONE in slope — plains needs >= ~410 mm/m at 17 mm/m
  of slope (floor 100 took its plateau fraction from 79.3% to 98.9%: the
  terrace artifact, back), the 20-25% rows need <= ~200 at ten times the
  slope. No (k, floor) pair produces that shape.
* MATERIAL-BAND EXEMPTION: capping only the relief-gated bands keeps the
  plains plateau bit-exact and leaves alpine 78.7% stranded — the decimetre
  band IS the pit field.
* SLOPE-RAMPED ENGAGEMENT: fixes every uniform class and leaves the incised
  sites broken (steepest 77.3%, third 67.0%): drainage is non-local, water
  exits through valley floors, and a slope-local exemption grants the outlet
  itself full-amplitude detail. Relief30 distinguishes a true plain (665 mm)
  from a valley floor (walls inside the 30 m baseline), which is why the
  shipped ramp is on relief.

MEASUREMENT TRAPS HIT IN THIS SESSION (recorded per house rule):
* Two "stride-1 steepest is stuck at 97%" hypotheses (cap disengaging on
  cliffs; broken source tiles) were both falsified by direct measurement
  before the real mechanism (the control window sits on an exempt bench) was
  confirmed by an offset window. The k128 diagnostic that falsified the first
  also produced a void number (moderate 98.3%) by reading a tile absent from
  the original cache — single-site probes must use the repaired set.
