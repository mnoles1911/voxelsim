THE PYRAMID'S DISCHARGE, AND THE WIDTH LAW
seed 20260719, tiles (-11,-4) (-11,-5) (-12,-5) (-11,-6), bake_ver 11 -> 12
2026-08-04
================================================================================

Two PRODUCT constants, neither of which can move a height. Both were A/B'd on
the real corridor as separate arms, because bake_ver 11's own record shows how
easily one change takes credit for another's effect.

  water_pyramid_single_receiver  (new) True
      build_flow_superblock's DISCHARGE sweep routes D8 instead of MFD, at
      every level. `acc` -- the AREA field, which decides every height -- stays
      MFD at mfd_p. Conditional key "q_single_receiver" added to
      superblock_inputs_fingerprint, on the entry_mode precedent: absent means
      MFD, which is what every block cached before bake_ver 12 did.

  water_width_from_law           (new) True
      channel_width_m(Q) decides the drawn EXTENT the way water_depth_m(Q)
      already decides depth, clamped to ground standing at least 100 mm below
      that reach's own water surface.


================================================================================
PART 1 -- THE SEAM. WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS.
================================================================================

The handover's diagnosis was right about WHERE and wrong about WHAT. It read
"the fine Q does not cross a fine tile boundary; each tile restarts from the
30 m superblock injection". The first half is the symptom. The second half
implies the downstream tile is STARVED, and it is not.

MEASURED at the (-11,-5)/(-11,-6) seam on the shipped bv11 dumps, comparing the
level-0 superblock's own injection against the fine tier either side.

  Geometry confirmed before anything was concluded from it. Row index tracks
  tile_y, column tracks tile_x; across the y-seam the two tiles' elevations
  agree to a MEAN of 0.072 m (p50 0.038, p99 0.737), and across the x-seam to
  0.420 m. The apron works for terrain. Flow runs NORTH: each tile's discharge
  maximum is on its row 0.

  At the seam, at the same world cell:

    (-11,-5) row 0    trunk Q  2.354e7 m3/yr  at column 5896
    (-11,-6) row 8191 trunk Q  7.176e5 m3/yr  at the same column   = 3.05%

  q_drawable is 1.5286e6, so the downstream tile reads the trunk as dry.

  THE TOTAL CROSSING THE BOUNDARY WAS NEVER SHORT. Rebuilding the superblock
  and scattering both sweeps through the identical crossings:

                              MFD (shipped)      D8         D8/MFD
    tile (-11,-6), 832 crossings
      injected Q, total       8.6261e7        8.5094e7       0.99
      injected Q, max cell    8.6554e6        2.0227e7       2.34
    tile (-11,-5), 536 crossings
      injected Q, total       1.8288e7        1.6962e7       0.93
      injected Q, max cell    3.8353e6        6.0753e6       1.58

  So the defect is DIVISION, not attenuation. Along the seam row the coarse
  trunk under MFD is split across three adjacent columns -- 9.50e6 / 7.08e6 /
  2.29e6 -- while D8 puts 2.11e7 in one, against the 2.354e7 the fine tier
  carries 180 m away. A hard threshold cannot use a fan.

  A test that only checked conservation would have passed throughout the bug.
  test_the_pyramid_routes_its_discharge_single_receiver says so explicitly.


WHAT THE FIX BOUGHT, AND WHAT IT DID NOT
-----------------------------------------
Three arms, same tiles, same terrain, everything else equal. "pyramid only" is
bv12 with water_width_from_law=False.

                            bv11        pyramid only     bv12 (both)
  wet pixels              158,458          163,643          317,665
  components (8-conn)         173              172              149
  longest span             14,827 m         15,028 m         15,030 m
  components >= 2 km           36               35               35
  components >= 5 km           14               14               14
  components >= 10 km           1                3                3
  longest piece at coast    9,881 m         15,028 m         15,030 m
  ribbon width p90           1.88 m           1.88 m           7.50 m
  ribbon width max           3.75 m           3.75 m          15.91 m

The apportionment is clean and neither change costs the other anything:

  * THE PYRAMID CHANGE buys reach LENGTH and the coast connection. The piece
    that reaches the sea goes 9,881 -> 15,028 m (+52%) and the count of >=10 km
    reaches goes 1 -> 3. It merges almost nothing (173 -> 172 components) and
    adds 3.3% more wet pixels.
  * THE WIDTH CHANGE buys the RIBBON and the merging. Wet pixels 1.94x, width
    p90 4.0x, and components 172 -> 149 -- the merge is the widening's doing,
    not the seam fix's, which is exactly what the arm exists to establish.

  The one number that fell, 36 -> 35 components >= 2 km, is two reaches merging
  into the 15,028 m one. Nothing got worse in either arm.

THE SEAM IS STILL A TERMINATOR, and this is the honest residual. Classifying
every multi-km reach by where its two ends sit:

                                    bv11        bv12
    reaches >= 2 km                   36          35
      ending at an INTERIOR seam      11          10
      ending only at the window       10          11
      ending in open ground           15          14

  Ten of thirty-five still stop on a tile edge, including the 978.1 m reach,
  which still ends at 190.5 m, 6.66 km short of the sea, on the same seam.

WHY, AND IT IS A SECOND MECHANISM, NOT A LEFTOVER OF THE FIRST. D8 fixed the
MAGNITUDE at the crossing. It cannot fix the POSITION. At the seam the coarse
D8 trunk sits at 30 m column 886 (x = -157,740) and the fine trunk at column
880 (x = -157,905) -- 165 m apart. The injected water enters at the fine cell
that is lowest inside that 30 m footprint and then routes on the fine surface,
and it does not arrive where the upstream tile's own accumulation arrives:

    (-11,-6) inlet row, trunk column 5896      bv11 3.05%   bv12 2.35% of upstream
    (-11,-6) inlet row, total                  bv11 0.355   bv12 0.475 of upstream
    (-11,-6) inlet row, wet cells              bv11 2       bv12 6

  The ground at the injection row explains it. Along tile (-11,-6)'s northern
  padded border (tile (-11,-5)'s row 512) over the 375 m spanning both channels,
  the elevation runs 194.5-199.8 m with no incised channel at all and a maximum
  local Q of 1.335e6 -- below the drawable cut. This is flat near-coast plain.
  On ground like that the thalweg is decided by relief the 30 m pyramid cannot
  see, so the concentrated discharge is injected on the wrong side of a divide a
  few metres high, and a 960 m apron does not heal it because there is no valley
  to fall into. The water ends up 1.3 km east.

  THE FIX THAT FOLLOWS FROM THIS, not built here: place the entry cell by the
  FINE surface's own thalweg -- the maximum fine accumulation inside the
  crossing footprint -- instead of by its lowest elevation. It needs one extra
  fine sweep (accumulate, find the thalweg, inject, accumulate again) and it is
  NOT a free change: _edge_entries deliberately scatters BOTH currencies through
  one set of crossings, so moving the entry cell moves the AREA injection too,
  which moves incision, which moves the ground. Doing it for the discharge alone
  breaks the property that "the width law reads a Q that belongs to the same
  channel the incision cut". That is a design decision, not a patch.


================================================================================
PART 2 -- THE WIDTH LAW
================================================================================

bake_ver 11 drew a centreline: 99.21% of wet pixels were a single 1.875 m pixel
against a law width of 3.53 m at p50. The plane is wet where a cell's OWN Q
clears the cut, and a single-receiver forest has one-cell-wide branches by
construction, so MFD's fan was what used to make the ribbon wide. That agreement
was an accident of dispersion and D8 correctly removed it.

WHICH BOUND SHAPES THE RIVER -- measured BEFORE the rule was designed, on the
bv11 dumps, by walking perpendicular transects and counting contiguous pixels
with ground <= the drawn water level:

                              (-11,-5)              (-11,-6)
    terrain-allowed width   p50 11.25  p90 46.88   p50 28.12  p90 163.12  m
    law width               p50  3.45  p90  4.40   p50  3.78  p90   5.58  m
    terrain allows the whole law width      90.10%              93.09%
    no room at all (1 px)                    6.63%               4.38%

  So the TERRAIN is not the binding constraint -- the LAW is. That decided the
  design: the law shapes the ribbon and the terrain clamp bites on the 7-10%
  that would otherwise be drawn uphill. A rule that took the terrain first would
  flood a valley floor, which is what vxc_bankprobe's "no containing ground
  within 120 m on 736 of 1,260 bank sides at 10 m depth" means in practice.

  The law's half-width is 0.78-2.23 fine pixels over this corridor, so the
  drawn ribbon is 1-5 px. There is no stamped band and no visible disc at that
  scale; what varies it is Q, continuously, plus the terrain trimming one flank
  and not the other.


WHAT IT DREW
------------
Ribbon width = the narrowest contiguous wet cross-section through a pixel,
minimised over row/col/diag/anti (tools/corridor_water_report.py). Over ALL wet
pixels, which is what the acceptance table quotes:

                        bv11      bv12
    min                 1.88      1.88   m
    p50                 1.88      5.30
    p90                 1.88      7.50
    p99                 1.88     10.61
    max                 3.75     15.91
    <= 1.9 m          99.21%    28.17%  of wet pixels

Over CENTRELINE pixels only -- cells whose own Q clears the cut, which is the
only population the law is a function of. After the change 48.5% of wet pixels
are widened ones whose own Q is a hillside trickle, so a law column computed
over every wet pixel is not apples to apples:

                                    bv11      bv12
    drawn width p50                 1.88      1.88   m
    drawn width p90                 1.88      5.62
    law width p50                   3.53      3.67
    drawn/law p50                   0.53      0.63
    drawn/law mean                  0.51      0.79
    within one pixel of the law    59.8%     93.1%
    under HALF the law             40.1%      1.7%
    over TWICE the law              0.0%      0.1%

  That is the statement worth keeping: the drawn ribbon now agrees with
  channel.h's width law to within one raster pixel on 93.1% of the network,
  against 59.8% before, and the share drawn at under half its own law width
  falls from 40.1% to 1.7%. It does not over-draw: 0.1% exceeds twice the law.

  The centreline p50 stays at 1.875 m because the law's half-width is 0.98 px
  at the median -- half the network's channel is narrower than the raster can
  resolve as more than one pixel, and no rule can change that at 1.875 m/px.


DEPTHS, AND THE ONE ARTEFACT WORTH LOOKING AT IN A SCREENSHOT
--------------------------------------------------------------
The water surface is FLAT across the channel, because that is what a water
surface is, so depth tapers on its own wherever the bed rises toward the bank.
The codec stores depth, so the client's sub-pixel zero-crossing lands on a
contour of the bed rather than on the raster step the widening added.

    depth, metres, bv12        n         min    p50    p90    p99     max
      centreline           163,643     0.392  0.499  0.724  1.046   1.073
      widened              154,022     0.100  0.591  1.010  1.950   8.924

  The centreline's range is IDENTICAL to bv11's -- widening adds cells, it
  never moves a level.

  The tail is real and is reported rather than hidden: 0.93% of widened cells
  stand deeper than 2 m and 0.029% (91 cells of 317,665) deeper than 5 m. Those
  are cut banks -- a cell within two pixels of the centreline whose ground drops
  several metres, which is what the outside of a bend looks like. Physically
  defensible, and the one thing to look at in a capture.

  0 wet pixels stand above the drawn ground, in bv11 or bv12. The clamp holds
  by construction and was checked rather than assumed.


================================================================================
PART 3 -- DO THE LAKES AND BASINS FILL?
================================================================================
Yes. Verified, not rebuilt.

  255 registered basins over the four tiles -- 246 overflowing lakes, 9
  terminal. ZERO dry playas, salt flats or seasonal basins, so lakes.h's
  `holdsWater()` filter (kind >= kBasinLakeTerminal) removes nothing here.

  total wet area   8,304,739 m2 = 8.305 km2   (2,362,237 fine cells)
  area             p50 6,676 m2   p90 32,972   max 2,821,887 (2.82 km2)
  depth            p50 3.03 m     p90 7.19     max 80.80 m
  empty extents    0

  floor < surface_mm <= spill_mm holds for all 255. No lake sits at its own
  floor; none stands above its own outlet. 0 of 255 would return an empty mask
  from lakes.h's `lakeExtentFill` (seed in bbox, surface != kNoWaterMm, and
  elev(seed) <= surface all hold).

  THE BASIN EXCLUSION IS REAL: of the 2,362,237 cells inside a registered
  lake's extent, 0 carry river water in the bake's plane. The bake writes basin
  cells dry by design and the client unions them back through
  CompositeWaterSampler, exactly once, with no duplicated bytes.

  Two places a registered lake could still fail to draw, neither of them a bake
  problem: `unresolvedBasins()` caches an EMPTY mask when the tile is not
  resident (lakes.h says explicitly that empty != dry -- watch the counter), and
  `implicitWaterFill` requires zMm >= the AMPLIFIED ground, so the single 0.58 m
  lake (~6 voxels) is within plausible detail-band amplitude.

PART 4 -- THE NUMBER THAT MATTERS
================================================================================
"Does a reach run from a mountain head to the shoreline without a break?"

ON THE RASTER ALONE -- no.
  Longest at the coast: 15,030 m, head 389.0 m, low end 10.9 m and 0.79 km from
  the nearest sea cell. The last stretch is a coastal basin, which the bake
  writes dry on purpose.
  The 978.1 m, 1,326.0 m and 1,540.3 m heads all still stop inland. The 978.1 m
  one ends at 190.5 m on the (-11,-5)/(-11,-6) seam, unchanged from bv11.

COMPOSED THE WAY THE CLIENT DRAWS IT -- yes, from a foothill, not a mountain.
Union of the river plane with the 255 lake sheets, labelled once, 8-connected:

                                   bv11                    bv12
    composed cells               2,520,690               2,679,897
    components                         258                     258
    longest span                    16,341 m                20,269 m
      head                           238.9 m                 389.0 m
      low end                       -476.0 m                -476.0 m
      lake share of its cells         40.2%                    5.0%
    second longest                  15,332 m                17,909 m
      head / low                240.4 / -476.0 m       269.1 / -476.0 m

  bv12's longest composed reach runs 20,269 m unbroken from 389.0 m down to
  -476.0 m -- out onto the seafloor -- and only 5.0% of it is lake sheet, so it
  is a river rather than a chain of lakes. bv11's longest was 16,341 m and 40.2%
  lake. A second reach of 17,909 m also runs from 269.1 m to the seafloor.

  For contrast, the 1,540.3 m head does appear in a 13,030 m composed component
  -- but 94.4% of its cells are lake sheet. That is a lake chain, not a river,
  and it should not be quoted as one.
