F6: THE PYRAMID DELIVERS WATER ONTO THE TILE'S EDGE, NOT INTO IT
================================================================
Measured 2026-08-05 on tile (-7,-5), bake_ver 14, seed 20260719.

This is a SECOND measurement of a mechanism the codebase already names and
already measured once: `pipeline.HYDROLOGY_RESIDUALS` #7, which flags itself as
"SEPARATE FROM CARRIED DISCHARGE (task #49), AND NOW THE BINDING ONE". The value
here is the magnitude -- on (-14,-5) it cost a factor of 6, here it costs 276 --
and a second currency showing the same loss independently.

THE MECHANISM, restated. `_edge_entries` finds parent cells that are OUTSIDE the
child and drain INTO it. "The child" is the PADDED domain, whose border is 960 m
outside the interior that ships. A stream can therefore enter the padded domain,
run its whole downstream path through the apron, and leave again without ever
crossing the tile that ships. Nothing is lost numerically; the water is
delivered to the wrong 960 m.

  DISCHARGE ONLY INCREASES DOWNSTREAM. A 276x fall from the padded maximum to

  The pyramid is NOT at fault and neither is task #49. The level-0 superblock
  (-2,-2) covering this tile carries discharge (q max 8.418e8 m3/yr = 26.7
  m3/s), the carried flag is 1, and the implied catchment runoff of 349 mm/yr is
  exactly what a real upstream catchment should look like. The water is
  computed, carried, and handed over correctly. It is handed over 960 m too far
  out.

  WHY THE TWO CURRENCIES LOSE BY DIFFERENT FACTORS (4.97x vs 276x) WITHOUT
  DISAGREEING. The interior's largest catchment, 227.38 km2, is essentially the
  tile's own area of 235.9 km2 -- i.e. entirely self-generated, with the
  imported 1,129 km2 contributing nothing. The area ratio looks milder only
  because the tile makes a lot of its own area. Discharge has no such floor on
  an arid tile (local runoff ~5.7 mm/yr), so the same geometric failure shows
  its true size there. One mechanism, two views.

  This matters beyond water: accumulation drives incision through A^m, so #7
  understates the TERRAIN's channel network too, not only its water.

  THE OFF ARM REPRODUCES THE REPO'S OWN INDEPENDENT MEASUREMENT of (-14,-5)

  just the fix.

  WATER-ONLY, ASSERTED. On both tiles, elevation, accumulation and flow are
  bit-identical between arms: 0 of 67,108,864 cells differ, max|diff| exactly 0.

  THE INFLOW MOVES IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, AND THAT IS CORRECT. On (-7,-5) it rises
  0.2% -- the predicted apron double count, estimated at 0.1% beforehand. On
  (-14,-5) it FALLS 6.6%, because parent cells whose flow crosses the padded rim
  but never reaches the interior are now correctly excluded. A stream that only
  clips the apron no longer counts as having reached the tile, which was listed
  as a risk to watch and is behaving as intended.

  STILL TO DECIDE: whether the default flips. That rolls bake_ver and
  invalidates every baked water plane in every cache root.

  The residual's own prescription, for the record: "a better NUMBER carried across the same seam
  cannot fix it; the seam has to deliver into the domain rather than onto its
  edge."

  Compute the crossings against the INTERIOR rectangle instead of the padded
  one, and inject the discharge currency there. Two properties make it worth
  measuring first:

   * it is exactly the prescription above, and it is a change of RECTANGLE, not
     of physics -- `_edge_entries` already takes the child rect as an argument;
   * IT CAN BE WATER-ONLY. Leave the AREA injection at the padded rim untouched
     so incision, and therefore the shipped ground, stay bit-identical and
     TERRAIN_VERSION does not roll. Move only the discharge currency, which
     rolls bake_ver. tools/verify_water_only_change.py proves the split held.

  RISKS TO MEASURE, not to argue away:

   * the deposit lands where the 30 m parent's D8 says the flow crosses, and the
     1.875 m interior surface may disagree about which side of an interfluve it
     is on (residual #3, bounded by one 30 m cell, heals within a few hundred
     metres -- but the healing distance is now inside the shipped tile);
   * a stream that genuinely only clips the apron must still contribute nothing;
   * conservation: inject at the interior rim INSTEAD of the padded rim for the
     discharge currency, never both, or the same water enters twice;
   * the apron's own q becomes understated. That is acceptable only because the
     apron is discarded -- but `overshoot_stats` and the contact gates read the
     interior, so check none of them silently depend on apron q.

A TRAP FOUND ON THE WAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  `tools/dump_stage_heightfields.py::_coarse_fetch` returns `.elevation` ONLY
  and discards the climate planes. `bake_tile` without a `climate_fetch` has
  `padded_climate = None`, so `runoff_field_mm_yr` returns None and the ENTIRE
  B6 water pass is skipped -- silently, with `water_stats` simply absent rather
  than zero. Anyone using that tool to investigate water gets a bake with no
  water in it and no error. Cost one 281 s bake to notice.
