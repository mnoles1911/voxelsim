decision actually needs. Every earlier fork number was taken at R0 = 128 m.

Default spawn, -VoxelPerfFlight=static, edit log cleared per leg, 2 legs each,
-VoxelPerfLogInterval=1 (see the resolution note below).

  settle-to-peak (11,010 chunks):

SETTLE TIME CANNOT DISTINGUISH THEM AT THIS SCALE, and saying "identical" would
be reading the instrument rather than the world. The whole cascade fills in ~6 s
and the log interval is 1 s, so there are six samples; both configurations
finish inside the same bucket. THE FILL CURVE resolves what settle cannot:

The fork is strictly AHEAD at every sample from t=1. The separation at t=4 is
~1,060 chunks against a within-config spread of ~60, so it is an order of
magnitude outside the noise floor -- unlike the settle number, which is inside a
single quantum.

WHAT THIS SETTLES: the fork is NOT A REGRESSION at the cascade that actually
ships, which was the open risk. A ~28 ms GPU round trip could plausibly have
failed to amortise over 11,010 chunks and made the common case worse. It does
not; it fills faster throughout and simply runs out of world before the
advantage can show up in a completion time.

WHAT IT DOES NOT SETTLE: cold fill is not the criterion the plan sets for Wave
D. That is per-ring dispatch rate x p50 UNDER MOTION, and nothing here moves the
camera. Stale results are also expected to rise with multi-frame latency and are
unmeasured. Edited chunks are carved out by NeedsOverlayAwarePath and a dig has
not been tried against the fork.

For the record, the same fork at R0 = 128 m: 36.3 / 38.3 s against a CPU
58.4 / 60.4 s, i.e. ~38% faster, where the fill is long enough for settle time
to resolve it.
