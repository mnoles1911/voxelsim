TASK #49 -- CARRYING DISCHARGE UP THE FLOW PYRAMID
seed 20260719, bake_ver 9 -> 10, 2026-08-04
================================================================================

THE DEFECT, RESTATED FROM ITS OWN DOCSTRING
-------------------------------------------
`bake.water.discharge_source` converted the flow pyramid's injected upstream
AREA into a discharge by multiplying it by the LOCAL runoff at the injection
cell. That is right only if a river's catchment has the climate of its own
mouth. Every river that leaves its climate zone was understated, and the
worst case -- wet mountains draining across an arid coastal plain -- was
understated to zero.

WHAT WAS BUILT
--------------
`FlowSuperblock` gained `q`: a runoff-weighted discharge in m^3/yr, from a
second MFD sweep over the same filled surface, at every level of the pyramid.
`_edge_entries` computes the boundary crossings ONCE and both currencies are
scattered through the same (entry cell, target cell) pairs, so area and
discharge cross at the same cell by construction. B6 adds the carried Q
directly instead of reconstructing one.

`q is None` is a third state, distinct from zero: a block built without climate
cannot say how much water crosses, B6 falls back to the old proxy, and the tile
reports `water_q_inflow_carried = 0`.

imported water. Compare it with the local column -- at (-14,-6) the arriving
water is worth 71 mm/yr of catchment instead of the 3.6 mm/yr of ground it

In absolute terms at (-14,-6): 2.50e7 m^3/yr carried, against 1.27e6 from the
proxy (19.7x) and against 5.87e7 in the stitched coarse world.

IT CORRECTS IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, which is the sign that it is a correction and
not a boost. (-13,-5) LOST two wet pixels: its local runoff is 217.5 mm/yr
while its catchment mean is 156, so the proxy had been OVER-stating the water
arriving there (41.0 km2 x 217.5 mm/yr = 8.92e6 against the true 6.39e6). A
change that only ever added water would not be measuring anything.

AND THE CORRIDOR IS STILL DRY. TWO REASONS, NEITHER OF THEM THE PROXY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. (-14,-7) IS NOT A RIVER MOUTH. It is 96.2% ocean, median elevation -115 m.
   Of its 395 cells with drawable discharge at 30 m, ZERO are above sea level.
   0.000% wet is the correct answer there. The corridor's coastline is in
   (-14,-6) (91.7% land), where 1,173 of 1,304 drawable cells are above sea
   level.

2. THE IMPORTED WATER DOES NOT CROSS THE TILE. Diagnosed on (-14,-5) by baking
   it in memory and reading B6's own fields:

       Q injected at the padded boundary     1.49e7 m^3/yr, 300 entry cells
       max Q anywhere in the PADDED domain   1.32e7
       max Q in the INTERIOR                 2.22e6
       q_drawable                            3.147e6
       interior cells at or above it         0
       perennial cells (Q >= 10 L/s)         21,044
       undrawn perennial fraction            99.3%

   Discharge only increases downstream, so a 6x drop from the padded maximum
   to the interior maximum cannot be dispersion. It proves the entire
   downstream path of that 1.32e7 stream stays in the APRON: the pyramid
   deposits its water 960 m outside the tile and the 1.875 m surface routes it
   straight back out.

   THE SINGLE-TERM CONTROL that settles it. The same tile, the same bake, the
   same superblock, with EXACTLY ONE field changed -- `q` set to None, i.e. the
   pre-task-#49 boundary condition:

                                    proxy (q=None)    carried Q
       Q injected at the boundary      (area x local)   1.49472e7, 300 cells
       max Q, PADDED domain             3.574e6          1.324e7      3.7x
       drawable cells, PADDED               24              142       5.9x
       perennial cells                   20,881           21,044
       max Q, INTERIOR                  2.219e6          2.219e6      1.00x
       interior wet cells                    0                0

   The interior maximum is IDENTICAL to four significant figures in both arms.
   The boundary condition -- in either currency, better or worse -- contributes
   nothing at all to the tile that ships. The 3.7x and 5.9x gains are entirely
   inside the apron.

   The interior reaches 70% of the drawable threshold on its own local runoff.
   The tile is short of a river by a factor of 1.4, where the proxy left the
   BOUNDARY short by a factor of 46 -- but closing the boundary gap did not
   move the interior at all, because nothing crosses.

WHAT THAT MEANS FOR WHAT IS SCOPED NEXT
----------------------------------------
Task #49's premise -- "discharge is faked from local runoff" -- was correct and
is now false. Its expectation -- that fixing it would put water at the mouth --
was not, and the two must not be conflated.

The binding constraint is now HYDROLOGY_RESIDUALS #7 with #3: a level hands its
child only what CROSSES the child's boundary, deposits it AT that boundary
using the 30 m parent's D8 idea of where the crossing is, and the fine surface
disagrees. Carrying a better number across the same seam cannot fix that. The
seam has to deliver INTO the domain rather than onto its edge.

Deliberately not attempted here, per the task's own instruction to stop at a
coherent point rather than half-build both.

Two real bugs were caught by these tests before any bake was spent: the
crossing scatter indexed the RECEIVER instead of the ENTRY cell (inflating a
4-crossing fixture from 8,000 to 12,000 m^2), and the superblock's runoff
volume was mm/yr times m^2 with no /1000 (every discharge in the pyramid
1000x too large; the climate-boundary test read 1.46e6 mm/yr of implied
catchment runoff).
