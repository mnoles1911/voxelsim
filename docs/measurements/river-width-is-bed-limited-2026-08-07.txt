CAN THE RIVERS BE MADE DRAMATICALLY WIDER BY ADDING WATER?  No.
Tile (-4,-4), seed 20260719, bake_ver 17. Measured 2026-08-07.

THE QUESTION. The owner, flying the marker: "the magenta water blocks should be
filling out laterally... the river volume is not filling all of the open air
tiles to its left, right, front and center". The probe measures the DRAWN river
at 1.62 px = 3.04 m mean width (thinning ratio on the baked wet mask). So: how
much water would it take to make that 20 m?

METHOD. `--diagnostic --npz-dir` re-bakes without writing the world and dumps
`elevation_m` and `water_surface_m` -- THE BAKE'S OWN GROUND, not a Python
reconstruction (see the retraction in water-surface-roughness-2026-08-07.txt).
On a 2048 px = 3.84 km window centred on the wettest block, flood UPHILL ONLY
from the drawn surface: a dry cell joins when its ground lies under the level it
would inherit, raised by delta. Uphill-only is what stops it becoming the
documented "nearest channel floods the continent" failure; water spreads onto
banks, not down hillsides.

  delta_m    wet px     x base    implied mean width m
   0.00      137,096    1.384x           4.21
   0.25      148,290    1.497x           4.55
   0.50      159,639    1.612x           4.90
   1.00      181,139    1.829x           5.56
   2.00      223,995    2.262x           6.88
   4.00      303,801    3.067x           9.32
   8.00      439,028    4.433x          13.48

THE RESULT. Raising every river by TWO METRES only doubles its width, to 6.9 m.
EIGHT metres of extra depth -- an absurd river -- still lands at 13.5 m, short
of 20. Width grows roughly as delta^0.4, which is the signature of a V, not of
a floodplain. THE RIVERS ARE NARROW BECAUSE THE BED IS NARROW, not because they
are under-filled.

CONFIRMED INDEPENDENTLY by a real bake. A diagnostic bake at 1.5x reference
depth (CHANNEL_REF_DEPTH_M 0.3 -> 0.45, which is just inside the designed
ceiling of 1.57x) moved tile (-4,-4) from 0.543% to 0.659% wet -- 21% more
water for 50% more depth. That is the same curve.

THIS REVERSES SOMETHING I WROTE. The pressure-test of the pasted watershed plan
rejected its carve proposal partly on "room p50 20.6-37.5 m against a law asking
1.5 m, and fill_to_local_surface already uses 75-82% of it... not a case of the
terrain withholding space." That cannot be reconciled with the table above: if
there were 20-37 m of room at the water's level, a 2 m rise would not stop at
6.9 m. Whatever that figure measured, it was not width available at the drawn
water surface. The carve proposal deserves reconsideration ON THIS EVIDENCE --
which is a change of position, and the owner should know it is one.

WHAT IT DOES NOT SAY. It does not say carve. Carving costs a TERRAIN_VERSION
roll -- TERRAIN_VERSION IS the noise seed input, so 8->9 re-rolls B1 roughness
on every tile and is A NEW WORLD -- and the owner has already declined that for
now. It also does not say the current width is wrong; a 3 m mountain stream is
a real thing. It says only that ADDING WATER CANNOT MAKE THESE RIVERS WIDE, so
any plan that promises width by tuning depth, the depth exponent, or the extent
rule is promising something the terrain will not give.

CAVEAT: one 3.84 km window on one tile, chosen as the wettest. Worth repeating
on a low-gradient tile before treating the exponent 0.4 as general.
