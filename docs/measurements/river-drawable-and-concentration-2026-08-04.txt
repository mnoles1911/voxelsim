THE CONCENTRATION HALF: WHY D8 AND NOT A LARGER mfd_p
------------------------------------------------------
The brief named two candidates and flagged the trap. Both reasons below are
against the mfd_p route; the second is decisive and is a measurement, not an
argument.

1. mfd_p IS HASHED IN TWO PLACES. It is in BakeConstants.as_payload (so it
   decides the area field, hence A^m, hence every height) AND in
   superblock_inputs_fingerprint, where it drives the superblock MFD at 30 m
   and 120 m/px. A second scalar reaching only the fine water sweep leaves the
   pyramid and the tile routing the same water two different ways; threading it
   through the pyramid instead invalidates every cached superblock through a
   fingerprint that does not currently see it. Either way a routing change
   reaches a TERRAIN cache, which is what the TERRAIN/BAKE version split exists
   to prevent.

2. LARGE p SILENTLY LOSES WATER IN float32. _accumulate_mfd evaluates
   (drop * inv_dist) ** p in the surface's own dtype, and the bake carries
   float32. After the epsilon fill a flat's drop is ~2 ULP of the domain's
   magnitude, so the slope there is ~2.6e-4 at 3 km elevation and
   2.6e-4 ** p underflows float32 at p ~ 11. Every weight becomes 0, tot is 0,
   the sweep reads the cell as a PIT and DROPS its whole accumulation.
   Conservation is the detector -- fraction of the budget that never reaches a
   terminal cell, on a float32 surface at production magnitude:

     p = 1.1     lost < 1e-9
     p = 8.0     lost   (small)
     p = 32.0    lost > 50%
     D8          lost < 1e-9

   That is not a hypothetical tuning limit. It bites on exactly the flat,
   filled, near-sea-level ground a river has to cross to reach a coast.
   Asserted in tests/test_bake_flow.py::test_accumulate_d8_beats_high_p_underflow.

3. And at the largest p float32 tolerates it is not single-receiver anyway: a
   neighbour at 0.8x the steepest slope still takes 0.8^10 = 11% of the flow.

DOES THIS LEAVE PARENT AND CHILD ROUTING DIFFERENTLY? The pyramid still carries
Q under MFD; the fine tier now concentrates. That is not the trap the brief
described, and the reason is what crosses the boundary: the superblock delivers
a per-cell TOTAL at the tile's edge cells and the child does not re-route the
parent's interior. Where the parent's MFD spread a trunk over several 30 m
cells, those all land in the same valley and the child's D8 forest re-converges
them -- the child HEALS the parent's dispersion rather than amplifying it. It is
also unchanged from bake_ver 10 in the only respect that is measurable here: the
injected quantities per tile are identical, because superblock_inputs_fingerprint
saw no change and the cached blocks were rebuilt to the same fingerprints
(64b6aecdcfcb at L1; 3f4dae81d1e2 / 6f5b1fcff952 at L0).

  MFD @ 2.0 px  (bv10)      3.1467e6      29,159   2,019   1,113 m     0

  D8  @ 1.5 px  both        1.5286e6     165,243      36  16,341 m    25

BOTH HALVES HELP, AND THEY ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE.

  * The THRESHOLD alone lengthens the longest reach 3.8x but makes the
    fragmentation WORSE: 2,019 -> 4,930 components. Lowering the cut admits more
    marginal cells, and MFD splits those too, so it buys more pieces of river.
    On its own this half is half a win.
  * CONCENTRATION alone is the larger effect on both axes: 10.6x the longest
    reach and 92x fewer components (2,019 -> 22).
  * TOGETHER: 16,341 m and 25 components over 2 km, against 11,792 m and 12 for
    concentration alone. The count goes 22 -> 36, which is the threshold half's
    marginal headwaters again -- but they now arrive as reaches rather than as
    fragments, and the trade buys +4.5 km of longest span and 13 more
    multi-kilometre rivers.

Neither half is worse than the baseline on the number that matters. The
threshold half IS worse than the baseline on component count when run alone, and
that is stated rather than averaged away.

WHAT NOW FRAGMENTS THE NETWORK -- IT IS NOT THE TWO CAUSES THAT WERE FIXED
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Shipped raster 173 components; raw cut 36. The 137 extra components come from
4.11% of cells, and they are the B5 basin exclusion -- BY DESIGN, and the client
composes them back. At bv10 basins cost 5 components and 0 m of span; they are
now the largest single term in the raster's component count, purely because
everything else got better.

Of the 36 raw components, classified by where the DOWNSTREAM end sits:

  24  the OUTER boundary of the four-tile measurement window -- the window, not
      a defect. The two longest (16,341 m and 15,332 m) leave the box at -476 m,
      i.e. having already reached the sea.
  11  an INTERIOR SEAM between two corridor tiles. This is a real product break.
   1  inland.

35 of 36 end within one pixel of a tile edge. THE INTERIOR SEAM IS THE NEXT
DEFECT, and it was invisible before: the fine Q field does not cross a fine tile
boundary. Each tile accumulates over its own padded domain and restarts from the
30 m superblock injection at its edge, so where two tiles meet, the two Q values
either side of the seam are not the same number and a hard threshold can cut
between them. Under MFD's general fragmentation this was one break among two
thousand; now the network is otherwise continuous and it is 8 of the 25
multi-kilometre reaches, including the longest.

Not fixed here, and deliberately not guessed at.


THE COST: THE RASTER NOW DRAWS A CENTRELINE
--------------------------------------------
This is the honest negative, and it is what the brief asked to see.

  raster ribbon width, m   min   p50   p90   p99    max    <=1.9 m
    bv10                  1.88  1.88  5.62  7.50  10.61    51.67%
    bv11                  1.88  1.88  1.88  1.88   3.75    99.21%

  law width (channel_width_m(Q) at the same pixels), m
    bv10                  3.75  4.37  5.92    --   8.85
    bv11                  2.81  3.53  5.47    --   8.81

The MEDIAN was already one pixel at bv10; what is gone is the upper tail. p90
falls 5.62 -> 1.88 m and the widest ribbon 10.61 -> 3.75 m, so essentially every
drawn river is now a single 1.875 m pixel -- 19 voxels at 100 mm.

THE MECHANISM, so it is not mistaken for a coincidence. The plane is wet where a
cell's own Q clears the cut, so the drawn ribbon is however many ADJACENT cells
clear it. MFD's fan is what made it wide: beside a trunk the neighbours carried a
share large enough to clear the cut too. A single-receiver forest has one-cell
branches by construction. So bv10's width was a by-product of the same dispersion
that was breaking the network -- the two cannot both be had from the mask alone,
and bv10's apparent agreement with the law (drawn p50 5.30 m against law p50
4.87 m on (-12,-5)) was an accident.

Net: the network is now right and NARROWER than the channel it describes
(1.88 m drawn against 3.53 m of law at p50), where before it was slightly wider.
Depth falls the same way and for a composition reason: p50 0.728 -> 0.482 m
because the lower cut admits many more small streams, and the floor 0.505 ->
0.392 m is exactly water_depth_m at the new q_drawable.

THE FIX IF THE OWNER WANTS IT, stated but NOT implemented, because it is a third
change and a decision about what the world looks like: let channel_width_m(Q)
decide EXTENT the way water_depth_m(Q) already decides depth -- dilate each wet
cell to its own law width. That is more consistent than either version rather
than new hydraulics, and it is the only way to get a continuous network AND a
river as wide as the law says it is.
