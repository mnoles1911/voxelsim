GEOMORPHONS AT WORLDGEN v21 — RE-MEASURING THE v12 TERRAIN VALIDATION
======================================================================

Written because every `frac_ridge_peak` figure in circulation comes from
docs/terrain-validation-2026-07.md, which was measured at WORLDGEN v12 on
2026-07-29. The current version is 21. v13, v14, v18, v19, v20 and v21 all
shipped terrain-shaping changes in between; v20 deleted an entire 3D density
band. A search of docs/ and docs/measurements/ finds no post-v12 geomorphon
measurement of any kind — terrain-stage-metrics-2026-07-29.json is the v12 raw
output and nothing has re-run the suite since. Confirmed before starting.

THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER. On the FINE tier the v12 defect is gone: the client's
detail pass now moves 1.875 m mean slope by +1.0% and `frac_ridge_peak` by
+3.4%, against v12's +204% and +255%. On the COARSE tier it is real, it is
smaller than it was, and it is exactly where the brief guessed: on ground with
`relief30` under 900 mm the coarse client MORE THAN DOUBLES the carrier's
1.875 m mean slope (1.363 deg -> 3.229 deg, +137%), and 98.7% of the energy it
adds sits at wavelengths of 3.2 m and longer — the two SHAPING octaves, not the
microrelief band. The amplification falls monotonically as relief rises and is
+0.5% on alpine ground. 23.4% of the surviving world's land sits in the fully
exempt zone.

BUT THE LEVER IS NOT THE ONE NAMED IN THE BRIEF. `engageQ10` gates the detail
GRADIENT CAP, and the gradient cap is not what is putting metre-scale landform
on flat ground. `kReliefScaleMinQ10` is — the 0.10x anti-terrace floor on
`reliefScaleQ10`, which is justified in carrier.h purely in terms of the metre
and material bands and is nonetheless applied to the 25.6 m and 6.4 m landform
octaves as well. See section 7.

READ THESE TWO TABLES TOGETHER AND THREE THINGS FALL OUT.

  (a) THE FINE TIER'S RESIDUAL GOES TO ZERO WITH THE GROUND — 30 mm at the
      flattest bin, monotone all the way up. That is what a self-affine
      continuation looks like and it is the behaviour carrier.h's
      `reliefScaleQ10` comment describes: "flattening it to a plane removes the
      decoration entirely".

  (b) THE COARSE TIER'S DOES NOT. It bottoms out at 191 mm RMS / 583 mm p99 and
      stops falling. On ground whose ENTIRE relief across a 60 m baseline is
      under 900 mm, the client lays down excursions whose 99th percentile is
      583 mm — 65% of the landform's whole relief, at metre wavelengths. That is
      the floor, and it is 6.4x the fine tier's answer on identically flat ground.

  (c) THE CAP IS WORKING AND IS NOT THE PROBLEM. Between the 2700-3600 bin
      (uncapped) and the 7500-10000 bin (fully capped) the residual FALLS from
      318 mm to 282 mm while the ground gets three times steeper — the cap
      taking amplitude out faster than relief puts it in. The amplification
      percentage falls smoothly through the ramp (20.7 -> 12.0 -> 5.4 -> 1.4).
      The ramp is doing exactly its job. What it does not and cannot do is
      remove the residual on the exempt side, because there it is switched off
      by design and the floor is what remains.

THE COARSE TIER'S CONTRIBUTION IS 98.7% LANDFORM AND 1.3% TEXTURE. The two
SHAPING octaves (25.6 m and 6.4 m) plus the low-frequency envelope that their
own relief- and curvature-gating imposes on them account for essentially all of
it; the microrelief band (1.6 m, 0.4 m, 0.2 m) contributes 1.3% of the energy at
this posting. The fine tier, where those two octaves are DELETED, puts 68-77% of
its energy in the 3.2-12.8 m band — which is its 3.2 m shaping octave doing
precisely what the table says it should.

This matters more than any other number in this file, because it separates two
things that the exemption currently treats as one. carrier.h justifies
`kReliefScaleMinQ10` — the 0.10x floor — entirely in terms of texture:

    "the MATERIAL band (0.4 m and 0.2 m) is not relief-gated at all"
    "this floor keeps a little of the METRE band alive on the flattest ground,
     which is what actually breaks a terrace run. 0.1x of a 500 mm octave is
     50 mm at 1.6 m"

Every worked example in that comment is about 1.6 m and below. The floor is
nevertheless applied to `kDetailOctaves[0] = {25600, 2600}` and
`kDetailOctaves[1] = {6400, 1100}` as well, where 0.10x is 260 mm and 110 mm,
raised by `kCurvatureScaleMaxQ10` = 1.75x to 647 mm of landform-wavelength
amplitude that does not go away no matter how flat the ground gets. 647 mm is
the figure the brief quoted, and it is confirmed arithmetically here
((2600 + 1100) x 0.10 x 1.75 = 647.5 mm) and observationally by the spectrum.


7. IS THE PROBLEM REAL, AND HOW BIG
------------------------------------

REAL, ON THE COARSE TIER ONLY, AND THE BRIEF NAMED THE WRONG CONSTANT.

  Size, stated four ways:
    * on relief30 < 900 mm ground the coarse client turns a 1.363 deg carrier
      into a 3.229 deg surface (+137%), where the fine tier on the SAME cells
      reaches 2.148 deg. Excess over our own better answer: +1.08 deg, or 50%.
    * on the flat window it takes `frac_ridge_peak` from 0.0497 — a value that
      sits BELOW both real plains references — to 0.162, which is 2.2x Illinois
      and 3.0x the Llano Estacado.
    * `frac_flat_coarse_thresh` falls 0.557 -> 0.075 on the same pass: 87% of
      the cells that read as flat at 3 deg stop doing so.
    * 23.4% of the surviving world's land is in the fully exempt zone and 44.8%
      is at least partly exempt, so this is not a corner case.

  NOT REAL ON THE FINE TIER. +8.4% mean slope on the flattest bin, +1.0% over
  the flat window as a whole, `frac_ridge_peak` +3.4%. Where a baked .vxtl v2
  tile exists — the shipping configuration — the client is very nearly
  transparent and the remaining distance to the Earth references is the BAKE's,
  not the client's. v12's recommendation 1 ("condition the client's detail
  amplitude on landform relief, not only on carrier gradient") was carried out
  and it worked.

  RESIZED, NOT DISSOLVED. The v12 number for this defect on the coarse tier was
  `frac_ridge_peak` x 18.6 and mean slope +415%; it is now x 3.26 and +25.5%
  over a whole window, concentrated on the flattest fifth of it. That is a 5-16x
  improvement and a real residue, not a phantom.

  AND THE MECHANISM IS NOT `engageQ10`. `engageQ10` switches off the detail
  GRADIENT CAP. Section 5 (c) shows the cap doing its job wherever it is on, and
  section 6 shows that what survives on the exempt side is 98.7% landform-
  wavelength energy from the two shaping octaves — which the cap was never the
  primary control on. The control on those two octaves is `reliefScaleQ10`, and
  specifically its 0.10x floor. Attacking `kDetailCapReliefLoMm` would either
  re-cap the decimetre texture on flat ground (the anti-terrace regression the
  ramp exists to prevent, and the drainage regression amplifier.cpp measured at
  99.0% -> 97.5% stranded) or do nothing to the octaves that are actually
  responsible.


8. THE SECOND JOB: kFineDetailOctaves' 3200 mm PROVISIONAL ENTRY
-----------------------------------------------------------------

The comment at voxel-core/src/amplifier.cpp:512 is stale in the way the brief
described: it derives 900 mm from a lambda^0.8 ramp, the shipped value is 100,
and v18 ("move the fine ladder's budget to sub-metre, halving rib length") is
why. It also states that the calibrating measurement does not exist.

CAN IT BE RUN? YES, WITH ONE SUBSTITUTION. There are no s16 fine tiles anywhere
under D:\voxelsim\tile-cache — only s1. But `--fine-dir` accepts the .vxtl v2
tile that `dump_stage_heightfields.py --emit-vxtl` writes from this run's own
bake, which is the same substitution v12 section 8.2 made, and the probe then
reports `cal.pixel_size 1875` and `cal.band_edge_measured 1`, i.e. it is
genuinely calibrating against a fine-tier raster and not predicting from a
coarse one. Three runs, all inside tile (-5,3), 720 m footprints centred on the
three windows of section 2:

  site    H_used   r2     target_rms_7m5   amp_3200   amp_1600   amp_400   amp_200
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  flat    0.607   0.959      1.147 m         488        343       157       101
  mid     0.704   0.964      3.024 m        1253        770       323       175
  steep   1.025   0.983      2.219 m         332        310        92        24
  shipped                                    100        100       400       200

SO THE MEASUREMENT IS POSSIBLE AND ITS ANSWER IS "NOT YET". `amp_3200` spans
3.8x across three sites in a single tile. That is not measurement noise — the
model residual is 7.5-8.3% at every site and r2 is 0.96+ — it is the constant
being genuinely site-dependent, because it is solved from the site's own fitted
Hurst exponent and 7.5 m target and both of those vary with the landform. Note
also `H_used` = 1.025 on the steep site: a Hurst exponent above 1 is outside the
fBm range the ladder model assumes and says the fit there is reading landform
rather than the roughness band, so that row should not be averaged in as if it
were a third sample of the same quantity.

Setting a single global constant from this needs a site-selection and averaging
policy that neither the tool nor the comment defines, and none of the three
numbers speaks to the rib-length reason v18 chose 100. NO NUMBER IS PROPOSED. If
this is to be closed properly it needs (a) a defined corpus of calibration sites
spanning the classes, (b) a stated rule for combining them, and (c) a re-run of
whatever rib-length measurement v18 used, since a 3-5x increase would undo it.
The one thing that can be done cheaply and should be: fix the comment, which
currently documents a value of 900 that has never shipped.

================================================================================
10. PROPOSED CHANGE — NOT APPLIED, AND CONDITIONAL ON ONE MORE MEASUREMENT
================================================================================

Stated separately because the measurement above stands whether or not this is
ever done, and because it depends on a claim this run did not test.

WHAT. Give the LANDFORM band its own, lower relief floor, separate from the
material band's. Today `reliefScaleQ10` clamps at `kReliefScaleMinQ10` = 102
(0.10x) for every octave in `kDetailOctaves`. The proposal is a second constant
applied only to indices [0, kLandformOctaveCount) — the 25.6 m and 6.4 m
shaping octaves — leaving the 1.6/0.4/0.2 m bands, `engageQ10`, the gradient-cap
ladder and every threshold in it untouched.

WHY THIS AND NOT THE RAMP. Section 6: the coarse tier's contribution on exempt
ground is 98.7% landform-wavelength and 1.3% texture. Section 5(c): the cap is
already correct wherever it is engaged. carrier.h's own justification for the
floor is written entirely about 1.6 m and below. Nothing in the repo argues the
landform octaves need a floor at all; they inherited one.

THIS DOES NOT ASSUME MONOTONICITY IN SLOPE. It touches no (k, floor) pair on the
gradient allowance and does not re-open the question amplifier.cpp:1140-1192
settled. `engageQ10`, `kDetailCapReliefLoMm` and `kDetailCapReliefHiMm` are
unchanged; the non-monotone allowance the ramp exists to express is expressed
exactly as it is today.

SIZE, FROM THE 0-900 BIN. Coarse S3 there is 3.229 deg from a 1.363 deg carrier
(+1.866 deg). The fine tier's own answer on the same cells is 2.148 deg, which
would need the coarse detail to add +0.785 deg instead — a factor of 0.42 on a
band carrying ~99% of the residual. 0.42 x 102 is about 43/1024, i.e. a landform
floor near 0.04x. Because `reliefScaleQ10` is linear above its clamp, a floor of
102 currently binds everywhere below relief30 = 1594 mm, so lowering it reaches
the 0-900 bin entirely and most of the 900-1800 bin — the two bins that carry
+137% and +61%. The 1800-2700 bin (+32%) is already on the linear ramp and this
would not touch it.

THE MEASUREMENT THAT MUST COME FIRST, AND COULD KILL IT. amplifier.cpp justifies
the flat exemption with a drainage result: on the coarse plains carrier, 99.0%
of area is stranded at 10 cm postings and client detail takes it to 97.5%. That
was measured with the WHOLE ladder present. If the 1.5 points come from the
6.4 m octave rather than from the decimetre bands, this proposal regresses
drainage on exactly the ground the exemption was built to protect, and the right
answer is to leave it alone. Re-running docs/measurements/drainage-ladder-v13-
2026-07-29.txt's sweep with the landform band alone scaled down decides it, and
it is cheap. Do not ship this without it.

SECONDARY NOTES. A lower floor only ever scales detail DOWN, so no surface bound
can loosen and the two BOUND goldens should not move (same argument v21's commit
message makes for the micro cap). `kWorldGenVersion` rolls, `worldgen.ush` and
the prebuilt SPIR-V need the mirror, and the amplifier/deep-column/mips/coarsegen
digests will move. The fine tier is unaffected: `kFineDetailOctaves` has one
shaping octave at 3.2 m and this constant would be read through the same path,
so whether the fine tier gets the new floor or keeps 0.10x is a decision that
should be made on the fine tier's own numbers — which, per section 3, currently
say it is fine.
