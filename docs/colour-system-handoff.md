# The voxelsim colour system — handoff

> **SUPERSEDED, 2026-08-22. Read [voxel-colour-system.md](voxel-colour-system.md)
> first.** This document's §8 ends on an open question -- how the palette and the
> biome LUT should relate -- and [ADR-0009](adr/0009-voxel-colour-system.md)
> answers it: the biome *modulates* the material, per material, by a weight in
> the engine header. Its recommendation of "(2) heading toward (3)" is what was
> built.
>
> Three other things below are now out of date and are marked where they appear:
> the two-palettes section (there is one, plus generated copies), the claim that
> terrain colour is not the palette's (it is, since ADR-0009), and the channel
> budget (`TexCoords[5]` is now spoken for). Everything else -- especially §1's
> invariants, §5's warning about overloading a channel, and §7's traps -- is
> still current, which is why this file is kept rather than deleted.

Written 2026-08-11, for a session picking up terrain and biome colour. This is
the whole picture: what has been decided, what is built, what is still two
things pretending to be one, and the traps that have already cost real time.

Read `docs/adr/0008-flat-per-voxel-material-colour.md` alongside this. The ADR is
the decision; this is the state of the world.

---

## 1. The decision: flat colour per voxel face, varied per voxel

**Every voxel face is a single flat colour. There is no texture and no sub-voxel
detail.** Signed off with a reference screenshot as the spec.

Two alternatives were considered and rejected, and the reasons matter because
they will come back:

- **Texture array**, one tile per material per face class. Rejected on LOOK, not
  cost: it puts a pattern *inside* a face, and at 5–10 cm a face is a few pixels
  across, so the pattern reads as noise.
- **World-space procedural detail** at the hit point. Rejected because it places
  structure at a *finer* scale than a voxel, which fights the cubic read the art
  direction is built on.

If you find yourself wanting either, you are proposing to revisit the ADR. That
is allowed, but do it explicitly.

### Four invariants — check any new code against these

1. **One entry per `vxc::Material`.** Enforced by `static_assert` on the table.
   A material added without an appearance is a build failure, not a black voxel
   found in a screenshot weeks later.
2. **The tint is keyed to the VOXEL, not the face.** All six faces of one cube
   share the hash of its integer position. Hashing per face gives a cube a
   different colour on its top than its side, and it reads as six unrelated
   squares instead of one solid object.
3. **Variation has two frequencies and BOTH are required.** Per-voxel jitter
   supplies near-field dither; a slow patch term supplies what survives
   distance. Jitter alone averages back to its own mean once voxels fall below a
   pixel, so a varied hillside flattens to grey exactly where the variation is
   doing the most work. Anything that drops the patch term "as a simplification"
   is removing the half that carries.
4. **Face class is for MATERIAL difference, not shading.** `kFaceTop` /
   `kFaceSide` / `kFaceBottom` exist so grass is green on top and soil
   underneath, and a cut trunk shows heartwood on its ends. Light and ambient
   occlusion are the renderer's business and are applied on top. Baking a
   top-is-brighter bias into the table double-counts them and goes wrong the
   moment the sun moves.

## 2. The one definition

`voxel-core/include/voxelcore/materialpalette.h` — `vxc::kMaterialPalette`, 26
entries, one per `vxc::Material`. Per entry:

- `Rgb face[3]` — base colour per face class, **sRGB**
- `voxelJitter`, `voxelHue` — per-voxel lightness and hue drift, 1/255ths
- `patchStrength`, `patchScaleVox` — the slow term: strength in 1/255ths,
  wavelength in voxels

All `uint8_t`, deliberately: ADR-0008 added no new float to voxel-core.

**Every consumer reads a copy GENERATED from it.** Never transcribe it.

| Consumer | Generated by | Drift check |
|---|---|---|
| `asset-forge/forge/palette.py` | `asset-forge/tools/gen_palette.py` | `python -m forge.cli selftest` |
| `ue-project/Shaders/VoxelMaterialPalette.ush` | `ue-project/Tools/gen_material_palette_ush.py` | `tools/compile-shaders.ps1`, which CI runs |

Both drift checks have been proved to fire, not merely written. Perturbing one
colour names the differing line and both sides; reordering the table reproduces
the historical `palette is out of step with the enum at index 19`.

**Why this paranoia.** Material IDs were once declared in two places, drifted,
and because `MaterialId` is a byte the result rendered as *something* rather
than failing — nine of thirteen materials were out of range before anyone
noticed. Colour is the version nobody catches, because a wrong colour still
looks like a colour.

## 3. What actually draws terrain today — and it is NOT the palette

> **OUT OF DATE (ADR-0009).** It IS the palette now, blended with the climate
> per material. The measurement in this section -- that a categorical id cannot
> survive the FColor vertex-attribute path -- is still true and still the reason
> the id is read from the quad's own bits in the pooled shader instead. What
> changed is that the hook this section names as "the way back in" was taken.

This is the part most likely to surprise you.

**Terrain colour comes from a climate biome LUT, not from material id.**

`M_VoxelTerrain` samples `T_VoxelBiomeLUT` (64×64 Whittaker diagram) at
`U = precipitation`, `V = temperature`, both arriving in `VertexColor.A` and
`VertexColor.B`, already remapped by `VoxelClimateProbe` to this world's
measured p1–p99 so the whole LUT is reachable. On top of that the graph adds
three modifiers, all authored as material parameters: rock on slope, beach
within a few metres of sea level on flat ground, and snow driven by temperature
with an elevation term OR'd in for the highest ground.

`VertexColor.R` is a **binary surface/subsurface flag**, not a material id:
255 = take the biome colour, 0 = keep a flat subsurface rock tone.

### Why R stopped carrying the material id — do not undo this by accident

An exposure-proof probe measured `VertexColor.R * 255` arriving in the shader as
**~6–8 where the CPU wrote 4**. Every categorical id threshold evaluated to 0
across the whole frame. The FColor vertex-attribute path is not
value-preserving, so a *categorical* id cannot survive it. `VoxelClimateProbe.h`
records the measurement.

Only 0 and 255 are fixed points of any monotonic per-channel transform, which is
why the flag is binary and why the graph's thresholds are deliberately loose.

**The consequence:** a cave wall is one flat rock tone rather than
bedrock/rock/gravel/subsoil each having their own. That is a known regression
against a per-material palette this graph briefly had, and getting it back is
probably your job.

**The way back in:** on the GPU pooled path, `Decoded.MaterialId` is unpacked
inside `VoxelQuadVertexFactory.ush` from the quad's own bits and never touches
the FColor transform, so an exact integer compare **is** sound there. The
`MAT_WATERMARK` sentinel branch already does exactly this and its comment says
so: "the smallest possible instance of the thing biomes need next: per-material
appearance in the pooled renderer". That is the hook. Use it.

Note also that the pooled path (`voxel.Stream.GPU`, on by default) is what
actually draws. A change made only in `UVoxelChunkComponent`'s vertex builder
will be invisible — that mistake cost a whole session once.

## 4. There are still TWO palettes, and one dead texture

> **OUT OF DATE.** There is one, in the engine header, plus three generated
> copies. `terrain_palette.py` is generated in full -- ADR-0009 moved its last
> authored column, `BIOME_TINT`, into the header as a weight. `T_VoxelPalette`
> is still dead. The recommendation in this section is what was done.

**`ue-project/Tools/terrain_palette.py`** is a second table. Its docstring calls
itself "single source of truth". It is:

- **16 entries**, stopping at `MAT_WATERMARK`. The ten asset materials (bark,
  heartwood, deadwood, six leaf types, pale bark) have **no appearance at all**
  on the UE side.
- **LINEAR**, where the engine header is sRGB.
- Carrying a `BIOME_TINT` boolean column that the engine header does not have and
  arguably should not — "does the surface biome colour replace this material's
  own albedo" is UE-side policy.
- `assert len(PALETTE) == PALETTE_WIDTH` with `PALETTE_WIDTH = 16`.

It feeds two things: `gen_terrain_textures.py`, which bakes the RGB column into
`T_VoxelPalette.png`, and `terrain_material_common.py`, which turns the
`BIOME_TINT` column into graph arithmetic.

**And `T_VoxelPalette` is dead.** The graph has no palette texture sample at
all; `PALETTE_TEXTURE` is a dead constant. The texture is still generated and
still imported.

**Recommended:** generate `terrain_palette.py`'s RGB from the engine header,
leaving it to own only `BIOME_TINT`, and extend the drift check to cover it.
That work is already queued as Phase 3.2 in `docs/asset-forge-plan.md` and an
agent may have started it — check before duplicating. **Do not create a third
palette under any circumstances.**

## 5. The channel budget — this is the real constraint

Every interpolant is spoken for. Know this before designing anything.

> **UPDATED (ADR-0009).** `TexCoords[3]/[4]` now carry the material's base
> colour and its biome weight, and `TexCoords[5]` carries the two variation
> scalars. `[5]` cost nothing: Unreal packs customised UVs two per `float4`, so
> the one holding `[4]` already reserved its lanes.
>
> `VertexColor.A` (precipitation) is now **free on terrain quads** — §3a removed
> climate from the near field, and the clipmap reads the LUT through its own
> vertex colours. **`VertexColor.B` is NOT free**: it carries temperature on
> terrain quads, which is likewise unused, but on WATER quads it carries
> `Decoded.TopBoundary`, which the World Position Offset needs. Reclaiming B
> means reclaiming it on the terrain branch only.
>
> The rest of this table, and the warning under it, still hold.

| Channel | Carries |
|---|---|
| `VertexColor.R` | surface/subsurface flag (or corner height on water) |
| `VertexColor.G` | ambient occlusion, **modulated by the GI path in the pixel shader** |
| `VertexColor.B` | temperature (or water top boundary) |
| `VertexColor.A` | precipitation |
| `TexCoords[0]` | world-planar UVs, wrapped to a 32 m period |
| `TexCoords[1]/[2]` | claimed by local lights (`LocalRGB` → EmissiveColor) |
| `TexCoords[3]/[4]` | being wired now for the material palette RGB + isAsset |
| `GIUVW` (TEXCOORD1 slot) | GI volume lookup |

**Do not overload a channel something else already modulates.** `VertexColor.A`
briefly carried the water marker's opacity; the GI pixel path scales `G`, and
the GI volume only covers about ±38 m round the camera, so the marker went
see-through *exactly when flown close to* — reported as "flying close to the
magenta blocks makes water completely see through". A debug signal that is a
function of lighting is not a signal.

The cheap idiom for adding data: compute it at **pixel rate** inside
`GetMaterialPixelParameters` and hand it out through a `TexCoords[n]` slot. That
adds no VS→PS interpolant. Both the local-lights work and the palette wiring use
it.

## 6. How to change the material graph

`M_VoxelTerrain` is a **generated artifact**. Edit
`ue-project/Tools/create_voxel_material.py` and the shared
`ue-project/Tools/terrain_material_common.py`, never the `.uasset`.
`create_clipmap_material.py` builds the clipmap's equivalent from the same
`build_terrain_base_color()`, so a change there lands in both.

Running the generator is a commandlet:

```
UnrealEditor-Cmd.exe <uproject> -run=pythonscript -script=<file> -unattended -nop4 -nosplash
```

**One editor per box is a hard rule here.** Two capture sessions on one machine
killed each other's frames for hours and read exactly like a slow
configuration. Check whether another session holds it before you launch.

`BaseColor = albedo * VertexColor.G * DebugTint`. Note it can only **darken** —
anything that needs to brighten has to go somewhere else.

### What you CAN verify without an editor

`tools/dxc/bin/x64/dxc.exe` is a standalone compiler, and
`tools/compile-shaders.ps1` compiles every kernel to **both** ADR-0001 targets
(DXIL and SPIR-V) plus the palette. `VoxelQuadVertexFactory.ush` itself cannot be
handed to DXC — it opens with Unreal private includes and compiles inside a
material permutation — so the pattern established is: put the logic in a header
that CAN be compiled, exercise it from a test `.usf`, and leave the factory
holding one call per side. Copy that pattern. A shader change nobody can compile
is a shader change nobody has checked.

## 7. Traps, each one already paid for

- **sRGB vs linear.** The engine header authors sRGB; `terrain_palette.py` is
  linear; textures are imported with `srgb=True` so `gen_terrain_textures.py`
  sRGB-encodes on the way in. Skipping that encode was a real bug that made
  every colour come back darker and more saturated than authored. State which
  space you are in, every time.
- **The classifier labels almost everything `MAT_SAND`.** On real diffusion
  tiles the surface classifier's precipitation thresholds are calibrated for the
  synthetic sampler, not for WorldClim's 0–12000 mm/yr quantisation. So today the
  biome path effectively owns all outdoor appearance and material id barely
  varies outdoors. If you fix the classifier, a lot of colour code that "worked"
  will start being exercised for the first time.
- **Two renderers can be live at once.** `voxel.Stream.GPUMaxLevel` puts the
  pooled and component paths in one frame; any shading divergence shows up as a
  seam at the ring boundary. The shipped config is fully pooled.
- **Silent success is this project's signature failure.** A weathering pass
  removed 20 voxels from a stone of 90,000 for months and reported success. A
  test harness reported "no dead sliders" while the mechanism it tested did
  nothing. Whatever you build, **prove the check fires** by breaking the thing it
  guards and showing the failure.

## 8. The open design question, which is yours to answer

> **ANSWERED, 2026-08-22, by ADR-0009: option (2), with the weight per material
> in the engine header, and (3) still the real prize.** The recommendation below
> is what was built, including its reasoning about the classifier. Kept in full
> because the argument is the argument.

The palette gives every material an appearance. The biome LUT gives every
surface a climate-driven colour. **Nothing has decided how those two relate.**

The immediate step being taken is deliberately conservative:
`BaseColor = lerp(biomeAlbedo, paletteRGB, isAsset)` — the palette colours only
the ten asset materials, terrain keeps its climate colour, and it is inert until
assets are in the world. That is a first step, not the answer.

The real question is what a grass voxel in a rainforest and a grass voxel in a
tundra should be. Three shapes worth weighing:

1. **Biome replaces material** (today). One flat rock tone underground, climate
   colour above. Simple; loses per-material identity where you can see it most.
2. **Biome modulates material.** The palette gives the base and the LUT tints
   it. Keeps bedrock distinguishable from gravel in a cave while a hillside
   still reads as its climate. Needs a decision about how strongly, and the
   `BIOME_TINT` column is already a crude version of this.
3. **Biome selects material.** Worldgen emits different material ids per biome
   and colour is purely the palette. The cleanest of the three, and the most
   work, because it pushes the problem into the classifier — which, per the trap
   above, currently emits one material for nearly all land.

My recommendation is **(2) heading toward (3)**: modulate now because it is
reversible and needs no worldgen change, while treating the classifier fix as the
real prize, because a colour system whose input is "everything is sand" is being
judged on a signal it never receives.

Whatever you pick, write it down as an ADR amendment. The invariants in §1 stay
either way.
