EXCLUDED, AND WHY
  beach 19:30 is NOT in this folder. Both attempts (240 s and 420 s settle)
  reported unloaded=206482 and unloaded=303052 respectively. jobsInFlight and
  pendingJobs were 0 both times and the counter was flat for the last 30 s, so
  it is a lifetime counter rather than live churn -- but the harness rule is
  that any non-zero settle counter means the frame must not be judged, so it is
  excluded rather than argued about. Dusk is covered by alpine-1930, which is
  clean. Something at this site/hour reproducibly churns; not investigated.

DATE QUANTISATION (expected, not a bug)
  -VoxelDate=03-20 was requested for all frames; the calendar's reachable dates
  are 7.61 real days apart at DaysPerYear=48, so the frames land on 03-16,
  03-19, 03-20 and 03-23. Declination varies ~1.6 deg across that span, i.e.
  negligible for comparing the hours against each other.

TWO THINGS THESE FRAMES DO NOT SETTLE
  1. FRAMING. -SpawnPitch reaches the controller (the log reads back "control
     pitch 45.0 deg") but the rendered frame barely reframes between -20, +10
     and +45 at this site: mean abs pixel difference 10.6/255 between -20 and
     +45, and the skyline row does not move monotonically. My beach frames are
     geometrically identical to the pre-existing archive shot
     world-maps/seed20260719/vistas/vista-beach-arid-t-4_-10.png, so this is how
     the vista pose has always behaved here and is not new. Consequence: these
     captures are mostly ground, and are weak evidence about the sky itself.
     Looking up with the mouse in an interactive session is not subject to this.
  2. STAR-AMBIENT CALIBRATION. tools/sky-albedo-check.py on the alpine night
     frame returns ground/sky = 1.0640 against its [0.75, 0.85] band. This is
     NOT evidence the calibration is wrong: the script's own header says it is
     provisional and must not be used as a gate, the frame is not its reference
     capture (no +3 EV bias, wrong site), and its auto-detected "snow" region
     had a median of 0.01664 against the sky's 0.01681 -- i.e. it measured dark
     rock, not snow. The documented reference site (-84480,53760) is in tile
     (-6,3), which has no fine tile baked, so the proper re-measurement cannot
     be run as written without baking that tile first.
